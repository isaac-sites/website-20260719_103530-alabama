## Introduction

Lumberton’s April 1975 UFO wave was a concentrated series of night-time reports from police officers and civilians across south-eastern [North Carolina]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-north-carolinas/' | relative_url }}). Witnesses commonly described a silent, brightly lit object that appeared V-shaped, wedge-shaped or triangular, could hover, and then seemed to accelerate rapidly. The most memorable account placed it above a [water tower]({{ 'water-tower/' | relative_url }}) behind Lumberton High School, where an officer said a downward beam illuminated the tower before the object departed without audible engine or rotor noise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://s3images.coroflot.com/user_files/individual_files/original_pdf_221275_cenkof8pltjfkxtu3zp3xxmx0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: s3images.coroflot.com">[Coroflot]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">s3images.coroflot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/North_Carolina_cd988f_lumberton_ufo_wave_72eb1c-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Lumberton Wave" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The case matters because it was not merely a single person glimpsing a distant light. Reports circulated through [police radio]({{ 'police-radio/' | relative_url }}) networks, officers in several communities made observations, and civilian UFO investigators later assembled a substantial case study. Yet the wave remains unresolved rather than proven extraordinary. Much of what survives comes through a privately produced investigation, later summaries and restricted archives, while radar records, photographs, precise measurements and complete original police documentation are either absent or not publicly available.

## The nights of concentrated sightings

The recognised wave ran from 3 to 9 April 1975, with Lumberton as its centre but reports extending into surrounding parts of south-eastern North Carolina. The principal contemporary investigation was conducted by Jennie Zeidman, Lee Speigel and Ted Phillips. Zeidman’s resulting 59-page monograph, *The Lumberton Report: UFO Activity in S. North Carolina, April 3–9, 1975*, was published by the Center for UFO Studies, or CUFOS, in May 1976. Library and archival catalogues confirm both the date range and the existence of this dedicated investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://s3images.coroflot.com/user_files/individual_files/original_pdf_221275_cenkof8pltjfkxtu3zp3xxmx0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: s3images.coroflot.com">[coroflot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">s3images.coroflot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation...</span></span></span>

Later summaries give differing totals. A CUFOS publication notice described 57 reported events involving more than 48 police officers and numerous civilians. Recent retellings more cautiously refer to over 30 law-enforcement witnesses across a seven-county area. These figures are not necessarily direct contradictions: one “event” could include several observers, one officer could witness the phenomenon more than once, and later writers may count only people whose accounts they regard as clearly documented. The variation nevertheless warns against repeating popular claims such as “80 witnesses” or “40 officers” as exact, independently verified numbers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cufos.org/PDFs/books/A%20Helicopter-UFO%20Encounter%20Over%20Ohio.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cufos.org">[Center for UFO Studies]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cufos.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Center for UFO StudiesA Helicopter-UFO Encounter over OhioOctober 30, 2023 — by J Zeidman · Cited by 4 — THE LUMBERTON REPORT—UFO Activit...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 30, 2023</span></span></span>

The first widely cited sequence began at about 1.45 a.m. on 3 April. Lumberton patrolmen Phil Stanton, P. H. Atkinson and Neil McCormick reportedly noticed an unusual light near the water tower behind Lumberton High School. Atkinson initially considered a helicopter, which is significant: the officers did not begin with the assumption that they were watching an exotic craft. As they changed position to investigate, however, they reported movements and an abrupt disappearance that they found difficult to reconcile with that first identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/the-1975-lumberton-ufo-wave-police-across-north-carolina-report-a-silent-v-shaped-craft/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The 1975 Lumberton UFO Wave: Police Across North Carolina Report a Silent V-Shaped Craft - Openminds.tv...</span></span></span>

Stanton’s account became the defining episode. From roughly 200 yards away, he said he stopped his patrol car, switched off its engine and observed an object close to the tower. He described a triangular form about the apparent size of his police car, rows of lights along its sides and a large forward spotlight. According to the later investigative account, the beam shone down and illuminated lettering on the water tower; the light then went out and the object accelerated away silently. His estimate that it was roughly 200 feet above the ground depended on treating the nearby tower as a reliable distance and height reference, making the report more specific than a sighting against an empty sky—but still dependent on visual judgement at night.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://s3images.coroflot.com/user_files/individual_files/original_pdf_221275_cenkof8pltjfkxtu3zp3xxmx0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: s3images.coroflot.com">[Coroflot]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">s3images.coroflot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation...</span></span></span>

Police radio traffic appears to have linked this observation to reports farther north. Stanton reportedly alerted officers in the direction of St Pauls. Officer R. T. Hagens and civilian John McPherson then described an intense light rising from behind trees outside the town. Hagens said it moved slowly at first, accelerated, changed direction and disappeared within approximately 15 to 20 seconds. Both men reported no sound, and Hagens produced a sketch showing a broad curved or V-shaped outline with lights along both sides and a central red light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://s3images.coroflot.com/user_files/individual_files/original_pdf_221275_cenkof8pltjfkxtu3zp3xxmx0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: s3images.coroflot.com">[Coroflot]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">s3images.coroflot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation...</span></span></span>

Another concentration followed around 1 a.m. on 4 April, when reports reached police and sheriffs’ departments beyond Robeson County. A later reconstruction names Newton Grove officer John Hayes among those alerted that an unusual object appeared to be moving towards his area. Reports continued intermittently until 9 April and then apparently stopped, producing the sharply bounded “wave” or “flap” that distinguishes Lumberton from isolated North Carolina sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://s3images.coroflot.com/user_files/individual_files/original_pdf_221275_cenkof8pltjfkxtu3zp3xxmx0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: s3images.coroflot.com">[Coroflot]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">s3images.coroflot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation...</span></span></span>

## Police and civilian witness accounts

The law-enforcement involvement is the strongest feature of the Lumberton case, but it should not be overstated. Police officers are accustomed to observing vehicles, judging suspicious activity and reporting details under pressure. Their testimony therefore carries more contextual weight than an anonymous account recorded decades later. Officers also had radios that could establish a rough chronology and direct separate patrols towards an object’s reported path.

Police status does not, however, turn visual estimates into instrument readings. Night-time observers can misjudge an object’s distance, size and speed, especially when its only visible features are bright lights. An apparently car-sized object nearby and a much larger object farther away may create nearly the same image on the eye. Reported rapid acceleration can also result when a light changes brightness, disappears behind cloud, turns away or is viewed from a moving patrol car.

What makes Lumberton more interesting than a simple misidentified star is the reported combination of features. Zeidman’s investigation characterised the recurring object as brightly lit and V- or wedge-shaped, with alleged behaviour ranging from hovering to rapid flight, flat turns, a manoeuvrable spotlight and little or no noise. Her summary said that only three of more than 50 witnesses reported sound, even though many believed the object was within about 200 feet. Those are striking claims, but the distances and speeds were generally estimates rather than values established by radar, triangulation or calibrated photography.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://s3images.coroflot.com/user_files/individual_files/original_pdf_221275_cenkof8pltjfkxtu3zp3xxmx0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: s3images.coroflot.com">[Coroflot]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">s3images.coroflot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Triangular UFOs: An Estimate of the Situation...</span></span></span>

The reports were not perfectly uniform. Some witnesses chiefly saw a brilliant light; others perceived a structured triangle or V. Colours are variously given as red, white, green or combinations of them. Some descriptions emphasised a spotlight, while others centred on rows of lights. This could indicate different viewing angles of one conventional or unconventional object, several unrelated objects folded into one wave, or the way expectations spread once officers and residents knew that unusual lights were being reported.

Radio communication cuts both ways. It supports the idea that reports occurred in real time rather than being invented long afterwards. It could also have influenced later descriptions: once a dispatcher mentioned a V-shaped object with coloured lights, subsequent observers may have looked for and interpreted ambiguous lights within that shared framework. The best evidential test would be to compare independently written statements made before witnesses discussed the event. Publicly accessible accounts do not make it possible to perform that comparison systematically.

Civilians broadened the witness pool, and some reportedly observed the phenomenon alongside officers. That reduces the likelihood that the entire story arose from police rumour alone. Nevertheless, the surviving case is still mostly testimonial. No clear contemporary photograph or film has emerged, and no publicly documented radar track has been tied securely to the sightings. Claims of a possible landing or physical traces appear in later descriptions, but the accessible material does not provide a sufficiently complete chain of documentation to establish that an object touched the ground. The cautious conclusion is that several people reported unusual lights and apparent structured forms—not that a physical craft of unknown origin was demonstrated.

## What ordinary explanations can and cannot resolve

Aircraft, helicopters, astronomical objects, balloons and satellites have all been proposed in general discussions of the wave. Each can account for part of the testimony, but no publicly available reconstruction has convincingly matched one explanation to every major report.

**Aircraft or helicopters** naturally explain red, green and white lights, apparent directional movement and activity across several counties. The Fayetteville region also sits within a part of North Carolina long associated with military and civilian aviation. Yet the repeated claims of hovering at low altitude, extreme acceleration and silence would require substantial witness error, unusual viewing geometry or the blending of several different aircraft reports. The officers’ initial helicopter hypothesis is plausible, but their insistence that the object lacked rotor noise was one reason investigators rejected it.

**Venus or another bright celestial object** could produce a stationary or slowly shifting light, particularly through broken cloud after unsettled weather. Such objects are frequently misperceived as moving when viewed from a travelling vehicle. Venus cannot, by itself, readily account for accounts of rows of lights, a downward beam illuminating a water tower or an object rising from behind nearby trees. Those details may be inaccurate estimates or later elaborations, but they prevent a simple planet identification from resolving the whole case.

**Balloons and satellites** may explain silent luminous objects, but the reported hovering, low-altitude manoeuvres and spotlight behaviour are poor matches. Satellites also move on predictable paths and do not stop, reverse or illuminate local structures. In 1975 there was no modern phenomenon comparable to today’s conspicuous trains of newly launched communications satellites, making that particular explanation less relevant than it is for current sighting waves.

**Atmospheric effects after thunderstorms** deserve consideration because recent summaries place the first sightings shortly after severe weather. Lightning-related luminous phenomena, reflections from low cloud and distorted distant lights can all appear unusual. The difficulty is that “atmospheric effect” remains a broad possibility rather than a tested explanation unless historical weather observations, sightlines and the timing of each report can be reconstructed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/the-1975-lumberton-ufo-wave-police-across-north-carolina-report-a-silent-v-shaped-craft/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The 1975 Lumberton UFO Wave: Police Across North Carolina Report a Silent V-Shaped Craft - Openminds.tv...</span></span></span>

The most economical interpretation may be that the Lumberton wave was a mixture: aircraft or distant lights generated some initial reports, police radio and news coverage increased attention, and subsequent witnesses interpreted several unrelated stimuli through the emerging image of a silent V-shaped object. This explains why details overlap without requiring every observer to have seen precisely the same thing. It remains an inference, however, not a demonstrated solution.

## The archival gaps that prevent a verdict

The Lumberton case received more organised investigation than many local UFO reports. CUFOS published Zeidman’s dedicated monograph in 1976, and copies or associated material entered specialist collections. Rice University’s Woodson Research Center lists *The Lumberton Report* in the Jacques Vallée papers, but the relevant collection material is currently restricted, with the listed series not due to open until 2028 and later case files remaining closed until 2031.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archives.library.rice.edu/repositories/2/archival_objects/343235" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.library.rice.edu">[Rice University Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.library.rice.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">archival objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rice University ArchivesThe Lumberton Report: UFO Activity in S. That restricted material may eventually clarify how many witnesses gave first-hand statements, whether police logs were copied, how interviews were conducted and which accounts were independent. It may also reveal inconsistencies omitted from later summaries. Until then, researchers often rely on Zeidman’s conclusions as quoted by later writers, rather than checking every statement against the underlying interview notes.

The timing of the investigation matters as well. Project Blue Book, the United States Air Force’s formal UFO study, had ended in 1969, six years before Lumberton. The wave therefore did not receive the kind of Air Force case file associated with earlier North Carolina incidents. Investigation was led by civilian UFO researchers, including people connected with CUFOS and J. Allen Hynek’s post-Blue Book work. This produced a richer narrative record than a newspaper clipping alone, but not an official federal finding.

Later coverage has mainly revived rather than resolved the story. Recent articles repeat the police testimony, triangular shape and silent flight, while sometimes increasing the witness totals or calling the episode one of America’s earliest major triangle waves. Such historical comparisons are interesting, but similarity to later triangular-UFO reports does not establish that all involved the same phenomenon. Nor does a later witness or investigator’s continued conviction supply missing measurements from 1975.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/the-1975-lumberton-ufo-wave-police-across-north-carolina-report-a-silent-v-shaped-craft/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The 1975 Lumberton UFO Wave: Police Across North Carolina Report a Silent V-Shaped Craft - Openminds.tv...</span></span></span>

The fairest verdict is therefore **unresolved but incompletely documented**. Lumberton is stronger than a lone, anonymous light report because it involved clustered observations, named police witnesses, contemporaneous radio communication and a focused follow-up investigation. It is weaker than its reputation sometimes suggests because the public record lacks decisive sensor data, clear imagery, complete original statements and an independently reproducible analysis. Within North Carolina’s UFO history, its lasting importance lies not in proving that an extraordinary craft visited Robeson County, but in showing how a credible-looking multi-witness event can remain resistant to both confirmation and confident debunking when the crucial evidence is testimonial and the archives are fragmented.
