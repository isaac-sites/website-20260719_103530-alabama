## Introduction

The Herbert Schirmer case began as a brief roadside UFO report and became a detailed alien-abduction narrative only after [hypnosis]({{ 'hypnosis-bfdc2e/' | relative_url }}). In the early hours of 3 December 1967, the young Ashland police officer said he saw a metallic, brightly lit object near the junction of US Highway 6 and [Nebraska]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-nebraskas-ufo/' | relative_url }}) Highway 63. His first account described an unusual craft rising rapidly from the road area. The occupants, spacecraft interior, telepathic messages and journey aboard the object emerged later during hypnotic questioning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://podcastufo.com/officer-herbert-schirmer-and-the-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: podcastufo.com">[Podcast UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">podcastufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Podcast UFOOfficer Herbert Schirmer and the UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Staff members from Colorado flew out to...Read more...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Nebraska_e9c60b_herbert_schirmer_cas_9cc8bc-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Schirmer Case" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That sequence is the central issue. There is reasonable evidence that Schirmer promptly reported a disturbing experience and sincerely came to believe an extraordinary event had occurred. There is no comparably strong evidence that a vehicle landed, that occupants approached him or that an abduction filled a genuine period of “[missing time]({{ 'missing-time/' | relative_url }})”. The case matters within Nebraska’s UFO history because it shows how an initially limited sighting could be transformed by psychological investigation into one of America’s best-known contact stories.

## The roadside sighting near Ashland

Schirmer was on overnight police duty in Ashland, a small town west of Omaha. At about 2.30 am, he approached a highway junction and noticed red lights ahead. He initially interpreted them as lights on a stopped lorry, but said that his headlights revealed a silvery or aluminium-coloured object hovering close to the road. He described a flattened or football-like form, flashing red lights and a structure around its middle resembling a walkway. According to his account, it rose with a rushing or siren-like sound, emitted an orange-red glow below and then accelerated out of sight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tumblr.com/stendek/5292103036/condon-report-demonizes-herb-schirmer-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tumblr.com">[Tumblr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tumblr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Condon Report Demonizes Herb Schirmer UFO CaseCondon Report Demonizes Herb Schirmer UFO Case. The strongest evidence that something troubled him that night is the contemporary police record. On returning to the station, Schirmer entered a short statement in the log reporting that he had seen a flying saucer at the junction. This was not a story constructed decades later solely for television or publication. The University of Colorado [investigators]({{ 'investigators/' | relative_url }}) who examined the case also reported that a superior regarded him as dependable and truthful.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://podcastufo.com/officer-herbert-schirmer-and-the-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: podcastufo.com">[Podcast UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">podcastufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Podcast UFOOfficer Herbert Schirmer and the UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Staff members from Colorado flew out to...Read more...</span></span></span>

That does not establish what he saw. No other witness was identified as having observed the object at close range, and the sighting produced no photograph, radar track or independently recorded instrument reading. Investigators found no firm physical trace that could demonstrate a craft had occupied the road. A small fragment reportedly collected from the area contained mainly iron and silicon, but its connection to the sighting was considered too uncertain for it to carry evidential weight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://podcastufo.com/officer-herbert-schirmer-and-the-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: podcastufo.com">[Podcast UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">podcastufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Podcast UFOOfficer Herbert Schirmer and the UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Staff members from Colorado flew out to...Read more...</span></span></span>

Schirmer also reported physical and emotional after-effects, including a headache, a buzzing sensation and a reddish mark on his neck. These details became prominent in later retellings, but there is no surviving clinical evidence showing that the mark had an unusual cause. It appears not to have been documented in a way that would allow later medical assessment or laboratory testing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tumblr.com/stendek/5292103036/condon-report-demonizes-herb-schirmer-ufo-case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tumblr.com">[Tumblr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tumblr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Condon Report Demonizes Herb Schirmer UFO CaseCondon Report Demonizes Herb Schirmer UFO Case. The original roadside report is therefore more substantial than a rumour but much narrower than the story for which the case became famous. It supports the conclusion that Schirmer reported seeing an unidentified object. It does not, by itself, support an abduction.

## How the abduction account emerged

The case attracted the attention of the University of Colorado UFO project, usually called the Condon Committee after its director, physicist Edward Condon. The United States Air Force had commissioned the university to assess whether UFO reports justified continued scientific and military investigation. Schirmer’s incident appeared as Case 42 in the project’s final report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.colorado.edu/coloradan/2021/11/05/condon-report-cu-boulders-historic-ufo-study" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: colorado.edu">[University of Colorado]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">colorado.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on colorado.edu.</span></span></span>

Schirmer travelled to Colorado in February 1968 for interviews and psychological testing. Psychologist R. Leo Sprinkle conducted sessions involving hypnotic or partly hypnotic techniques. During this process, Schirmer gave a much longer narrative than the one recorded on the morning of the sighting. Sprinkle later became closely associated with UFO experiencers and the use of hypnosis in alleged contact cases; his archived papers show that UFOs, paranormal experiences and the psychology of “contactees” became major professional interests.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://podcastufo.com/officer-herbert-schirmer-and-the-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: podcastufo.com">[Podcast UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">podcastufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Podcast UFOOfficer Herbert Schirmer and the UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Staff members from Colorado flew out to...Read more...</span></span></span>

Under questioning, Schirmer described beings emerging from the object and preventing him from drawing his pistol. He said they were roughly human in form but had elongated heads, narrow eyes and unusual skin. Their uniforms supposedly bore a winged-serpent emblem. He recalled being taken aboard the craft, shown its interior and told about its propulsion, energy sources and purpose on Earth. Some versions included claims about extraterrestrial bases and the visitors’ interest in electricity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nalda.ch/products/9786130457662" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nalda.ch">[Nalda]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nalda.ch</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nalda.ch.</span></span></span>

These details are memorable, but their late appearance sharply limits their value as independent evidence. They were not included in the brief station log and were developed in an interview setting already organised around the possibility of a UFO encounter. Later hypnosis by other investigators produced still. Researcher Kevin Randle, although critical of the Condon project in general, has noted that some of the questioning was suggestive. One sequence moved from asking whether Schirmer had drawn his gun to asking whether he had been prevented from doing so, a formulation capable of encouraging a particular interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2008/10/schirmer-abduction.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">schirmer abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It was in the 1960s that the Air Force... The distinction is important. Hypnosis can produce vivid experiences and sincere confidence without verifying that the recalled events happened. United States Department of Justice guidance warns that information obtained in a hypnotic state cannot simply be assumed accurate and that hypnosis should be used only rarely in investigations because of serious reliability objections. Experimental research likewise indicates that hypnosis may increase confidence in memories without increasing their accuracy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.justice.gov/archives/jm/criminal-resource-manual-287-use-hypnosis-purpose" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: justice.gov">[Department of Justice]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">justice.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Department of Justice287. Use of Hypnosis—Purpose</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Department of Justice287. Use of Hypnosis—Purpose</span></span></span>

This does not mean Schirmer knowingly invented the occupants. A person can honestly report a detailed hypnotic memory that combines genuine recollection, imagination, expectations and suggestions received during questioning. The problem is evidential: once such material has been generated, neither sincerity nor descriptive richness can reliably separate recovered fact from confabulation.

## Was there really missing time?

The “missing-time” claim rests on an apparent difference between the time Schirmer associated with the sighting and the time at which he returned to the station. Accounts commonly describe a gap of approximately 20 minutes, although the precise reconstruction is not as secure as later summaries often imply. It is unclear who first identified the discrepancy, whether all relevant clocks were synchronised and how closely Schirmer had checked the time before or during the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://midimagic.sgc-hosting.com/ufocond.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: midimagic.sgc-hosting.com">[MidiMagic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">midimagic.sgc-hosting.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Midi Magic The Condon Cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Condon Cases - MidiMagicCase 42: Ashland NE 12/03/1967 0230 CST Herbert Schirmer. A clock discrepancy is not automatically evidence of amnesia. People routinely estimate short periods poorly, particularly late at night, when tired, frightened or intensely focused on an unexpected sight. Schirmer had been working an overnight shift and believed that he had just observed something extraordinary. A delay could have included watching the object, leaving his vehicle, examining the area, driving back, pausing or simply misremembering when he last checked the time.

The chronology also creates a practical difficulty for the later account. Twenty minutes is a short interval in which to accommodate the approach of several beings, immobilisation or mental control, entry into a craft, an extensive tour, a conversation about propulsion and extraterrestrial operations, departure and the restoration of ordinary awareness. That does not make the story impossible on its own terms, but it shows why the clock gap cannot function as straightforward corroboration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2008/10/schirmer-abduction.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">schirmer abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It was in the 1960s that the Air Force... Most importantly, the abduction explanation was inferred after investigators began probing the gap. There was no independent record showing Schirmer’s location throughout the period, no second witness to his alleged boarding and no physical data demonstrating that an event occurred during the unaccounted-for minutes. The missing time is therefore better described as an uncertain timetable discrepancy than as a documented interval of erased memory.

## What the official investigation actually found

The Condon Committee did not establish that Schirmer had encountered an extraterrestrial craft. Its investigators acknowledged that people who knew him regarded him as trustworthy, and Sprinkle appears to have believed that Schirmer was relating an experience he considered real. Those judgements address honesty, not external accuracy. A sincere witness can still misidentify an object, misunderstand an experience or develop false memories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://podcastufo.com/officer-herbert-schirmer-and-the-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: podcastufo.com">[Podcast UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">podcastufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Podcast UFOOfficer Herbert Schirmer and the UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Staff members from Colorado flew out to...Read more...</span></span></span>

Psychological testing produced mixed and disputed interpretations. One assessment cited in later discussions suggested that Schirmer’s word-association performance raised doubts about either his honesty or his own confidence in the sighting. Sprinkle’s view was more sympathetic, but his notes also referred to sleep disturbance, buzzing sensations, unusual dreams and troubling nocturnal behaviour reported by Schirmer. These details do not provide a diagnosis, and they should not be used to dismiss him casually. They do, however, complicate the image of an uncomplicated witness whose recollection can be treated as a neutral recording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2008/10/schirmer-abduction.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">schirmer abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It was in the 1960s that the Air Force... The final study treated the case as unverified rather than as proof of a landed vehicle. The wider Condon Report concluded that continued large-scale scientific investigation of UFOs was unlikely to produce significant discoveries, although critics later challenged both its methods and its overall judgement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://midimagic.sgc-hosting.com/ufocond.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: midimagic.sgc-hosting.com">[MidiMagic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">midimagic.sgc-hosting.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Midi Magic The Condon Cases</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Condon Cases - MidiMagicCase 42: Ashland NE 12/03/1967 0230 CST Herbert Schirmer. Even where correctly reported, that would show only that the examiner found no clear signs of deliberate deception under the conditions of the test. It could not determine whether a perceived event happened as described, whether memories created under hypnosis were historically accurate or whether an unusual object was extraterrestrial.

## Why the evidence weakened over time

Later retellings increased the story’s detail but did not add proportionate verification. Books, interviews and documentaries repeated descriptions of the occupants, their emblem, the craft’s interior and messages about alien bases. Each repetition made the narrative more culturally recognisable, yet none supplied the missing independent evidence: a corroborating witness, original recording of the full event, reliable medical documentation, verifiable physical trace or technical data.

Some later material is also difficult to audit because it passed through popular writers rather than neutral investigators. Randle has argued that writer Warren Smith, who helped promote and expand the case, was unreliable and sometimes embellished stories. Randle also noted claims of landing traces that appeared only after earlier searches had failed to find them. Even readers who distrust the Condon Committee therefore have reason to treat later pro-abduction literature cautiously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2008/10/schirmer-abduction.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">schirmer abduction</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It was in the 1960s that the Air Force... Modern memory research further weakens the assumption that hypnosis recovered a hidden recording of the event. Reviews of hypnosis and false memory describe how suggestion, expectation and imaginative involvement can generate convincing recollections. The difficulty is not simply that a hypnotised person might lie. It is that hypnosis can make an inaccurate memory feel subjectively certain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11832514/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCthe role of hypnosis in memory recall and false</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PMCthe role of hypnosis in memory recall and false</span></span></span>

The case would look stronger had the detailed account preceded hypnosis or had specific hypnotic claims led investigators to evidence unknown to Schirmer and independently verified afterwards. Neither occurred. The beings’ statements, spacecraft design and supposed operating bases remained narrative elements rather than testable discoveries.

## Why the case still matters in Nebraska


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Schirmer’s encounter remains one of Nebraska’s most recognisable UFO stories because it joins three powerful ingredients: a police witness, a precise local setting and an apparent gap in time. It also sits at a turning point in American UFO culture, when reports were moving beyond distant lights and discs towards stories of close contact, medical examination and abduction.

</div>

For Nebraska’s state history, its value is therefore less as evidence of extraterrestrial visitation than as a well-documented example of how a case evolves. The police log preserves a modest initial claim. The Condon investigation adds contemporary interviews and testing. Hypnosis then introduces the extraordinary elements that dominate public memory. Later writers and popular culture reinforce those elements while the physical evidence remains unchanged.

Ashland has continued to remember the case through local media, artwork and commercial references, including a graphic retelling and themed products. In 2021, a Nebraska legislative resolution referred to the encounter as part of Ashland’s local identity. These commemorations show that disputed incidents can become durable community folklore even when their factual status remains unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.3newsnow.com/news/local-news/local-ufo-story-revitalized-after-beer-comic-book-comes-out" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: 3newsnow.com">[KMTV 3 News Now Omaha]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">3newsnow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on 3newsnow.com.</span></span></span>

The fairest assessment is that Schirmer probably experienced something he found genuinely alarming and reported it promptly. What that roadside stimulus was cannot now be determined. The alien-abduction account is considerably weaker because its decisive details emerged through a method now recognised as vulnerable to suggestion and false confidence. The case is unresolved at the level of the original sighting, but the evidence does not justify treating the later hypnosis narrative as a verified event.
