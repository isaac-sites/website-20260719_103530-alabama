## Introduction

One of the most important questions raised by the Westchester Boomerang sightings in western [Connecticut]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-connecticuts-ufo/' | relative_url }}) is not whether witnesses saw lights, but why so many people became convinced they were looking at a single gigantic craft. Reports from Danbury, New Fairfield and Ridgefield frequently described an immense dark V or boomerang shape outlined by coloured lights, apparently silent and far larger than any conventional aircraft. Yet the leading sceptical explanation proposes that many of these sightings involved multiple light aircraft flying in formation at night.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

## How common fate and closure shape perception

Psychologists have long recognised that the human brain automatically groups visual elements into meaningful patterns. Two particularly relevant principles are known as **common fate** and **closure**.

Common fate refers to the tendency to treat separate points as parts of the same object when they move together. If several lights maintain the same spacing, speed and direction, observers naturally perceive them as a unified entity rather than as unrelated sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gestaltprinciples.com/principles/common-fate" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gestaltprinciples.com">[gestaltprinciples.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gestaltprinciples.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gestalt Principles Common Fate</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gestalt PrinciplesCommon Fate - Gestalt Principles of DesignDiscover how the Gestalt principle of Common Fate groups elements through sha...</span></span></span>

Closure is the tendency to mentally fill in missing information. When lights appear at the edges of a shape, the brain often assumes there is a continuous object connecting them. Even though observers may only see points of light, they can become convinced they are also seeing the outline of a dark structure between those lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ixdf.org/literature/topics/gestalt-principles" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ixdf.org">[ixdf.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ixdf.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">updated 2026August 30, 2016 — Gestalt Principles are laws of human perception that describe how humans group similar elements, recogniz...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 30, 2016</span></span></span>

In the Connecticut accounts, witnesses often reported rows or arcs of red, green and white lights forming a V-shaped boundary. Once the lights were mentally grouped together, the empty darkness between them could be interpreted as the body of a vast craft. The resulting impression was not simply "a group of lights" but a coherent object with apparent size, shape and direction of travel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

This mechanism helps explain why sincere witnesses could describe a huge boomerang even if no observer could actually see a clearly illuminated fuselage.

## Why darkness distorted distance and size

Night-time observation removes many of the visual clues people normally use to judge distance. During daylight, buildings, trees, terrain and atmospheric perspective help estimate how far away an object is. Against a dark sky, those reference points largely disappear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/Night_Ops_Ch13.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Chapter 13: Night Operations</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation Administration Chapter 13: Night Operations</span></span></span>

When distance becomes uncertain, estimates of size can change dramatically. A formation of aircraft several miles away may appear to be a single object much closer. If the observer unconsciously assumes the lights are nearby, the apparent dimensions of the object increase enormously.

This effect was particularly important in the Hudson Valley and western Connecticut sightings. A wide formation viewed from a distance could span a substantial angle of the sky. Witnesses who interpreted the lights as belonging to one nearby craft could reasonably conclude that the object was hundreds of feet wide or larger.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

The darkness itself also encouraged the impression of a solid object. Instead of seeing empty space between lights, observers often reported a black silhouette blocking out the stars. Human perception tends to favour coherent shapes, and once the mind identifies a possible outline it can become difficult to separate the lights from the imagined structure connecting them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://hci-lecture.de/HCI/topics/human/Human_4_Gestalt_Laws/Human_4_Gestalt_Law/Human_4_Gestalt_Law.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hci-lecture.de">[hci-lecture.de]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hci-lecture.de</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gestalt LawsVisual perception of objects and the environment through recognizing patterns or configurations. ## How turns and light changes mimicked strange manoeuvres

Formation flying can produce effects that appear extraordinary from the ground, especially when observed at night.

According to accounts linked to the [Stormville Airport]({{ 'stormville-trail/' | relative_url }}) explanation, participating pilots used bright lights and sometimes altered colours or lighting patterns while flying in close formation. Some reports stated that lights could be switched or changed simultaneously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

From a distance, several ordinary aircraft executing a coordinated manoeuvre can appear to be one object changing shape. Small adjustments in spacing may transform a V into a crescent, diamond or boomerang. When all the lights move together, observers tend to interpret the change as a manoeuvre by a single craft rather than as separate aircraft altering formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

The same principle applies to apparent hovering. A broad formation moving partly toward the observer may show very little sideways motion against the background sky. To someone on the ground, the lights can seem stationary even while the aircraft continue moving. Pilots associated with the case noted that a very wide formation could appear motionless when viewed from long distances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>
