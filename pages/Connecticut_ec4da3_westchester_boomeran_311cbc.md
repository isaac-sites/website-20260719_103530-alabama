## Introduction

The Westchester Boomerang was probably not one enormous craft. The strongest evidence indicates that many of the celebrated 1983–84 sightings were caused by groups of light aircraft flying close formation at night, their navigation, beacon and landing lights creating the outline of a vast V, crescent or circle. A New York State Police officer reportedly followed one display back to [Stormville Airport]({{ 'stormville-trail/' | relative_url }}), where pilots acknowledged making formation flights and appeared amused by the public reaction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

That explanation matters particularly to [Connecticut]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-connecticuts-ufo/' | relative_url }}). The wave crossed the state line into Fairfield County, producing repeated reports around Danbury, New Fairfield and Ridgefield. It became the state’s best-known mass-sighting episode and a powerful example of how accurately observed lights can be assembled—quite sincerely—into a misleading picture of a single solid object. Yet the aircraft theory is stronger as an explanation of the wave’s recurring pattern than as a proven solution to every individual testimony. Some witnesses described close, low objects, visible structure or movements that are difficult to match precisely with distant aeroplanes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.newstimes.com/local/article/Author-seeks-info-on-UFO-sightings-in-1983-9963803.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newstimes.com">[News-Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newstimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Author seeks info on UFO sightings in 1983 9963803</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">News-TimesAuthor to seek info on area UFO sightings in 198312 Oct 2016 — Zimmermann has organized a UFO conference Saturday at the Danbur...</span></span></span>

## How the sightings spread into western Connecticut

The core wave began in the Hudson Valley in March 1983 and continued prominently through the summer of 1984. Reports clustered in Westchester, Putnam and Dutchess counties in New York, but the geography did not stop at the Connecticut border. Interstate 84, which links the Brewster area with Danbury, provided both a corridor for observers and a natural route along which an airborne formation might be seen from several towns in succession. Connecticut accounts were concentrated mainly in the western part of Fairfield County, especially Danbury, New Fairfield and Ridgefield.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

Witnesses commonly described a line or arc of white, red and green lights. Depending on their viewing angle, the arrangement appeared as a boomerang, triangle, V, horseshoe, circle or broad wing. Many observers concluded that darkness filled the space between the lights, turning the pattern into the edges of a single black object. Its estimated size ranged from that of a large airliner to a football field or more. Reports also emphasised slow movement, apparent hovering and an unexpected lack of engine noise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

The scale of the public reaction grew because the displays were repeated. This was not one fleeting meteor or an isolated light reported by a single household. Drivers stopped along roads; groups watched together; police and local newspapers received calls; photographs and video recordings circulated. Later reporting has put the broader number of Hudson Valley witnesses in the thousands, although such totals combine different dates, locations and descriptions and should not be treated as a count of people who all saw exactly the same phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.timesunion.com/hudsonvalley/history/article/ufo-sightings-westchester-pine-bush-mystery-19363246.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: timesunion.com">[Times Union]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">timesunion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Times Union The mysterious history of the Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Times UnionThe mysterious history of the Hudson Valley UFO sightingsJuly 2, 2024 — 2 Jul 2024 — In the 1980s and &#x27;90s, the Hudson Valley...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2024</span></span></span>

Connecticut recollections preserved the most dramatic version of the story. One account publicised decades later concerned an IBM executive near Danbury who reportedly saw something larger than a Boeing 747 apparently hanging motionless in the sky. Such testimony explains why the wave remained culturally powerful even after the aircraft explanation became widely known: witnesses did not merely recall points of light, but an encounter with something enormous and physically present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.newstimes.com/local/article/Author-seeks-info-on-UFO-sightings-in-1983-9963803.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newstimes.com">[News-Times]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newstimes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Author seeks info on UFO sightings in 1983 9963803</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">News-TimesAuthor to seek info on area UFO sightings in 198312 Oct 2016 — Zimmermann has organized a UFO conference Saturday at the Danbur...</span></span></span>

## Why separate aircraft could look like one enormous object

The formation-flight explanation has unusually concrete support compared with many proposed solutions to UFO cases. New York State Police Sergeant Kenneth V. Spiro told contemporary reporters that a trooper had tracked the lights to Stormville Airport in Dutchess County. According to Spiro, the source was a group of small aircraft flying in formation, with bright lights that could be altered or switched. The trooper reportedly spoke with pilots who were enjoying the confusion their flights had created.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

Accounts of the flights usually identify Cessna 152s and other single-engined light aeroplanes. Pilots were said to practise V-shaped, crescent and other coordinated formations, sometimes with remarkably small apparent gaps between aircraft. As the formation changed heading, banked or turned towards an observer, its outline could shift dramatically. A V could flatten into a line, close into an arc or appear to rotate, without any aircraft performing an extraordinary manoeuvre.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

Several features of the classic “giant craft” follow naturally from this arrangement.

**The coloured outline:** Aircraft carry red and green position lights, white lights and flashing beacons. Seen together, several sets of lights can produce the changing red, green and white pattern repeatedly described by witnesses.

**The dark body:** Against a night sky, the aeroplanes themselves may be difficult or impossible to distinguish. Once the lights appear to form a regular outline, the visual system tends to treat the empty area between them as part of one coherent object.

**The apparent hovering:** A broad formation seen from a long distance may show little angular movement, especially when flying partly towards or away from the observer. Without a reliable distance estimate, slow movement can be interpreted as hovering.

**The sudden disappearance:** If the aircraft turned away, extinguished powerful forward-facing lights or switched lights in a coordinated way, the apparent object could seem to vanish rather than fly away.

**The changing shape:** Different viewing positions transform the projected geometry. Observers separated by only a few miles might describe a V, line or circle while looking at the same formation from different angles.

The psychological mechanism does not require gullibility. Visual research shows that people naturally group nearby elements that move together into a single perceived unit—a principle known as “common fate”. Proximity, similarity, continuity and closure also encourage the brain to infer a complete shape from incomplete visual information. A set of synchronised lights therefore provides almost ideal conditions for perceiving one object rather than several aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3482144/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCA Century of Gestalt Psychology in Visual Perception I</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PMCA Century of Gestalt Psychology in Visual Perception I</span></span></span>

Night-time conditions make the size error more powerful. With no visible fuselage, horizon or landscape detail near the lights, an observer cannot confidently determine distance. A small formation nearby and a much larger object farther away can occupy the same apparent angle in the sky. Federal Aviation Administration guidance warns that darkness and misleading patterns of lights can produce serious errors even among trained pilots, including false movement and difficulty distinguishing airborne, ground and celestial lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/airplane_handbook/12_afh_ch11.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">12 afh ch11</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">12 afh ch11</span></span></span>

Silence is also less decisive than it first appears. Witnesses who believed the lights were only a few hundred feet away expected a loud engine sound. If the formation was actually much farther off, its noise might have been faint, delayed, masked by traffic or wind, or simply not associated with the lights. The absence of heard sound therefore helped reinforce the incorrect distance estimate: the object seemed both close and impossibly quiet because its assumed closeness came from its apparent size, not from a measured range.

## Did the pilots intend a hoax?

Calling the entire episode a deliberate hoax may go further than the evidence permits. Police accounts indicate that some pilots knew their flights were being interpreted as UFOs and found the reaction entertaining. It is also plausible that later flights were made with greater awareness of the effect, particularly once newspapers and television had publicised the mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

However, the formation activity may have begun as recreational flying or a test of piloting skill rather than as a planned deception. The crucial fact is not whether the first pilots set out to manufacture a UFO wave, but that repeated night formations could reproduce its central visual characteristics. Once the publicity cycle began, witnesses were primed to look for a “boomerang”, while pilots had an incentive to repeat a display that was attracting attention.

The label “hoax” can also obscure the difference between the source and the experience. The aircraft were ordinary, but the witnesses’ perception of a giant object could still have been entirely sincere. There is no need to suppose that thousands of people fabricated their observations. A smaller number of recurring aircraft displays, viewed from different places and amplified through news coverage, could generate a much larger body of honest reports.

## Which witness claims remain difficult to reconcile?

The aircraft explanation is compelling because it addresses the wave’s most repetitive and publicly observed pattern. It is less secure when applied retrospectively to every report assigned to the Westchester Boomerang. The surviving testimony varies greatly in quality: some accounts were recorded promptly, while others were retold years later; some concerned distant light formations, while others described a seemingly close structured object.

The most difficult claims fall into three broad groups.

**Very low altitude reports:** Some witnesses said the object passed just above trees or houses. One widely circulated testimony described a football-field-sized form appearing only a few hundred feet overhead for several minutes. A formation of conventional aircraft at that distance should normally have produced visible individual aeroplanes and noticeable engine noise. Such claims do not fit comfortably unless the distance and size estimates were substantially wrong.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://unsolved.com/gallery/hudson-valley-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unsolved.com">[Unsolved Mysteries]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unsolved.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mysteries Hudson Valley UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mysteries Hudson Valley UFO</span></span></span>

**Visible structure between the lights:** Witnesses sometimes reported a solid grey or black surface, an underside, beams, windows or structures hanging beneath the object. A dark patch inferred between coordinated lights could explain a general impression of solidity, but detailed structural descriptions are harder to evaluate. They may represent genuine visual information, silhouettes assembled from the separate aircraft, or elaboration as the observer tried to interpret an ambiguous scene.

**Exceptional motion:** A few reports included vertical acceleration, sudden ascent or instantaneous disappearance. Coordinated lights being extinguished, a banking turn or a change in perspective can mimic abrupt motion. Claims of a large object physically shooting upwards, however, would require better positional, radar or photographic evidence before they could be tested. The eyewitness description alone cannot establish whether the movement belonged to an object or to the lights’ changing geometry.

The reported incident near the Indian Point nuclear plant illustrates the evidential problem. UFO investigators said guards observed a huge object close to the plant and that armed security action followed. The New York Power Authority disputed important parts of that story, while a Nuclear Regulatory Commission spokesman said the agency had no documentation confirming the claimed event. Officials and state police considered formation-flying Cessnas a likely explanation, noting that pilots used the plant as an easily recognised landmark.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

This does not prove that no unusual event occurred near Indian Point. It does show why the most dramatic version should not be repeated as established fact. The central claims depend heavily on second-hand investigator accounts, while the institutional response preserved in later reporting was denial or absence of records.

## How strong is the formation-flight explanation?

For the Westchester Boomerang as a case family, formation flying is the best-supported explanation. It is linked to the correct region and period; police reportedly traced a display to an airport; pilots acknowledged making the flights; and the known appearance of closely grouped aircraft matches the recurring coloured-light formations. It explains why the phenomenon returned on multiple evenings and why its apparent shape changed between V, crescent, circle and line.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Hudson_Valley_UFO_sightings" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1984 Hudson Valley UFO sightings</span></span></span>

It also accounts for a feature that an exotic-craft theory handles poorly: the close resemblance between the identified aircraft displays and the supposedly separate unexplained object. Both appeared in the same region, during the same wave, as slowly moving arrangements of coloured lights with broadly similar shapes. Proposing that an unknown giant craft repeatedly operated alongside visually similar aircraft formations is possible, but it adds an unnecessary second cause unless a particular report contains reliable evidence that excludes the planes.

The explanation is not a complete event-by-event reconstruction. No publicly available master log matches every witness’s time and location with the route, number and identity of each aircraft. Some later sources repeat police statements without providing the original operational records, and several pilots remained anonymous. These gaps justify caution about declaring every sighting individually solved.

They do not, however, restore equal weight to an extraordinary interpretation. Unsupported details in a witness report cannot by themselves outweigh a demonstrated source capable of producing the wave’s defining appearance. The fairest assessment is that formation-flying light aircraft explain a substantial—and probably dominant—share of the Westchester Boomerang sightings. A smaller residue remains unresolved because the information needed to test it was never collected, not because those cases provide strong evidence of a single enormous unknown craft.

## What the wave changed in Connecticut’s UFO history

The Boomerang wave remains important because it was not simply a story about people mistaking one aeroplane for something strange. It demonstrated how several ordinary aircraft, arranged carefully and viewed under poor night-time conditions, could produce a remarkably consistent mass impression of a solid machine hundreds of feet across.

For Connecticut, the episode also shows the weakness of treating witness numbers as a substitute for independent physical evidence. Large groups can sincerely report the same mistaken interpretation when they are exposed to the same visual arrangement. At the same time, dismissing witnesses as foolish misses the real lesson: the formation was persuasive because normal human perception is designed to connect lights moving together into a single object.

Later retellings strengthened the wave’s cultural reputation but did not materially strengthen the case for an unknown giant craft. The most durable investigative finding remains the link to formation flights from Stormville. The unresolved accounts are historically interesting, especially those claiming close range or visible structure, but they lack the radar records, calibrated imagery, aircraft logs and contemporaneous documentation needed to overturn the conventional explanation. The Westchester Boomerang is therefore best understood not as Connecticut’s strongest evidence for an extraordinary vehicle, but as its clearest demonstration of how an extraordinary-looking UFO wave can grow from a repeatable aviation display.

    return encodeURIComponent(String(value || '').replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim()).replace(/%20/g, '+');
  }
  function buildUrl(query, reference, marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket];
    if (!cfg) return '#';
    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
