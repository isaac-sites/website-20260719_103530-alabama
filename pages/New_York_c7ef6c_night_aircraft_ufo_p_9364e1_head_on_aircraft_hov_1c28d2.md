## Introduction

One of the most important reasons a night-time aircraft can be mistaken for a hovering UFO is surprisingly simple: it may be flying almost directly towards the observer. When an aircraft approaches head-on, most of its motion is towards the viewer rather than across the sky. As a result, there is very little sideways movement for the eye to detect. Against a dark background with few visual reference points, a perfectly ordinary aircraft can appear to hang motionless in the air, drift slowly, or hover for minutes at a time. Aviation researchers, pilot training materials and studies of aircraft detection all recognise that objects on a collision course can appear nearly stationary because their apparent position changes only slightly until they draw much closer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/Night_Ops_Ch13.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[faa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When no stars are visible because</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation Administration[PDF] Chapter 13: Night Operations - Federal Aviation AdministrationMarch 8, 2019 — Isolated ground lights...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2019</span></span></span>

## How Head-On Motion Reduces Visible Sideways Travel

Human vision is much better at detecting sideways motion than motion directly towards or away from us. A car crossing a road is easy to track because its position shifts rapidly against the background. A car driving straight towards you changes size but remains in nearly the same place within your field of view.

Aircraft approaching from a distance behave similarly. Even though they may be travelling at well over 100 miles per hour, their apparent position against the sky changes very little. The movement is mostly hidden in the gradual increase of brightness and size rather than obvious sideways travel. To an observer on the ground, the aircraft can therefore seem almost fixed in place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/AccuWeather/posts/these-airplanes-might-look-like-theyve-paused-mid-air-but-its-actually-just-the-/1341872487797394/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[facebook.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">an airplane can appear to “hover” in place. This is not merely a UFO-related claim. Aviation safety research specifically deals with the problem of detecting aircraft on collision courses because such targets can appear stationary for extended periods. A classic warning sign of a collision threat is that another aircraft remains in the same part of the windscreen while steadily growing larger.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.02735" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

For a ground observer at night, the effect can be even stronger because:

* Only a few lights are visible.

The result is a bright light that appears suspended in darkness.

## Why Uncertain Distance Makes Normal Speed Look Impossibly Slow

The illusion becomes more convincing when distance cannot be estimated accurately.

A person who knows an aircraft is ten miles away will interpret slow apparent movement differently from someone who believes the same light is only a few hundred metres distant. Yet at night there is often no reliable way to determine which interpretation is correct. The eye sees angular movement rather than true speed.

If an approaching aircraft is mistakenly assumed to be nearby, its lack of visible sideways motion can seem extraordinary. Witnesses may conclude that a large object is hovering just above the landscape when in reality a much smaller aircraft is several miles away and travelling at normal speed. FAA guidance on night operations notes that darkness and limited visual references can produce significant errors in judging distance, attitude and movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/Night_Ops_Ch13.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When no stars are visible because</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation Administration[PDF] Chapter 13: Night Operations - Federal Aviation AdministrationMarch 8, 2019 — Isolated ground lights...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2019</span></span></span>

Brightness can reinforce the mistake. Powerful landing lights are designed to be visible from long distances. Seen head-on, these lights can dominate the observer's perception while concealing the aircraft's true distance. A bright light often feels closer than it really is, making the apparent lack of motion seem even more remarkable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/Night_Ops_Ch13.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">When no stars are visible because</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation Administration[PDF] Chapter 13: Night Operations - Federal Aviation AdministrationMarch 8, 2019 — Isolated ground lights...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 8, 2019</span></span></span>

## Why Hovering Reports Often Feel Completely Convincing

The illusion is effective because witnesses are not inventing what they see. They genuinely observe a light that appears nearly stationary.

In some cases, the effect is strengthened by subtle changes in direction. An aircraft following an approach path may appear to stop, creep sideways, then stop again as the viewing geometry changes. Small course corrections can create the impression of intelligent manoeuvring even though the aircraft remains on a routine flight path.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/AccuWeather/posts/these-airplanes-might-look-like-theyve-paused-mid-air-but-its-actually-just-the-/1341872487797394/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">an airplane can appear to “hover” in place. Night-time conditions also make it difficult to judge whether an object is approaching or receding. Aviation training materials describe situations in which lights can create misleading impressions of direction and movement, particularly when observers lack reliable reference points.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.chinook-helicopter.com/standards/Illusions/Visual_Illusions.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: chinook-helicopter.com">[chinook-helicopter.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">chinook-helicopter.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Visual Illusions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">At night, an aircraft may appear to be going away when, in fact, it is approaching. ## How the Effect Shaped Hudson Valley Witness Reports

The Hudson Valley UFO wave remains one of New York's most famous aerial mysteries. Between the early and mid-1980s, thousands of residents reported enormous illuminated craft, frequently described as V-shaped, boomerang-shaped or city-sized objects moving silently over the region.
