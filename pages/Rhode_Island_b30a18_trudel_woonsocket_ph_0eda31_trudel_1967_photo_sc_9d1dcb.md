## Introduction

Harold Trudel’s seven daylight photographs from East Woonsocket in June 1967 remain among the best-known UFO images associated with [Rhode Island]({{ 'what-really-defines-rhode-islands-ufo/' | relative_url }}). At first glance they appear to show a large domed craft moving near high-voltage power lines. Yet the photographs could never establish one of the most important facts in photographic evidence: scale. The images recorded only a two-dimensional view. Without a measured distance to the object, a second camera angle, independent witnesses, or physical reference points known to occupy the same space as the object, the photographs could not determine whether the subject was a large craft hundreds of metres away or a much smaller object much closer to the camera. However, the photographs do not record any information about distance. A camera lens compresses three-dimensional space into a flat image. Unless investigators know how far away the object is, its apparent size in the frame reveals very little. A small object close to the camera can appear identical to a much larger object farther away. Photographic analysts have long noted that determining scale requires additional information beyond the image itself, such as triangulation from multiple viewpoints, known dimensions at the object's location, or precise measurements of the scene.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-04-4.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical InquirerIssue-04-4.pdfJames W. ## Why Power Lines and Perspective Do Not Establish Scale

Many viewers assume the power lines visible in the photographs solve the scale problem. If the object was beside or above the lines, it might seem possible to estimate its size.

The difficulty is that the photographs do not prove the object and the wires occupied the same plane in space. In a photograph, objects at very different distances can appear to overlap. An aeroplane thousands of metres away can seem to pass directly behind a nearby lamppost, even though they are nowhere near each other.

For the power lines to provide a reliable measurement, investigators would need evidence that the photographed object was genuinely adjacent to them. The images alone do not provide that proof. The object appears near the wires, but its exact position relative to them cannot be measured from a single viewpoint. As a result:

* The object could have been large and distant.
* It could have been medium-sized and somewhat nearer.
* It could have been very small and close to the camera.

## Why a Sequence of Images Still Was Not Enough

The existence of seven photographs is often presented as a strength of the case, and in some respects it is. Multiple frames can reveal whether an object changes position, shape or orientation.

Yet a sequence does not automatically solve the scale problem.

All seven Trudel photographs were taken from broadly similar viewpoints. Because there was no widely separated second camera position, investigators could not use parallax—the apparent shift in an object's position when viewed from different locations—to calculate distance. Parallax is one of the most reliable ways to determine how far away an object is. Without it, the object's range remains largely speculative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-04-4.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical InquirerIssue-04-4.pdfJames W. The photographs therefore documented appearance but not geometry. They showed something in front of the camera, but they did not provide the information needed to calculate how large that something was.

## Missing Witnesses, Originals and Measurements

The scale question became even harder because other forms of corroboration were absent.

No independent witness was known to have photographed the object from another location. No radar track fixed its position in the sky. No survey measurements were taken at the scene to reconstruct the object's location. The case depended primarily on Trudel's account of where the object had been.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newengland.com/yankee/history/ufo-sightings-alien-sightings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newengland.com">[newengland.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newengland.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aliens in New England?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Timeline of UFO Sightings and...As the UFO hovered over the power lines, Trudel snapped pictures for about five minutes. Later researchers also faced limitations involving the photographic record itself. Questions arose over the availability and quality of original materials used for analysis, making it difficult to perform the sort of detailed geometric study that might have strengthened or weakened claims about the object's true distance. When crucial measurements are missing at the time an event occurs, later investigators often cannot recover them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2019/03/Issue-04-4.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical InquirerIssue-04-4.pdfJames W. As a result, the central question—how far away was the object?—remained unanswered.

## How the Scale Problem Fed the Hoax Debate

The inability to establish scale opened the door to alternative explanations.

If the object's distance was unknown, then the possibility of a small nearby model could not be ruled out from the photographs alone. Later sceptical discussions of Trudel's photographic claims frequently focused on exactly this issue. ## What the Scale Question Means for the Rhode Island Case

For Rhode Island UFO history, the Trudel photographs remain important because they are visually striking and unusually clear compared with many reported sightings. They helped make the Woonsocket area one of the state's most discussed UFO locations and continue to appear in books, articles and retrospectives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newengland.com/yankee/history/ufo-sightings-alien-sightings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newengland.com">[newengland.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newengland.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aliens in New England?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A Timeline of UFO Sightings and...As the UFO hovered over the power lines, Trudel snapped pictures for about five minutes. Their enduring weakness is that they never answered the most basic quantitative question: how big was the object? The photographs show a shape, but they do not provide a reliable way to calculate distance, diameter or altitude. Because power lines, trees and perspective alone cannot solve that problem, the images cannot demonstrate that a large craft was present.

That does not automatically prove a hoax, nor does it confirm an extraordinary vehicle. It means the photographs occupy an uncertain middle ground. They are genuine photographs of something seen through a camera lens, but they lack the measurements needed to transform a striking image into strong evidence of a large unidentified craft over Rhode Island.
