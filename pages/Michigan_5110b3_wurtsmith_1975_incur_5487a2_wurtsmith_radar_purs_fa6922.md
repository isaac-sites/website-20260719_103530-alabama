## Introduction

The most important point about the KC-135 pursuit during the [Wurtsmith]({{ 'wurtsmith/' | relative_url }}) Air Force Base incident is that it did not establish extraordinary aircraft performance. What it established was far narrower: a military tanker crew reported seeing lights over Lake Huron, obtained a radar return known as a “skin paint”, tracked it for a limited distance, believed there may have been a second target, and then lost contact. The surviving Air Force messages do not describe impossible speeds, abrupt accelerations, or continuous radar tracking. Instead, they document a short and fragmentary interception attempt during a confusing security incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/assort1.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113455-667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[Defense Logistics Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Defense Logistics Agency[PDF] NMCC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I, - Executive Services DirectorateSeptember 26, 2012 — Tanker lost all contact approY.iwatell&#x27; sightings at Wurtsmith AFB, Michigan at...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

## How the KC-135 Was Sent to Investigate

After reports of a low-flying unidentified aircraft near Wurtsmith Air Force Base on the night of 30–31 October 1975, military authorities sought additional information beyond the ground observations. A KC-135 Stratotanker returning to the area was directed towards Lake Huron to help identify radar contacts associated with the security alarm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/assort1.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113455-667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[Defense Logistics Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Defense Logistics Agency[PDF] NMCC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I, - Executive Services DirectorateSeptember 26, 2012 — Tanker lost all contact approY.iwatell&#x27; sightings at Wurtsmith AFB, Michigan at...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

The tanker was not a dedicated fighter interceptor. It was a large aerial refuelling aircraft whose crew happened to be available in the vicinity. That context is important because the mission was essentially an ad hoc identification effort rather than a carefully planned air-defence interception. The crew's observations therefore became one piece of evidence among several, alongside security-force reports and ground radar observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/assort1.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113455-667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[Defense Logistics Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Defense Logistics Agency[PDF] NMCC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I, - Executive Services DirectorateSeptember 26, 2012 — Tanker lost all contact approY.iwatell&#x27; sightings at Wurtsmith AFB, Michigan at...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

## What Visual and Radar Contact Was Reported

The key contemporary report states that the tanker, flying at approximately 2,700 feet, obtained both visual contact and a radar return while over Lake Huron. In radar terminology, a “skin paint” generally means a direct radar reflection from a physical object rather than an inferred position or procedural track. According to the report, the crew maintained this contact for roughly twenty miles while the target moved south-eastward.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/751030wurtsmith_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">751030wurtsmith dir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportA tanker at 2700 feet had visual and skin paint out over Lake Huron for about 20 miles heading SE. The report states that the crew “thinks” it saw a second skin paint rather than confirming a second aircraft beyond doubt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/751030wurtsmith_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">751030wurtsmith dir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportA tanker at 2700 feet had visual and skin paint out over Lake Huron for about 20 miles heading SE. ## What Happened When Contact Was Lost

One of the most frequently misunderstood elements of the Wurtsmith story concerns the end of the pursuit.

The original message reports that the tanker lost all contact approximately thirty-five miles south-east of the base over Lake Huron. The record does not state that the target accelerated away at extraordinary speed. Nor does it describe a dramatic radar event in which a tracked object instantly vanished while being continuously monitored. It simply records that contact was lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/751030wurtsmith_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">751030wurtsmith dir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportA tanker at 2700 feet had visual and skin paint out over Lake Huron for about 20 miles heading SE. Tanker reports he think...</span></span></span>

Other accounts connected with the incident mention lights being extinguished or becoming difficult to follow. In practical aviation terms, losing visual contact with a distant light source at night is not unusual. Radar contact can also be intermittent, especially at relatively low altitude, over water, and during an improvised tracking effort rather than a dedicated interception. The surviving documents do not provide the detailed radar data that would be necessary to reconstruct exactly why contact ended.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/assort1.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113455-667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[Defense Logistics Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Defense Logistics Agency[PDF] NMCC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I, - Executive Services DirectorateSeptember 26, 2012 — Tanker lost all contact approY.iwatell&#x27; sightings at Wurtsmith AFB, Michigan at...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

## Why Losing the Targets Does Not Prove Exotic Speed

Many later retellings implicitly treat the disappearance of the radar contact as proof that the object outran the KC-135 or demonstrated capabilities beyond known aircraft. The contemporary record does not support that conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/assort1.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113455-667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[Defense Logistics Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Defense Logistics Agency[PDF] NMCC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I, - Executive Services DirectorateSeptember 26, 2012 — Tanker lost all contact approY.iwatell&#x27; sightings at Wurtsmith AFB, Michigan at...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

* The target moved beyond effective visual or radar tracking range.
* The crew lost sight of lights that were switched off or obscured.
* Radar returns became intermittent.
* More than one ordinary aircraft may have been involved, creating confusion about which target was being followed.
* The original ground reports, radar observations and airborne observations may not all have referred to exactly the same object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.esd.whs.mil/Portals/54/Documents/FOID/Reading%20Room/UFOsandUAPs/assort1.pdf?ver=2017-05-22-113455-667" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: esd.whs.mil">[Defense Logistics Agency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">esd.whs.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Defense Logistics Agency[PDF] NMCC</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">I, - Executive Services DirectorateSeptember 26, 2012 — Tanker lost all contact approY.iwatell&#x27; sightings at Wurtsmith AFB, Michigan at...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2012</span></span></span>

## What the Pursuit Actually Established

Viewed narrowly, the KC-135 episode strengthened the Wurtsmith case by showing that the incident was not based solely on a single witness or a ground observer's mistake. A military aircraft crew reported visual contact and at least one radar return associated with the event. That gives the incident greater evidential weight than many routine UFO reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/751030wurtsmith_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">751030wurtsmith dir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportA tanker at 2700 feet had visual and skin paint out over Lake Huron for about 20 miles heading SE. Viewed more broadly, however, the pursuit did not establish the extraordinary claims that later grew around it.
