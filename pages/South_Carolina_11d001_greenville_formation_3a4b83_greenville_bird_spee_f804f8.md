## Introduction

A flock of low-flying birds is one of the most frequently suggested explanations for the 13 May 1952 [Greenville]({{ 'greenville/' | relative_url }}) sighting, and it deserves serious consideration. The four amateur astronomers at Furman University reported four reddish oval objects crossing almost overhead in a compact formation before disappearing in about three seconds. Because no reliable estimate of distance exists, the reported speed cannot be separated from the objects' altitude. A nearby flock could appear to sweep across a large section of the sky very quickly, while an object at high altitude making the same apparent movement would require a far greater true speed. The question is therefore not simply whether birds can fly that fast, but whether the reported geometry, lighting and viewing conditions are consistent with a nearby flock rather than something more distant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.auricmedia.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Project_Blue_Book.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: auricmedia.net">[auricmedia.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">auricmedia.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">QUsstfteaifon cancelled -or changed to.iFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

## Reconstructing the reported path across the sky

The observation lasted only around three seconds. According to the Project [Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book-e3c41a/' | relative_url }}) summary, the formation was first noticed nearly overhead before disappearing towards a low elevation. The witnesses described four reddish-yellow or reddish-brown oval objects arranged in a diamond that appeared to wobble while travelling. One observer believed they were probably geese, whereas the other three disagreed. Investigators ultimately classified the case as "Unknown", not because they ruled out birds, but because they considered neither explanation decisive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.auricmedia.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Project_Blue_Book.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: auricmedia.net">[auricmedia.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">auricmedia.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">QUsstfteaifon cancelled -or changed to.iFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

That brief duration is central to the bird hypothesis. Human observers naturally estimate speed from angular motion across the sky rather than from true velocity. Without knowing the range to the objects, apparent speed is ambiguous. A flock only a few hundred metres away can cross a large angular distance in seconds, creating the impression of extraordinary motion even while flying at ordinary bird speeds.

This uncertainty explains why the three-second observation alone cannot establish whether the objects were exceptionally fast. It constrains only their apparent movement, not their actual velocity.

## What bird speed implies about altitude and distance

Most geese and large ducks cruise at roughly 40 to 60 mph (65 to 95 km/h), with higher speeds possible under favourable conditions such as tailwinds. Those figures are entirely compatible with a formation traversing a wide section of the observer's field of view if the birds were relatively low and nearby.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/everyday-mysteries/categories/zoology/item/why-do-geese-fly-in-a-v/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Library of Congress Why do geese fly in a V? &#124; Library of Congress</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Library of CongressWhy do geese fly in a V? &#124; Library of CongressNovember 19, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 19, 2019</span></span></span>

The geometry changes rapidly with distance:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Nearby flock:** At a few hundred metres' range, birds flying at normal cruising speeds can appear to sweep dramatically across the sky in only a few seconds.
* **Moderate altitude:** At greater distances, the same birds would cover a much smaller angular distance during the same interval and would appear slower.
* **High-altitude object:** If the Greenville objects were thousands of metres away, a three-second crossing would imply a much higher true speed than ordinary birds can achieve.

</div>

This dependence on distance is why the Blue Book investigators did not dismiss the bird explanation solely because of the reported speed. The observation simply lacked enough geometric information to calculate a meaningful velocity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.auricmedia.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Project_Blue_Book.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: auricmedia.net">[auricmedia.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">auricmedia.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">QUsstfteaifon cancelled -or changed to.iFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

The reported diamond formation is also compatible with bird flight, although not in a straightforward way. Migrating geese are best known for V formations, but smaller groups can temporarily adopt irregular diamonds, staggered lines or compact clusters while changing position. Four birds are especially capable of forming short-lived geometric arrangements that differ from the classic V.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/everyday-mysteries/categories/zoology/item/why-do-geese-fly-in-a-v/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Library of Congress Why do geese fly in a V? &#124; Library of Congress</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Library of CongressWhy do geese fly in a V? &#124; Library of CongressNovember 19, 2019...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 19, 2019</span></span></span>

## Could the appearance also fit birds?

Speed is only one part of the puzzle. The visual description also has to be considered.

The witnesses described reddish oval objects that seemed to wobble and to fly "sideways". Those impressions can arise naturally when viewing birds from below at night. Changes in wing position during flapping alter the visible shape from moment to moment, while alternating reflections from the body and wings can create an impression of wobbling or shifting orientation.

However, the lighting conditions complicate the explanation. Blue Book's analyst noted that the observers had deliberately chosen a dark observing site away from nearby ground lighting. The report argued that bright reflections from birds would normally require a significant source of illumination, and the investigators questioned whether sufficient light was available to make the birds appear so conspicuous. That reservation became one reason the case remained unresolved rather than being identified as birds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.auricmedia.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Project_Blue_Book.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: auricmedia.net">[auricmedia.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">auricmedia.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">QUsstfteaifon cancelled -or changed to.iFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

The reddish colour likewise cuts both ways. Artificial lighting reflected from feathers can produce warm tones, particularly from sodium or incandescent sources, but the absence of strong nearby lighting weakened that explanation in the investigators' view.

## How uncertain estimates affect the conclusion

The strongest argument for the bird theory is not that every reported detail matches perfectly. Rather, it is that the observation contains too little geometric information to exclude an ordinary nearby flock.

Several key quantities remain unknown:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the actual altitude of the objects;
* their distance from the observers;
* the precise angular path across the sky;
* whether the witnesses saw reflected light or the birds themselves.

</div>

Without those measurements, neither apparent size nor apparent speed can be converted into true dimensions or velocity.

Conversely, the evidence does not positively identify birds either. Three of the four observers rejected that interpretation despite being experienced sky-watchers, and the Air Force analyst acknowledged genuine difficulties with the lighting conditions. The official conclusion therefore reflected uncertainty rather than endorsement of an extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.auricmedia.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Project_Blue_Book.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: auricmedia.net">[auricmedia.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">auricmedia.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">QUsstfteaifon cancelled -or changed to.iFebruary 11, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 11, 2026</span></span></span>

Within the broader Greenville Formation Sighting and the Bird Theory discussion, this is the critical point: the reported three-second sweep makes a low-altitude flock physically plausible, but only if the formation was relatively close to the observers. Because the available evidence cannot establish that distance, the bird explanation remains credible without becoming conclusive.
