## Introduction

The most unusual aspect of Joe Simonton's 18 April 1961 Eagle River encounter was not his description of a hovering craft, but the fact that he produced physical evidence. Simonton claimed that three small occupants of the craft handed him several thin cakes after he provided them with water. Because the alleged "[space pancakes]({{ 'space-pancakes/' | relative_url }})" could be examined in a laboratory, investigators had an opportunity rarely available in UFO cases: they could test an object rather than rely solely on witness testimony.

The results, however, proved decidedly ordinary. Government laboratory examinations found no exotic materials, no unusual chemistry and no abnormal radioactivity. Instead, the cakes closely resembled familiar buckwheat pancakes made from common terrestrial ingredients. ## How the pancake samples were tested

After Simonton reported his experience to local officials, portions of the cakes were passed to investigators and eventually reached the U.S. Air Force's Aerospace Technical Intelligence Center (ATIC) at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base. Rather than relying on visual inspection alone, the Air Force requested laboratory analysis from specialists within the U.S. Department of Health, Education and Welfare's Food and Drug Administration (FDA).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vadim-andreev.narod.ru/ufo/art14.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vadim-andreev.narod.ru">[Vadim Andreev&#x27;s Pages]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vadim-andreev.narod.ru</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vadim Andreev&#x27;s PagesБлины Джо Симонтона</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vadim Andreev&#x27;s PagesБлины Джо Симонтона</span></span></span>

The examination included several straightforward tests appropriate for an unfamiliar food sample:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Microscopic analysis** to identify plant material and food components.
* **Bacteriological examination** to determine whether anything biologically unusual was present.
* **Radioactivity measurements** to check for abnormal radiation that might suggest an unconventional origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vadim-andreev.narod.ru/ufo/art14.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vadim-andreev.narod.ru">[Vadim Andreev&#x27;s Pages]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vadim-andreev.narod.ru</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vadim Andreev&#x27;s PagesБлины Джо Симонтона</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vadim Andreev&#x27;s PagesБлины Джо Симонтона</span></span></span>

</div>

The laboratory's purpose was not to determine whether UFOs existed, but simply to identify the physical composition of the submitted material.

## What ingredients and physical properties were reported

The laboratory's findings were remarkably mundane.

Microscopic examination identified:

* fat
* starch
* buckwheat hulls
* wheat bran
* soybean hulls

Based on those ingredients, the analysts concluded that the material appeared to be **an ordinary pancake made predominantly from buckwheat**. The bacteriological examination found nothing exceptional, while radiation measurements were entirely consistent with an object of normal terrestrial origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vadim-andreev.narod.ru/ufo/art14.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vadim-andreev.narod.ru">[Vadim Andreev&#x27;s Pages]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vadim-andreev.narod.ru</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vadim Andreev&#x27;s PagesБлины Джо Симонтона</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vadim Andreev&#x27;s PagesБлины Джо Симонтона</span></span></span>

Later summaries of the case sometimes describe the cakes more generally as containing ordinary baking ingredients such as flour, fat, salt and milk-derived components. Although wording differs slightly between secondary accounts, the consistent conclusion is that investigators found nothing chemically or physically inconsistent with a conventional food product.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oddwoo.com/eagle-river-enigma.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oddwoo.com">[OddWoo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oddwoo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Odd Woo The Eagle River Enigma: Joe Simonton’s Alien Pancakes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Eagle River Enigma: Joe Simonton’s Alien Pancakes - OddWoo...</span></span></span>

## Why ordinary results weakened the extraordinary claim

The Eagle River case remains unusual because physical evidence existed at all. Yet physical evidence is only persuasive if it differs in some meaningful way from ordinary objects.

In Simonton's case, the laboratory findings created a sharp contrast between the witness's extraordinary narrative and the ordinary nature of the recovered material. Had the cakes contained previously unknown compounds, impossible manufacturing characteristics or unexplained isotopic signatures, the case would have become considerably stronger. Instead, every reported laboratory result pointed towards familiar food ingredients.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vadim-andreev.narod.ru/ufo/art14.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vadim-andreev.narod.ru">[Vadim Andreev&#x27;s Pages]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vadim-andreev.narod.ru</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vadim Andreev&#x27;s PagesБлины Джо Симонтона</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vadim Andreev&#x27;s PagesБлины Джо Симонтона</span></span></span>

This does not prove that Simonton deliberately fabricated his story. Laboratory analysis cannot determine where a food item came from or who prepared it. It simply showed that the cakes themselves carried no detectable physical signature distinguishing them from ordinary earthly pancakes.

For investigators, that distinction mattered. Simonton's credibility and consistency remained separate questions from the evidential value of the samples. A sincere witness could still be mistaken, and ordinary food could not independently verify an extraordinary encounter.

## What the tests mean for the Wisconsin case today

Within [Wisconsin]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-wisconsins-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s UFO history, the Eagle River incident is often remembered as the "space pancake" case precisely because it produced something tangible to examine. The laboratory work remains one of the clearest examples of physical evidence being subjected to scientific testing in an early American UFO investigation.

Ironically, the tests are memorable not because they uncovered a mystery, but because they largely removed one. The samples did not reveal alien technology, unknown chemistry or inexplicable materials. Instead, they reduced the evidential weight of the pancakes themselves, leaving the case to rest almost entirely on Joe Simonton's uncorroborated testimony.

That balance explains why the Eagle River encounter continues to occupy an unusual place in Wisconsin's UFO history. It is neither a straightforward hoax exposed by laboratory work nor a case strengthened by extraordinary physical evidence. Rather, it illustrates how a genuinely unusual claim can survive in public memory even when the only testable artefacts appear entirely ordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vadim-andreev.narod.ru/ufo/art14.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vadim-andreev.narod.ru">[narod.ru]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vadim-andreev.narod.ru</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vadim Andreev&#x27;s PagesБлины Джо Симонтона</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vadim Andreev&#x27;s PagesБлины Джо Симонтона</span></span></span>
