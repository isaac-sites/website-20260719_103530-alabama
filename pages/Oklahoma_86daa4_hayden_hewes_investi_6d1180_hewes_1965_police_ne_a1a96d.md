## Introduction

During Oklahoma’s intense [1965 wave]({{ '1965-wave/' | relative_url }}) of UFO reports, [Hayden Hewes]({{ 'hayden-hewes/' | relative_url }}) gained an advantage that most civilian investigators lacked: a fast-moving information network linking police officers, highway patrol personnel, journalists and local witnesses. Rather than waiting for newspaper stories to appear days later, Hewes often learned of sightings while events were still unfolding. That network helped make him one of the most visible UFO investigators in Oklahoma during the mid-1960s, but it also exposed a recurring problem. ## How Police and Journalists Alerted Hewes

Hewes had spent years building contacts through his Oklahoma City-based UFO organisation. By 1965, those relationships extended into local law-enforcement agencies, newsrooms and broadcasting stations. When unusual aerial reports arrived through official channels, officers or reporters sometimes contacted Hewes directly, allowing him to begin interviews before stories faded or witnesses became difficult to locate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmondlifeandleisure.com/remembering-a-man-who-investigated-the-unusual-p19337-76.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmondlifeandleisure.com">[edmondlifeandleisure.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmondlifeandleisure.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">remembering a man who investigated the unusual p19337 76</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">29, 1943, in Cape Girardeau, Mo. ## The Patrol Tower Observations and Claimed Triangular Fix

One of the most ambitious aspects of Hewes's 1965 work involved attempts to use multiple official observers to estimate the position of reported objects.

During the 1965 flap, reports came not only from ordinary citizens but also from police officers, highway patrol personnel and observers stationed at elevated locations. Hewes argued that observations from separate points could sometimes be compared to determine where an object might have been located in the sky. Accounts associated with his investigations describe efforts to correlate sightings from different observation points and patrol facilities, producing what was often described as a triangular or triangulated fix on an object's position.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmondlifeandleisure.com/edmond-underground-in-edmond-abuzz-with-uco-sightings-p10350-87.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmondlifeandleisure.com">[edmondlifeandleisure.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmondlifeandleisure.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">in &#x27;65 Edmond abuzz with UCO sightings11 Sept 2014 — That Saturday, Hewes traveled to Wynnewood to interview Officer Sikes...</span></span></span>

The basic idea was straightforward. If observers at multiple locations recorded the direction of an object at approximately the same time, the sight lines could theoretically be plotted on a map. Where those lines crossed would provide an estimated location. In principle, this method is stronger than relying on a single witness because it attempts to use geometry rather than impression alone.

However, the available records show major practical limitations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Exact times were not always synchronised.
* Witnesses often estimated directions rather than measuring them.
* Elevation angles were rarely recorded precisely.
* Distances remained uncertain.
* Most observations were made under night-time conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmondlifeandleisure.com/edmond-underground-in-edmond-abuzz-with-uco-sightings-p10350-87.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmondlifeandleisure.com">[edmondlifeandleisure.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmondlifeandleisure.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">in &#x27;65 Edmond abuzz with UCO sightings11 Sept 2014 — That Saturday, Hewes traveled to Wynnewood to interview Officer Sikes...</span></span></span>

</div>

As a result, claims of triangulated positions were generally suggestive rather than conclusive. They demonstrated an effort to treat sightings systematically, but they did not produce the kind of instrument-based tracking associated with radar or modern scientific observation networks.

## Why Rapid Reporting Still Left Major Evidence Gaps

The greatest strength of Hewes's network was also its greatest weakness. Reports arrived quickly, but most consisted of eyewitness testimony rather than hard evidence.

The Wynnewood sightings illustrate the problem. Contemporary accounts describe Hewes travelling to interview Officer Sikes after reports reached his organisation. Other witnesses reported unusual objects elsewhere in the state during the same period, creating the impression of a widespread wave. Yet even when multiple reports arrived on the same day, investigators were still largely dependent on personal recollections and descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmondlifeandleisure.com/edmond-underground-in-edmond-abuzz-with-uco-sightings-p10350-87.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmondlifeandleisure.com">[edmondlifeandleisure.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmondlifeandleisure.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">in &#x27;65 Edmond abuzz with UCO sightings11 Sept 2014 — That Saturday, Hewes traveled to Wynnewood to interview Officer Sikes...</span></span></span>

### Few Instrument Records

Many reports originated from police officers, which gave them a degree of credibility, but credibility is not the same as verification. Most cases lacked radar data, calibrated photographs or independent technical measurements. Witnesses could describe what they saw, but investigators usually could not prove distance, speed or size.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmondlifeandleisure.com/edmond-underground-in-edmond-abuzz-with-uco-sightings-p10350-87.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmondlifeandleisure.com">[edmondlifeandleisure.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmondlifeandleisure.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">in &#x27;65 Edmond abuzz with UCO sightings11 Sept 2014 — That Saturday, Hewes traveled to Wynnewood to interview Officer Sikes...</span></span></span>
