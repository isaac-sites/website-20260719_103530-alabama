## Introduction

The Vought V-173 “Flying Pancake” occupies a unique place in [Connecticut]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-connecticuts-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history because it was not a rumour, a hoax or a mysterious light. It was a real aircraft, designed and flown in Stratford during the Second World War. Its flattened, disc-like shape looked remarkably similar to what later generations would call a flying saucer, and its unusual flight characteristics made it appear even stranger to observers unfamiliar with the project.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_V-173" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vought V-173</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vought V-173</span></span></span>

For that reason, the V-173 sits at the intersection of aviation history and UFO folklore. Some later writers suggested that sightings of the aircraft contributed to early reports of mysterious aerial objects in Connecticut. The historical record supports the idea that the aircraft surprised observers and generated curiosity. What it does not support is the stronger claim that the Flying Pancake was secretly responsible for large numbers of unexplained UFO reports or that it somehow proves a hidden origin for the flying-saucer phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_V-173" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vought V-173</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vought V-173</span></span></span>

## How the V-173 was designed and tested in Stratford

The aircraft emerged from the ideas of aeronautical engineer Charles H. Zimmerman, who believed that an extremely low-aspect-ratio wing could provide exceptional lift while allowing very short take-offs and landings. Working with Vought-Sikorsky in Stratford, Connecticut, he developed an experimental proof-of-concept aircraft known as the V-173.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_V-173" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vought V-173</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vought V-173</span></span></span>

Unlike conventional aircraft, the V-173's body was itself the wing. Viewed from above, it appeared almost circular, leading to the nickname “Flying Pancake”. Two large propellers mounted near the wingtips pushed air across the aircraft’s entire lifting surface, helping maintain control even at unusually low speeds. The design was intended to explore radical ideas about manoeuvrability and short-field performance rather than to resemble a flying saucer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/vought-v-173-flying-pancake/nasm_A19610120000" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airandspace.si.edu">[National Air and Space Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airandspace.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Air and Space MuseumVought V-173 &quot;Flying Pancake&quot;A proof-of-concept vehicle, this design, the V-173, it had a circular wing 23.3...</span></span></span>

The prototype first flew on 23 November 1942 with Vought chief test pilot Boone Guyton at the controls. Flight testing continued through the war years and eventually accumulated roughly 190 flights. Charles Lindbergh also flew the aircraft and reportedly found it easier to handle than its unconventional appearance suggested. Test results demonstrated impressive low-speed capabilities and an unusual resistance to stalling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_V-173" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vought V-173</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vought V-173</span></span></span>

Connecticut was therefore home to one of the most visually distinctive aircraft ever flown. Long before popular culture became obsessed with flying saucers, residents near Stratford could occasionally see a genuine disc-shaped experimental machine in the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ctinsider.com/connecticutmagazine/news-people/article/CT-Files-Connecticut-s-Real-life-Flying-Saucer-17042826.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ctinsider.com">[CT Insider]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ctinsider.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CT Files Connecticut s Real life Flying Saucer 17042826</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CT InsiderCT Files: Connecticut&#x27;s Real-life Flying Saucer5 Jul 2017 — But this flying saucer-like plane was real and wasn&#x27;t the work inst...</span></span></span>

## Why its shape and flight behaviour invited misidentification

The V-173 looked strange even on the ground. In flight, it could appear stranger still.

Most aircraft of the early 1940s had recognisable fuselages, wings and tails. The Flying Pancake compressed these familiar features into a rounded shape that could look unlike any ordinary aeroplane, particularly when viewed at a distance or from below. Its silhouette resembled a flattened disc rather than a conventional aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://airandspace.si.edu/collection-objects/vought-v-173-flying-pancake/nasm_A19610120000" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airandspace.si.edu">[National Air and Space Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airandspace.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Air and Space MuseumVought V-173 &quot;Flying Pancake&quot;A proof-of-concept vehicle, this design, the V-173, it had a circular wing 23.3...</span></span></span>

Its flight behaviour also encouraged confusion. The aircraft was designed to fly slowly while remaining controllable. Observers accustomed to seeing faster military aircraft could watch the V-173 moving at unexpectedly low speeds and maintaining attitudes that seemed unusual for an aeroplane. Reports from test pilots emphasised how difficult it was to stall and how effectively it could operate at low airspeeds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_V-173" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vought V-173</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vought V-173</span></span></span>

These characteristics matter because many UFO reports begin with an observer seeing something that does not match expectations. A disc-shaped aircraft moving slowly over coastal Connecticut in wartime conditions could easily be interpreted as something unfamiliar, especially by people who had no knowledge of classified or experimental aviation projects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_V-173" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vought V-173</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vought V-173</span></span></span>

The aircraft's testing programme was not entirely hidden, but it was not widely understood by the general public. Experimental military aviation often generated rumours, and Connecticut's concentration of defence and aerospace activity created conditions in which unusual aircraft could become local legends.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ctinsider.com/connecticutmagazine/news-people/article/CT-Files-Connecticut-s-Real-life-Flying-Saucer-17042826.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ctinsider.com">[CT Insider]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ctinsider.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CT Files Connecticut s Real life Flying Saucer 17042826</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CT InsiderCT Files: Connecticut&#x27;s Real-life Flying Saucer5 Jul 2017 — But this flying saucer-like plane was real and wasn&#x27;t the work inst...</span></span></span>

## Did the Flying Pancake inspire saucer reports?

There is a kernel of truth behind the claim.

Several aviation and museum sources note that the V-173's test flights reportedly produced UFO or “mystery aircraft” reports from surprised observers in Connecticut during the early 1940s. The aircraft's appearance was sufficiently unusual that witnesses could have described it in terms that later sounded similar to flying-saucer accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_V-173" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vought V-173</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vought V-173</span></span></span>

However, chronology is important. The great American flying-saucer wave began in 1947 after pilot Kenneth Arnold's famous sighting in Washington State. By that point, the V-173 had already been flying for years, and public fascination with disc-shaped craft was being shaped by national media coverage rather than solely by Connecticut aviation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vought_V-173" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vought V-173</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vought V-173</span></span></span>

As a result, historians generally view the Flying Pancake as one influence among many rather than the source of the flying-saucer phenomenon. It demonstrated that a real aircraft could resemble later UFO imagery, but there is no evidence that it directly created the national saucer craze or explains most UFO reports from the period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ctinsider.com/connecticutmagazine/news-people/article/CT-Files-Connecticut-s-Real-life-Flying-Saucer-17042826.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ctinsider.com">[CT Insider]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ctinsider.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CT Files Connecticut s Real life Flying Saucer 17042826</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CT InsiderCT Files: Connecticut&#x27;s Real-life Flying Saucer5 Jul 2017 — But this flying saucer-like plane was real and wasn&#x27;t the work inst...</span></span></span>

The stronger claims sometimes found in UFO literature—that secret Flying Pancake derivatives account for widespread saucer sightings—are not supported by the available documentary record. ## What the historical record does and does not prove

The historical evidence clearly establishes several facts.

First, Connecticut really was home to an aircraft that looked remarkably like later popular images of a flying saucer.
