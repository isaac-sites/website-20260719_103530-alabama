## Introduction

The Salem Coast Guard photograph is one of the most famous UFO images ever associated with [Massachusetts]({{ 'which-massachusetts-ufo-stories-still/' | relative_url }}) and one of the few photographs to enter the files of Project [Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book-ba6b5a/' | relative_url }}) as official evidence. Taken on 16 July 1952 at the Coast Guard Air Station on Winter Island in Salem, the image appears to show four bright oval objects flying in formation. For UFO researchers, it became a classic photograph from the height of the 1952 American flying-saucer wave. For sceptics and photographic analysts, it became a textbook example of how reflections, viewing angles and limited evidence can make a striking image difficult to interpret.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007680837/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library of CongressPhoto by Shell R. Alpert, a United States Coast Guard photographer at the Salem, Mass., air station at 9:35 A.M. on Ju...</span></span></span>

What makes the Salem photograph important is not that it proved anything extraordinary. Rather, it illustrates a recurring problem in Project Blue Book's Massachusetts record: a photograph can be genuine and still fail to demonstrate what it appears to show. More than seventy years later, the image remains famous precisely because investigators could neither conclusively authenticate an unusual aerial object nor definitively explain the photograph beyond doubt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[ufologie.patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 195218 Aug 2003 — The first analysis by US Air Force&#x27;s Project Blue Book concluded it was &quot;p...</span></span></span>

## How the Photograph Was Taken

According to Coast Guard records, photographer Shell R. Alpert was working at the Salem air station on the morning of 16 July 1952 when he noticed four bright objects outside the window of his photographic laboratory. He later reported that the lights appeared to fade and brighten. After briefly calling another serviceman, Thomas Flaherty, to observe them, Alpert used a camera that was already nearby and took a single photograph through the laboratory window.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007680837/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[loc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library of CongressPhoto by Shell R. The resulting image shows four luminous shapes arranged in a loose formation. Unlike many alleged UFO photographs of the era, the Salem picture was not an anonymous submission. It came from a Coast Guard installation, was preserved as an official Coast Guard photograph, and was later incorporated into Project Blue Book files. That official provenance helped give the photograph a credibility that many civilian UFO images lacked.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007680837/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[loc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library of CongressPhoto by Shell R. July 1952 was one of the most active months in American UFO history, with reports appearing across the country and attracting intense media attention. ## Why the Photograph Attracted Attention

The photograph possessed several features that investigators considered noteworthy.

First, there were two reported witnesses rather than one. Alpert stated that Flaherty also saw the objects. Second, the image originated from a trained military photographer rather than a casual observer. Third, the photograph was taken in daylight, when many claimed UFO images were still little more than distant lights or night-time streaks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://photos.usni.org/content/1083864jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: photos.usni.org">[photos.usni.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">photos.usni.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval Institute Photo ArchivesFour UFO&#x27;s sighted above the Salem, Masachusetts Coast Guard Air Station on July 16, 1952. These factors gave the Salem photograph an evidential status above many routine reports entering Blue Book files. The image circulated widely in newspapers and later became one of the best-known photographs associated with the Air Force UFO investigation programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/disasters-and-phenomena/u2s-ufos-and-operation-blue-book.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.navy.mil">[Naval History and Heritage Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.navy.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">u2s ufos and operation blue book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Naval History and Heritage CommandU-2s, UFOs, and Operation Blue Book24 Jan 2024 — Alpert took a photograph through a window screen that...</span></span></span>

## What Investigators Could and Could Not Verify

Project Blue Book's central difficulty was that the photograph contained almost no reliable information about distance, size or motion. The bright shapes could be interpreted in radically different ways depending on assumptions about where they were located relative to the camera.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[ufologie.patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 195218 Aug 2003 — The first analysis by US Air Force&#x27;s Project Blue Book concluded it was &quot;p...</span></span></span>

Investigators knew:

* The photograph was taken by Shell Alpert at the Salem Coast Guard Air Station.
* It was shot through a window.
* Another serviceman reportedly observed the lights.
* The image was not an obvious fabrication created by crude darkroom manipulation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007680837/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[loc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library of CongressPhoto by Shell R. These limitations were crucial. Without a known distance, even basic measurements become impossible. A small reflection a few inches from the camera can look remarkably similar on film to a large object thousands of feet away.

## Why the Window Became the Key Issue

The most important detail in later analysis was that the photograph had been taken through a window in the photographic laboratory. This immediately introduced the possibility that the bright shapes were reflections rather than external objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007680837/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library of CongressPhoto by Shell R. Alpert, a United States Coast Guard photographer at the Salem, Mass., air station at 9:35 A.M. on Ju...</span></span></span>

Project Blue Book reportedly considered several explanations during different stages of the investigation. One early assessment suggested the image might have resulted from a photographic trick or double exposure. Later reviews shifted attention toward reflections on the glass, including the possibility that nearby lights had been reflected into the window and photographed in a way that created the apparent aerial formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 195218 Aug 2003 — The first analysis by US Air Force&#x27;s Project Blue Book concluded it was &quot;p...</span></span></span>

The appearance of the objects encouraged this interpretation. The four shapes display similar brightness and geometry, characteristics consistent with multiple reflections originating from a common light source. ## Reflections, Exposure and Later Sceptical Explanations

As photographic analysis advanced, sceptical commentators increasingly focused on optical rather than aerial explanations.

One proposed explanation involved reflections of lights behind the photographer. If a bright indoor or nearby outdoor light source struck the window at the correct angle, the camera could record reflected images appearing to float outside. Similar effects are familiar to modern photographers who accidentally capture reflections in glass.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[ufologie.patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 195218 Aug 2003 — The first analysis by US Air Force&#x27;s Project Blue Book concluded it was &quot;p...</span></span></span>

## Why the Case Remained Unresolved

The Salem photograph occupies an unusual position between strong evidence and weak evidence.

It is stronger than many UFO photographs because the photographer was identified, the image entered official records, and the circumstances were documented. Yet it is weaker than often assumed because the photograph itself provides little objective information about the supposed objects. The image is striking, but it lacks the supporting measurements, multiple viewpoints or corroborating instrumentation that would allow investigators to determine what was actually photographed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007680837/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[loc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Library of CongressPhoto by Shell R. ## What the Salem Photograph Means in Massachusetts UFO History

Within Massachusetts UFO history, the Salem Coast Guard photograph remains significant less because of what it proves than because of what it demonstrates about evidence.

The image shows how a credible witness and an authentic photograph do not automatically produce a clear answer. It also highlights a recurring lesson found throughout Project Blue Book's Massachusetts files: photographs often create an impression of certainty that exceeds what investigators can actually verify. A dramatic image may survive for decades, while the missing contextual details needed to interpret it correctly disappear almost immediately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/htm/bluebookpics1501.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[ufologie.patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book case 1501, Salem, July 16, 195218 Aug 2003 — The first analysis by US Air Force&#x27;s Project Blue Book concluded it was &quot;p...</span></span></span>

For that reason, the Salem photograph continues to appear in discussions of Massachusetts UFO cases. It stands as one of the state's most recognisable official UFO images, yet also as a reminder that the strongest-looking evidence is not always the most conclusive.
