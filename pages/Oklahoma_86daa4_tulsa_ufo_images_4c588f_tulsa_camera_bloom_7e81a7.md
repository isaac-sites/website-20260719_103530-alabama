## Introduction

One of the most important sceptical questions about the famous 1965 Tulsa UFO photograph is whether the object's distinctive oval shape was ever a real shape at all. The image shows a bright, multicoloured form floating against a dark background, but it does not show a clearly defined craft with visible edges, windows, or surface details. That matters because photographic processes can transform a small bright light into a much larger, softer, and more complex-looking object on film. In the Tulsa case, the possibility of camera bloom, overexposure, and related film effects is one of the strongest conventional explanations for the image's appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thinkaboutitdocs.com/1965-tulsa-oklahoma-ufo-photograph/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1965 tulsa oklahoma ufo photograph</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1965 Tulsa Oklahoma UFO Photo: Multi-Colored Disc...April 8, 2013 — 8 Apr 2013 — They also observed that it resembled...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2013</span></span></span>

## How Bright Lights Bloom on Photographic Film

Photographers use the term "bloom" to describe the spreading of bright light beyond its true boundaries in an image. This can occur through a combination of lens effects, internal reflections, scattering within photographic emulsions, and overexposure. Instead of recording a precise point of light, the film records a larger glowing area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.dehancer.com/articles/bloom-what-it-is-and-how-it-works/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.dehancer.com">[dehancer.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.dehancer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blog Bloom: what it is and how it works</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dehancer BlogBloom: what it is and how it worksMay 13, 2020 — The Bloom term is commonly used to define the combined effect of bright lig...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 13, 2020</span></span></span>

The effect becomes especially noticeable when:

* A bright light is photographed against a very dark background.
* The camera lacks precise exposure control.
* The light source occupies only a small part of the frame.
* The image is later enlarged or cropped.
* The original negative contains limited detail in the brightest areas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.dehancer.com/articles/bloom-what-it-is-and-how-it-works/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.dehancer.com">[Dehancer Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.dehancer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blog Bloom: what it is and how it works</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dehancer BlogBloom: what it is and how it worksMay 13, 2020 — The Bloom term is commonly used to define the combined effect of bright lig...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 13, 2020</span></span></span>

These conditions closely resemble those associated with the Tulsa image. The published photograph contains little visible context beyond the luminous object itself. There are no identifiable landmarks, no horizon, and no nearby reference points that would allow viewers to judge scale. The brightest portion of the image appears heavily exposed, making it difficult to determine where any underlying structure ends and where photographic spreading begins.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thinkaboutitdocs.com/1965-tulsa-oklahoma-ufo-photograph/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1965 tulsa oklahoma ufo photograph</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1965 Tulsa Oklahoma UFO Photo: Multi-Colored Disc...April 8, 2013 — 8 Apr 2013 — They also observed that it resembled...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2013</span></span></span>

Film photography introduces another complication: halation. Halation occurs when intense light passes through photographic layers and scatters back into the emulsion, creating a glow or halo around bright regions. In colour film this often produces reddish or orange fringes around highlights, while other optical effects can introduce additional colour spreading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.analog.cafe/r/halation-in-film-photography-i3q4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: analog.cafe">[analog.cafe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">analog.cafe</span><span class="citation-popover-title">halation in film photography i3q4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Halation in Film Photography, Explained23 Jul 2025 — Halation in photography is an effect that manifests as white, red, or orange halos a...</span></span></span>

The result can be an image that looks larger, softer and more structured than the original source.

## Why Colour Bands Do Not Prove a Solid Structure

Supporters of the Tulsa photograph have often pointed to its striking colours. The image appears to contain zones of white, blue-green and reddish light, giving the impression of layers or bands within a disc-shaped object. Yet colour variation by itself is not evidence of a physical craft.

Bright lights photographed on colour film frequently produce uneven colour patterns. Different wavelengths can spread through lenses and film layers differently, while overexposed highlights often lose fine detail and merge into larger coloured regions. The final image may therefore show concentric zones or patches of colour that reflect the recording process rather than the object's true appearance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.analog.cafe/r/halation-in-film-photography-i3q4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: analog.cafe">[analog.cafe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">analog.cafe</span><span class="citation-popover-title">halation in film photography i3q4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Halation in Film Photography, Explained23 Jul 2025 — Halation in photography is an effect that manifests as white, red, or orange halos a...</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. If a photograph shows a sharply resolved metallic structure with visible components, colour variations might support the interpretation of a physical object. The Tulsa image does not provide that level of detail. Instead, it presents a luminous form whose apparent shape is largely defined by gradients of light rather than identifiable structural features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thinkaboutitdocs.com/1965-tulsa-oklahoma-ufo-photograph/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1965 tulsa oklahoma ufo photograph</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1965 Tulsa Oklahoma UFO Photo: Multi-Colored Disc...April 8, 2013 — 8 Apr 2013 — They also observed that it resembled...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2013</span></span></span>

Because the brightest areas are saturated, viewers cannot determine whether the colours represent:

* Separate parts of a solid object.
* Different intensities within a single light source.
* Optical effects created by the lens.
* Film bloom and halation.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.analog.cafe/r/halation-in-film-photography-i3q4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: analog.cafe">[analog.cafe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">analog.cafe</span><span class="citation-popover-title">halation in film photography i3q4</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Halation in Film Photography, Explained23 Jul 2025 — Halation in photography is an effect that manifests as white, red, or orange halos a...</span></span></span>
* Some combination of all four.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blog.dehancer.com/articles/bloom-what-it-is-and-how-it-works/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blog.dehancer.com">[dehancer.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blog.dehancer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blog Bloom: what it is and how it works</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Dehancer BlogBloom: what it is and how it worksMay 13, 2020 — The Bloom term is commonly used to define the combined effect of bright lig...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 13, 2020</span></span></span>

For that reason, the colour bands seen in reproductions of the photograph cannot be treated as proof that the camera captured a structured craft.

## How Much Weight Should Camera Effects Carry?
