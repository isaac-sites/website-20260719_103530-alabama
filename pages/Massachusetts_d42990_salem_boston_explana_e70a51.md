## Introduction

Two of [Massachusetts]({{ 'which-massachusetts-ufo-stories-still/' | relative_url }})’s best-known UFO episodes are valuable largely because they show how convincing mysteries can grow from ordinary visual mechanisms. The 1952 Salem photograph appears to show four brilliant objects above a Coast Guard air station, yet the strongest explanation is that lights inside the building were reflected in the window through which the picture was taken. In Boston in 1964, numerous witnesses reported a glowing elliptical object moving along the Charles River, but investigators matched most sightings to an aircraft towing an illuminated political advertisement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007680837/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[loc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Library of Congress</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">First daylight photo of - what are they?...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Massachusetts_d42990_salem_boston_explana_e70a51-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Photos and Lights" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Neither case is entirely tidy. The Salem image has an uncertain investigative history, while two Boston witnesses insisted that they saw the advertising aircraft and a separate object simultaneously. Those loose ends explain why both stories survived. They do not, however, outweigh the evidence that reflections, aircraft lights and imperfect timing account for most of what was reported.

## The Salem Coast Guard photograph

On 16 July 1952, Shell R. Alpert, a photographer serving at the US Coast Guard air station in Salem, took a photograph showing four bright, roughly oval patches arranged above the local skyline. The Library of Congress catalogue dates the exposure to 9.35 am, identifies it as official Coast Guard photograph 5554 and records that it was taken through a window screen. That last detail is central to understanding the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007680837/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">First daylight photo of - what are they?...</span></span></span>

The picture became memorable because it combined several qualities rarely found together in early UFO reports. It was taken in daylight, came from a military installation and carried an official government photograph number. The objects also formed an apparently purposeful pattern, encouraging viewers to interpret separate patches of light as craft flying in formation. Appearing during the extraordinary American sighting wave of 1952 gave the image additional cultural force: strange lights were already national news, and Project [Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book-ba6b5a/' | relative_url }}) had recently become the Air Force programme responsible for collecting and assessing such reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://prologue.blogs.archives.gov/2019/12/19/saucers-over-washington-the-history-of-project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prologue.blogs.archives.gov">[Pieces of History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prologue.blogs.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

Yet the photograph contains very little information about distance, size or motion. A photograph records light reaching the film; it does not establish that every luminous shape lay outside the building or high in the sky. Because Alpert photographed through glass, bright interior lamps could have produced virtual images that appeared superimposed upon the outdoor scene. Four similar lights reflected from a flat window would naturally retain a geometric arrangement, creating the impression of an organised formation.

This interpretation is strengthened by the objects’ appearance. They are overexposed blobs without visible structure, shadows or clear edges. No detail identifies wings, fuselages or solid surfaces. Their brightness is also conspicuous against a daytime sky. Reflections from nearby lamps can appear unusually intense and soft-edged in a photograph because they are out of focus and recorded against a more distant background.

Later summaries of the Blue Book material describe several changing assessments: possible double exposure or fabrication, followed by the possibility that the lights were reflections in the window. The Boston Globe’s examination of the files similarly described the objects as possible reflections of lamps. The changing labels are often presented as evidence that investigators could not explain the picture, but they more accurately reveal the difficulty of reconstructing one exposure after the room, camera position and lighting arrangement were no longer available for controlled testing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/news/nation/2015/02/06/air-force-voluminous-ufo-files-illuminate-bygone-era-new-england/5ZDe8dsNhQunIt13jLwedI/story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bostonglobe.com">[BostonGlobe.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bostonglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Boston Globe.com UF O ﬁles shed light on a bygone era in New England</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ﬁles shed light on a bygone era in New England - The Boston Globe...</span></span></span>

### What the photograph does establish

The surviving official record securely establishes who took the picture, where and when it was taken, and that the camera faced outdoors through a screened window. It also shows that the image itself was preserved rather than suppressed; it eventually entered the Library of Congress collection with no known publication restriction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.loc.gov/pictures/item/2007680837/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: loc.gov">[The Library of Congress]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">First daylight photo of - what are they?...</span></span></span>

What it does not establish is equally important. The photograph alone cannot demonstrate that the lights were airborne, distant or even outside. There is no sequence showing movement, no second camera angle, no usable scale and no physical trace. Accounts sometimes add that another Coast Guardsman witnessed the lights, but the accessible archival description centres on the single photograph and does not provide a comparably detailed independent observational record.

The fairest assessment is therefore not that the picture was conclusively debunked in every detail, but that it is weak evidence for an extraordinary aircraft. A reflection requires only glass, bright lamps and a favourable viewing angle—all known or highly plausible features of a photographic workspace. An unknown craft interpretation requires the image to contain depth and location information that it simply does not preserve.

## The Harvard Bridge advertising aircraft

Boston’s better-documented visual puzzle occurred on the evening of 9 September 1964. Beginning at about 8.30 pm, witnesses around Boston, Cambridge and the Charles River basin reported an illuminated elliptical or circular object. Eleven reports were examined closely, with descriptions including a dull-grey body, yellowish or white light, hovering, sudden acceleration and sharp changes of direction. Calls also reached WBZ, whose staff members went outside after reports of a strange light near the station’s Soldiers Field Road premises.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universalhub.com/2024/year-martians-took-interest-charles-river" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universalhub.com">[Universal Hub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universalhub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on universalhub.com.</span></span></span>

Investigators quickly identified an unusually good candidate: a Ski-Lite Advertising aircraft operating from Beverly Airport. It was carrying an illuminated sign promoting Republican governor John Volpe’s re-election campaign. According to the Air Force investigation, the company’s night advertising displays had already generated false UFO reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universalhub.com/2024/year-martians-took-interest-charles-river" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universalhub.com">[Universal Hub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universalhub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on universalhub.com.</span></span></span>

This was not a vague suggestion based solely on the presence of an aeroplane somewhere over the city. Investigators reconstructed its route, compared it with witness locations and plotted the aircraft’s track against the reported progress of the supposed UFO. They concluded that nine of the eleven witnesses had probably observed the advertising flight. The Air Force’s method—comparing times, bearings and routes—was considerably stronger than simply choosing a familiar object that resembled the description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universalhub.com/2024/year-martians-took-interest-charles-river" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universalhub.com">[Universal Hub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universalhub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on universalhub.com.</span></span></span>

An illuminated banner viewed at night can look very different from an aeroplane. The aircraft itself may be hard to see, while the brightest portion appears as a floating oval or rectangle. Flashing or changing text can make the light blink, fade or alter colour. At sufficient distance, the gap between aircraft and sign may disappear, leaving a single luminous form. Engine noise may be weak or masked by traffic, wind and city sounds.

Apparent movement can also be misleading. When a bright object is seen against a dark or cloudy sky with few fixed reference points, observers may overestimate speed or interpret a gradual bank as an abrupt turn. Different witnesses watching from moving cars or separate parts of the city can then describe what was physically one flight as hovering, darting or changing course sharply.

### The two reports that did not fit

The official explanation encountered a genuine problem: two witnesses said that the illuminated advertising aircraft and the unidentified object were visible at the same time. The most detailed account came from James Louis Warsher, described in the files as an MIT engineering student, and a companion travelling across the Harvard Bridge—not, despite wording in one report, the Anderson Memorial Bridge near Harvard Square.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universalhub.com/2024/year-martians-took-interest-charles-river" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universalhub.com">[Universal Hub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universalhub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on universalhub.com.</span></span></span>

Warsher reported seeing the sign displaying a message supporting Volpe while a separate elliptical object approached along the Charles. He described the [second light]({{ 'second-light/' | relative_url }}) as crossing the bridge, heading towards the weather beacon on the old John Hancock Building, making several apparently square turns, returning towards the river and climbing rapidly. An Air Force consultant regarded him as a reliable and technically capable observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universalhub.com/2024/year-martians-took-interest-charles-river" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universalhub.com">[Universal Hub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universalhub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on universalhub.com.</span></span></span>

That testimony prevents the advertising aircraft from being a complete explanation for every element of the night. Investigators considered whether spotlights reflecting from clouds might account for the second object, but lacked sufficiently precise local weather information to test the suggestion. The Boston Globe later highlighted the same contradiction: the aircraft explanation worked well for the wider cluster but did not neatly resolve the claim that both lights were observed together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universalhub.com/2024/year-martians-took-interest-charles-river" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universalhub.com">[Universal Hub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universalhub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on universalhub.com.</span></span></span>

Nevertheless, simultaneous-sighting testimony is not automatically decisive. Timings in witness reports are often estimates reconstructed after an event. The advertising aircraft could have remained visible over a wide area, allowing different phases of the same flight or its illuminated banner to be perceived as separate objects. Reflections on vehicle glass, the Hancock beacon, ground-based lights on clouds or another conventional aircraft could also have introduced an additional light. None of these possibilities can now be demonstrated with certainty, but they require fewer unsupported assumptions than a craft capable of silent hovering, instantaneous square turns and vertical acceleration.

## Why plausible explanations leave loose ends

These cases show why “explained” and “unexplained” are not always clean opposites. Investigators seldom recreate the exact conditions of a brief sighting. They work with photographs, memories, estimated times, incomplete weather records and objects that have long since moved. An ordinary explanation may account for most of the evidence while leaving one statement or visual feature unresolved.

In Salem, the window-reflection mechanism fits the photographic geometry but cannot now be reproduced in the original room with the original lamps and camera position. In Boston, the aircraft’s documented route fits most witnesses, while a small residue of testimony remains harder to reconcile. Those gaps are real, but a gap in reconstruction is not positive evidence for an extraordinary vehicle.

The cases also illustrate three recurring mechanisms in Massachusetts UFO history:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Glass removes depth clues.** A reflected indoor light can appear to occupy the outdoor landscape, especially when the viewer or camera is focused beyond the window.
* **Illuminated aircraft conceal their own identity.** A bright banner or sign may be visible while the towing aeroplane becomes almost invisible.
* **Human timing is approximate.** A difference of several minutes can determine whether two reports describe separate objects or different stages of one flight.

</div>

Official investigations were not infallible. Project Blue Book sometimes worked with limited staff, uneven records and conclusions that changed as new information arrived. Its files should therefore be read as historical investigations rather than unquestionable verdicts. The National Archives itself describes the programme as an effort to evaluate possible security threats and notes that most reports ultimately involved known objects such as aircraft or balloons, while some remained unidentified because the available evidence was insufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects - National ArchivesAugust 15, 2016 — Project BLUE BOOK has been declassi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 15, 2016</span></span></span>

## What these cases mean for Massachusetts

The Salem photograph and Boston light reports matter because they demonstrate how the state’s UFO history was shaped by modern technology as much as by anything in the sky. Photography created apparently objective evidence but introduced reflections and exposure effects. Aerial advertising placed unfamiliar illuminated forms over densely populated neighbourhoods. Television and telephone reporting quickly transformed individual impressions into a city-wide event.

Both incidents began with sincere observations. Nothing in the surviving evidence requires witnesses to have invented what they saw. The more useful distinction is between the experience and its interpretation: people genuinely saw striking lights, but the conclusion that those lights represented unknown craft was not securely established.

The Salem image is best regarded as a famous but evidentially weak photograph, with window reflection providing the most economical explanation. The 1964 Boston cluster is more informative because investigators had multiple witnesses and a traceable aircraft route. Most reports were convincingly associated with the illuminated advertising flight, although the Harvard Bridge testimony leaves a limited unresolved remainder.

Together, the cases offer a practical rule for assessing other Massachusetts sightings. Before asking what unknown vehicle might produce a light, first ask where the observer was standing, whether glass was present, which aircraft or advertising operations were active, how accurately the time was recorded and whether several reports genuinely overlap. In Salem and Boston, those ordinary questions explain far more than the dramatic images and descriptions initially suggest.

  function buildUrl(query, reference, marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket];
    if (!cfg) return '#';
    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
