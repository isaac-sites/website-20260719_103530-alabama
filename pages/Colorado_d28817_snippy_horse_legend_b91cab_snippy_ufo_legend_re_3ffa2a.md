## Introduction

The death of a horse near Alamosa, [Colorado]({{ 'what-colorados-ufo-history-really/' | relative_url }}), in September 1967 became famous not because anyone saw a UFO attack it, but because a series of newspaper stories, retellings and embellishments gradually transformed an unexplained animal death into one of the most influential UFO legends in American history. The case helped establish a pattern that would later dominate animal-mutilation folklore: unusual carcass conditions, reports of strange lights, alleged physical traces on the ground and speculation that grew faster than the available evidence. By the time the story spread nationwide, many readers encountered it not as a puzzling livestock death but as a dramatic “flying saucer” mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[Denver Public Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos and horse called snippy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Denver Public LibraryUFOs and a Horse Called Snippy20 Oct 2020 — This odd tale formed the impetus for Linda Mouton Howe&#x27;s seminal work, &quot;...</span></span></span>

Within Colorado’s UFO history, the importance of the [Snippy case]({{ 'snippy-case/' | relative_url }}) lies less in what happened to the horse and more in how reporting shaped public understanding. ## The First Reports and the Confused Name

One of the earliest signs that the story was changing as it spread was the horse’s name. The animal was actually a mare called Lady, but newspapers across the United States repeatedly identified her as “[Snippy]({{ 'snippy/' | relative_url }})”. Snippy was reportedly the name of her sire, yet the mistaken name became permanently attached to the case and remains the version most people recognise today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[denverlibrary.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos and horse called snippy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Denver Public LibraryUFOs and a Horse Called Snippy20 Oct 2020 — This odd tale formed the impetus for Linda Mouton Howe&#x27;s seminal work, &quot;...</span></span></span>

The first nationally distributed stories appeared in early October 1967, nearly a month after the horse was found. Headlines emphasised mystery and possible flying saucer involvement. Sensational wording encouraged readers to view the case through a UFO lens before any formal investigation had reached conclusions. Reports highlighted apparently bloodless remains, smooth cuts, missing flesh and a lack of tracks around the carcass. These details were often presented as established facts rather than observations that might have alternative explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutilation_of_%22Snippy%22_the_horse" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span></span></span>

As newspapers copied and condensed each other's reporting, the story became increasingly standardised. Readers in different states encountered similar dramatic claims, while uncertainties and contradictions received much less attention. The result was a powerful narrative: a healthy horse found mutilated under circumstances that supposedly defied normal explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutilation_of_%22Snippy%22_the_horse" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span></span></span>

## How Lights and Ground Marks Entered the Story

A crucial shift occurred when reports about the carcass became linked to separate stories about unusual lights in the [San Luis Valley]({{ 'san-luis-valley/' | relative_url }}). No witness reported seeing a UFO attack the horse. Instead, the UFO connection emerged by association. Accounts circulated that strange aerial objects had been seen in the region around the same period, and one elderly witness recalled a large object passing overhead, though she could not identify it. Over time, these separate elements were woven together into a single narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[Denver Public Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos and horse called snippy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Denver Public LibraryUFOs and a Horse Called Snippy20 Oct 2020 — This odd tale formed the impetus for Linda Mouton Howe&#x27;s seminal work, &quot;...</span></span></span>

These claims became central to later retellings because they appeared to provide physical evidence rather than mere speculation. Yet many were poorly documented. The reported radiation readings were not accompanied by detailed measurement records. The ground marks were not shown to have been created at the same time as the horse’s death. The alleged landing traces were interpreted through a UFO framework rather than demonstrated to be connected to an aircraft or unknown object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[Denver Public Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos and horse called snippy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Denver Public LibraryUFOs and a Horse Called Snippy20 Oct 2020 — This odd tale formed the impetus for Linda Mouton Howe&#x27;s seminal work, &quot;...</span></span></span>

The process resembles other famous UFO cases in which ordinary or ambiguous features of a landscape acquire significance once observers begin looking for evidence of a landing. In the Snippy case, the physical traces became more important in later retellings than they appear to have been in the earliest stages of the investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[Denver Public Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos and horse called snippy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Denver Public LibraryUFOs and a Horse Called Snippy20 Oct 2020 — This odd tale formed the impetus for Linda Mouton Howe&#x27;s seminal work, &quot;...</span></span></span>

## Why the Story Spread So Widely

The timing helped turn the case into a national phenomenon. The late 1960s were a period of intense public interest in flying saucers, and stories that combined UFOs with apparent physical evidence attracted substantial attention. The Snippy reports offered something unusual: not merely a sighting in the sky but a tangible object that people could visit and photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutilation_of_%22Snippy%22_the_horse" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span></span></span>

The case also introduced a theme that would become highly influential in later decades. Before Snippy, UFO reports usually focused on lights, craft or witness encounters. After Snippy, the idea that mysterious animal deaths might be linked to UFOs entered popular culture. ## What Later Investigations Weakened or Rejected

The strongest challenge to the legend came from later investigations that examined the available evidence more critically. Researchers associated with the University of Colorado's UFO study, commonly known as the Condon Committee, reviewed the case and concluded that there was no evidence linking the horse's death to abnormal or extraterrestrial causes. Investigators found no reliable support for claims that a UFO had been involved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutilation_of_%22Snippy%22_the_horse" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span></span></span>

Most importantly, no witness ever observed a UFO interacting with the animal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[Denver Public Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos and horse called snippy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Denver Public LibraryUFOs and a Horse Called Snippy20 Oct 2020 — This odd tale formed the impetus for Linda Mouton Howe&#x27;s seminal work, &quot;...</span></span></span>

## How a Mystery Became a Legend

The enduring significance of Snippy in Colorado UFO history is not that investigators uncovered evidence of alien activity. Instead, the case demonstrates how folklore develops. A dead horse, unusual carcass conditions, local stories about strange lights and a series of dramatic newspaper reports combined to create a narrative far more powerful than the underlying evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[Denver Public Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos and horse called snippy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Denver Public LibraryUFOs and a Horse Called Snippy20 Oct 2020 — This odd tale formed the impetus for Linda Mouton Howe&#x27;s seminal work, &quot;...</span></span></span>
