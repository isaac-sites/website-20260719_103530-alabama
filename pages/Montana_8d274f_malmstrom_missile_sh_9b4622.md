## Introduction

Ten Minuteman nuclear missiles assigned to Echo Flight at Malmstrom Air Force Base lost strategic alert status on the morning of 16 March 1967. That shutdown is documented. What is not documented contemporaneously is the dramatic claim now attached to it: that a glowing UFO hovered near the [Montana]({{ 'why-montana-became-a-ufo-landmark/' | relative_url }}) missile sites and disabled the weapons.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Montana_8d274f_malmstrom_missile_sh_9b4622-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Malmstrom" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The UFO interpretation rests mainly on accounts publicised almost three decades later, particularly by former launch officer Robert Salas. Those accounts describe strange lights, frightened guards and a second missile shutdown at another control centre. Yet the surviving military history places the confirmed Echo Flight failure at 08:45, records no verified unusual activity around the sites and treats the incident as a technical malfunction. The case matters because it joins a genuine Cold War systems failure to later testimony that is vivid but chronologically unstable. The most defensible conclusion is therefore narrow: Malmstrom suffered a serious and initially unexplained missile-control failure, but no surviving evidence proves that an unidentified object caused it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/malmstromufo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[documents.theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pdf14 Jun 2001 — On 16!-2rch 1967 at 084S, all sites in Echo (E) Flight, l&#x27;oaL... ## What happened at Echo Flight

Echo Flight was one of several groups of ten dispersed Minuteman launch facilities controlled from an underground launch control centre in central Montana. At 08:45 on 16 March 1967, all ten Echo missiles registered “No-Go” indications and lost strategic alert status within a very short interval. Contemporary records estimated that the sequence occurred over roughly 10 to 40 seconds. No other comparable flight in the wing failed at the same time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/malmstromufo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pdf14 Jun 2001 — On 16!-2rch 1967 at 084S, all sites in Echo (E) Flight, l&#x27;oaL... “Lost strategic alert” did not mean that nuclear warheads detonated, that missiles launched or that they were permanently destroyed. It meant that the launch facilities were no longer reporting as ready for immediate use. Maintenance and targeting personnel were sent to the sites, diagnostic information was recovered from the missile guidance systems, and the ten weapons were restored to alert without wholesale replacement of launch-facility equipment. The failure was nevertheless alarming because a single event appeared to have affected an entire flight of geographically separated missiles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2023/03/the-belt-montana-ufo-sighting-and.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Belt, Montana UFO Sighting and the &quot;Attack&quot; on Echo...3 Mar 2023 — Guidance &amp; Control channel 50 dump data was collected from E-7 fa...</span></span></span>

The wing’s historical report described a “normal controlled shutdown” at the affected sites, although investigators could not initially identify the signal or condition that had triggered it. Analysis of diagnostic data reportedly indicated that the guidance and control sections had not suffered the internal faults suggested by the first console indications. Investigators therefore considered whether an external electrical influence, a power disturbance or another common input had reached the missile-control equipment. Tests intended to reproduce the event did not establish a definitive cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/malmstromufo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pdf14 Jun 2001 — On 16!-2rch 1967 at 084S, all sites in Echo (E) Flight, l&#x27;oaL... That technical uncertainty is important, but it has often been overstated. “Cause not determined” does not mean that investigators found evidence of an exotic craft. It means that the available fault data did not identify a confirmed mechanism. Complex military systems can fail through brief electrical transients, component interactions or signals that leave incomplete diagnostic traces, especially when ten installations are linked to a common command and communications architecture.

## How the UFO story became attached

The best-known version comes from Robert Salas, a former Air Force missile officer who began speaking publicly about the incident in the 1990s. Salas said that while serving in an underground launch capsule, he received a call from a guard reporting strange lights moving silently in the sky. A few minutes later, according to his account, the guard called again in an agitated state and described a pulsating red or orange oval hovering above the facility’s front gate. Salas said missiles then began entering an unlaunchable condition while the object remained overhead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcnews.com/Technology/airmen-govt-clean-ufos/story?id=11738715" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcnews.com">[abcnews.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Former Airmen to Govt.: Come Clean on UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsFormer Airmen to Govt.: Come Clean on UFOs - ABC News...</span></span></span>

The story is powerful because it appears to connect two events in real time: a close-range sighting above a secured nuclear facility and a near-simultaneous systems failure underground. Salas has consistently argued that the timing makes coincidence unlikely, although he did not personally see the reported object from the buried capsule. His knowledge of the aerial event came through telephone reports from security personnel above ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcnews.com/Technology/airmen-govt-clean-ufos/story?id=11738715" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcnews.com">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Former Airmen to Govt.: Come Clean on UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsFormer Airmen to Govt.: Come Clean on UFOs - ABC News...</span></span></span>

Former targeting officer Robert Jamison later added that he had been called to assist with restarting missiles during the same general period and had heard rumours of UFO activity. He also recalled speaking with a distressed guard who described approaching red lights. Such recollections suggest that unusual-light stories circulated among some Malmstrom personnel. They do not, however, independently establish that an object was physically present at Echo Flight when its missiles failed. Jamison was not an eyewitness to a craft disabling the weapons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/2010/09/tinfoil-tuesdays-ufos-neutered-nukes-air-force-officers-claim" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Tinfoil Tuesdays: UFOs Neutered Nukes, Officers Claim</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Tinfoil Tuesdays: UFOs Neutered Nukes, Officers Claim</span></span></span>

Public interest grew further through books, documentaries and a 2010 press conference at which Salas and other former military personnel argued that UFOs had interfered with nuclear installations. News coverage often compressed the story into a simple claim that an object “shut down” ten missiles, leaving less room for the uncertain date, disputed location and difference between firsthand observation and later hearsay.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcnews.com/Technology/airmen-govt-clean-ufos/story?id=11738715" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcnews.com">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Former Airmen to Govt.: Come Clean on UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsFormer Airmen to Govt.: Come Clean on UFOs - ABC News...</span></span></span>

## Why the chronology is a major problem

The central weakness is that Salas’s later account does not fit neatly onto the documented Echo Flight event. The confirmed Echo shutdown occurred in the morning on 16 March. Salas has more recently placed his own experience during an evening shift on 24 March at Oscar Flight, near Roy, Montana, with Fred Meiwald as his commander. That is a different launch control centre, a different date and a different time of day.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/malmstromufo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pdf14 Jun 2001 — On 16!-2rch 1967 at 084S, all sites in Echo (E) Flight, l&#x27;oaL... In the channel 50 analysis it was sho....n th2.t the...</span></span></span>

Salas has explained that when he first learnt about the declassified [Echo records]({{ 'echo-records/' | relative_url }}), he assumed they described the event he remembered. Later research led him to identify Oscar Flight instead. This revision does not prove that his memory is false, but it removes the most straightforward link between his reported red object and the documented ten-missile Echo shutdown. A witness can accurately remember an alarming call while misremembering its date or later associating it with the wrong technical incident. After nearly 30 years, that risk is substantial.

There is also no comparable surviving official record showing that all ten Oscar Flight missiles went offline during Salas’s reported encounter. Fred Meiwald reportedly confirmed that guards had mentioned an unusual light, but disputed or did not recall the full missile-loss sequence described by Salas. Sceptical reconstructions consequently separate the story into two events: missiles failed at Echo without a verified UFO, while an unusual light may have been reported around Oscar without a documented flight-wide shutdown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mymetmedia.com/lets-talk-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mymetmedia.com">[mymetmedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mymetmedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">My Met Media Let&#x27;s talk UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">My Met Media Let&#x27;s talk UFOs</span></span></span>

Accounts connected with Echo Flight itself are also conflicted. Deputy crew commander Walter Figel recalled receiving a radio remark about a “UFO” near a launch facility, but he has described the comment as something he treated as joking or unconfirmed rather than as a formal sighting report. Echo commander Eric Carlson denied that a UFO was involved and regarded the shutdown as an equipment failure. Later writers have interpreted Figel’s recollections in opposite ways: proponents treat the reported word “UFO” as corroboration, while critics emphasise that neither officer described seeing an object and that Carlson rejected the claimed connection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.mymetmedia.com/lets-talk-ufos/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mymetmedia.com">[mymetmedia.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mymetmedia.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">My Met Media Let&#x27;s talk UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">My Met Media Let&#x27;s talk UFOs</span></span></span>

This is more than a minor disagreement over detail. The UFO case depends upon a tight sequence: object observed, missiles disabled, object departs. Once the reported sighting and the documented failure are separated by location, date or uncertain recollection, the apparent cause-and-effect relationship becomes much weaker.

## What the contemporary records say

The surviving command history is the strongest evidence because it was compiled close to the event and contains the engineering investigation. It records the ten-missile shutdown, diagnostic findings and concern within Strategic Air Command. It also states that rumours of unidentified flying objects around Echo Flight at the time of the fault were investigated and “disproven”. A mobile strike team that had visited launch facilities that morning reportedly told investigators that it had observed no unusual activity or sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/malmstromufo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pdf14 Jun 2001 — On 16!-2rch 1967 at 084S, all sites in Echo (E) Flight, l&#x27;oaL... That wording does not demonstrate that every guard was interviewed or that every relevant note survives. Military records can omit informal conversations, and the absence of a formal UFO report is not proof that no one saw an unusual light. Nevertheless, the history creates a serious evidential obstacle: the document that confirms the failure also records a contemporary check for unusual activity and reports a negative result.

Nor does the technical report describe physical damage consistent with a nearby attack. The missiles were returned to strategic alert, and diagnostic work focused on electrical and logic-system behaviour. Investigators considered an adverse power effect and attempted to reproduce the conditions, but the available tests reportedly produced negative results. The unresolved engineering question was how a common disturbance had generated the shutdown indications, not how a visible craft had penetrated the sites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2023/03/the-belt-montana-ufo-sighting-and.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Belt, Montana UFO Sighting and the &quot;Attack&quot; on Echo...3 Mar 2023 — Guidance &amp; Control channel 50 dump data was collected from E-7 fa...</span></span></span>

## Could an electrical effect explain it?

A common electrical disturbance has long been one of the leading technical alternatives. The sites were dispersed, but their reporting and control systems shared communications pathways and related equipment. An abnormal signal, voltage transient or failure in a common subsystem could potentially place multiple missiles into a controlled shutdown without producing obvious damage at every facility.

Investigators examined whether commercial power or another adverse power condition could have generated the event. Their tests did not conclusively reproduce the shutdown, which is why the original cause remained undetermined. That failure to reproduce the fault limits confidence in any single conventional explanation, but it does not make the UFO interpretation more probable unless separate evidence connects an object to the electronics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/malmstromufo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pdf14 Jun 2001 — On 16!-2rch 1967 at 084S, all sites in Echo (E) Flight, l&#x27;oaL... In 2025, *The Wall Street Journal* reported that Pentagon investigators had traced the Malmstrom story to a classified electromagnetic-pulse test capable of interfering with missile systems. This would offer a conventional explanation for both the unusual electrical effects and the later secrecy surrounding them. However, the detailed investigation and technical documentation supporting that claim have not been publicly released. The report should therefore be treated as a significant new allegation from officials familiar with a Pentagon review, not as a fully demonstrated solution available for independent examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wsj.com/politics/national-security/ufo-us-disinformation-45376f7e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wsj.com">[The Wall Street Journal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wsj.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wsj.com.</span></span></span>

The reported electromagnetic explanation also needs to be matched precisely to the historical evidence. It would have to account for the date, test location, affected equipment, safety authorisations and diagnostic signatures recorded in 1967. Until those underlying records are released, it strengthens the case for a classified terrestrial cause but does not conclusively close the investigation.

## What remains unexplained

Several elements remain legitimately uncertain. The exact technical trigger for the Echo Flight shutdown was not established in the declassified engineering material. Some former personnel remembered UFO rumours or reports of unusual lights around Malmstrom’s missile fields. The surviving archive may also be incomplete, particularly for informal security communications and any classified testing.

Yet the gaps are narrower than popular retellings imply. There is no contemporaneous document confirming a structured object above Echo Flight when its missiles failed. No photograph, radar record, instrument reading or physical trace ties an aerial phenomenon to the shutdown. The most detailed red-object narrative emerged decades later and migrated between Echo and Oscar Flight as Salas attempted to reconstruct its date and location. The missile failure is well evidenced; the proposed UFO mechanism is not.

Ordinary celestial objects may also explain at least some reported lights. Mars was conspicuous in the relevant period and has been proposed as the source of orange or reddish lights seen at a distance. That explanation cannot account for every claimed manoeuvre in Salas’s later description, but such motion reports were relayed verbally rather than preserved through radar or calibrated observation. Apparent hovering, sudden movement and changes of direction are particularly difficult to judge at night without a reliable distance reference.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://badufos.blogspot.com/2014/01/discovery-canadas-close-encounters-mars.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: badufos.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">badufos.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">discovery canadas close encounters mars</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">discovery canadas close encounters mars</span></span></span>

## Did a UFO disable the missiles?

The evidence does not justify that conclusion. Ten Echo Flight missiles unquestionably lost alert status on 16 March 1967, and the common cause was serious enough to prompt an engineering investigation. That remains one of the most striking documented technical incidents in Montana’s UFO history.

The claim of UFO involvement, however, depends on later witness narratives that conflict over date, flight and sequence. Contemporary military records acknowledge UFO rumours but say they were checked and not substantiated. A classified electromagnetic test has more recently been reported as the actual cause, although the supporting technical files have not been made public.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/malmstromufo.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">pdf14 Jun 2001 — On 16!-2rch 1967 at 084S, all sites in Echo (E) Flight, l&#x27;oaL... In the channel 50 analysis it was sho....n th2.t the...</span></span></span>

The fairest assessment is that Malmstrom combines a **confirmed missile shutdown**, **possible but poorly documented reports of unusual lights**, and an **unproved causal connection between the two**. Later reporting has expanded the story and produced additional testimony, but it has not supplied the missing contemporaneous evidence. In some respects, the evolving chronology has weakened the original UFO claim by showing that the most famous sighting account may not describe the documented Echo Flight failure at all.
