## Introduction

The crew of United Airlines Flight 105 made one of the most influential aviation reports of the 1947 flying-saucer wave. On the evening of 4 July 1947, Captain Emil J. Smith, First Officer Ralph Stevens and stewardess Marty Morrow all reported seeing groups of unusual, disc-like objects while their DC-3 was flying west after departing Boise on its route towards Seattle. Because the witnesses were experienced airline professionals and because three crew members independently confirmed that something unusual had been seen, the case quickly became one of the strongest pilot reports associated with the Pacific Northwest's early UFO history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. J. Smith...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 1947</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Washington_a27a66_washington_1947_ufo_662179_flight_105_testimony_30b6ed-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Flight 105 illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Washington_a27a66_washington_1947_ufo_662179_flight_105_testimony_30b6ed-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Washington_a27a66_washington_1947_ufo_662179_flight_105_testimony_30b6ed-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

At the same time, Flight 105 illustrates why even credible witnesses do not necessarily produce conclusive evidence. The crew's observations were visual only, no photographs or radar data were available, estimates of distance and speed were uncertain, and the sighting occurred after more than a week of intense national publicity following [Kenneth Arnold]({{ 'kenneth-arnold/' | relative_url }})'s Mount Rainier report. Those strengths and limitations have remained central to historical assessments of the case.

## What the crew reported on 4 July

United Airlines Flight 105 departed Boise, Idaho, shortly after 9 p.m. Mountain Time on 4 July 1947, heading towards Seattle. According to Captain Smith's account, the crew first noticed a formation of five bright, wingless objects ahead of the aircraft. Smith described one object as slightly larger than the others. Co-pilot Ralph Stevens initially assumed they were conventional aircraft and briefly switched on the DC-3's landing lights. The crew later said the objects appeared to react by spreading out from a tight cluster into a looser formation before repeatedly changing spacing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. J. Smith...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 1947</span></span></span>

The first group eventually disappeared, after which the crew reported seeing a second formation of four similar objects travelling westward. Smith later said the objects appeared flat underneath and rounded above, without visible wings or tails. He also believed they moved much faster than aircraft known at the time, although he repeatedly acknowledged that neither their distance nor their true size could be determined with confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. J. Smith...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 1947</span></span></span>

An important feature of the report is that stewardess Marty Morrow was called into the cockpit after the initial sighting rather than being the original observer. She nevertheless confirmed that she could also see unusual objects ahead of the aircraft. That independent confirmation strengthened the basic claim that more than one person observed something unusual, even if it did not resolve what the objects actually were.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/reports/470704emmett_e.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">470704emmett e</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">470704e...</span></span></span>

## Why professional witnesses matter

Flight crews are often given greater weight than casual observers because they spend thousands of hours looking through changing weather and light conditions while distinguishing aircraft, stars, clouds and atmospheric effects. Captain Smith was an experienced United Air Lines pilot, and Stevens was likewise familiar with airborne traffic and visual navigation.

The report also gained attention because Smith had publicly dismissed earlier "flying saucer" stories before his own experience. Newspaper interviews from late June quoted him suggesting that Kenneth Arnold may simply have misidentified reflections or some ordinary phenomenon. According to later recollections, Smith jokingly remarked before departure on 4 July that he would believe in flying discs only when he saw them himself. After the flight, he said his opinion had changed because he believed he had witnessed something he could not identify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. J. Smith...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 1947</span></span></span>

This apparent shift from scepticism to uncertainty made the story especially newsworthy. Contemporary newspapers treated it as evidence that trained aviation professionals were beginning to report similar experiences, helping transform the flying-saucer story from an isolated claim into a broader national discussion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. ## Publicity, geography and unresolved doubts

The Flight 105 report is often linked with [Washington]({{ 'why-washington-became-americas-first-ufo/' | relative_url }}) because the aircraft was bound for Seattle and became part of the wider Pacific Northwest wave that followed Kenneth Arnold's Mount Rainier sighting. However, the observation itself almost certainly occurred much earlier in the journey, shortly after departure from Boise and probably over south-western Idaho near Emmett, before the aircraft reached Oregon or Washington. That geographical distinction matters because the case belongs historically to the Washington-centred flying-saucer flap without actually being a Washington observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. J. Smith...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 1947</span></span></span>

Another important limitation is timing. By 4 July the phrase "flying saucer" had dominated newspapers for more than a week. Aircrew, airport staff and the general public were already discussing the reports, and Smith himself later recalled being teased by control tower personnel to watch out for [flying saucers]({{ 'flying-saucers/' | relative_url }}) before take-off. Such publicity does not invalidate the sighting, but it raises the possibility that expectations influenced how unusual aerial objects were interpreted. Historians routinely note this as a factor when weighing reports from the height of the 1947 wave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. J. Smith...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 1947</span></span></span>

The case also lacks the kinds of evidence that would allow modern investigators to test competing explanations. There were:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* no photographs;
* no radar confirmation;
* no recovered physical evidence;
* no precise measurements of distance, altitude or speed;
* no independent tracking from multiple locations that could reconstruct the objects' paths.

</div>

Without those data, extraordinary estimates of speed remain dependent on assumptions about how far away the objects actually were. If their distance was overestimated, then their apparent velocity would also have been exaggerated.

## How later investigators judged the case

Later UFO researchers generally continued to regard Flight 105 as one of the stronger pilot reports from 1947, largely because it involved multiple trained witnesses observing the objects for several minutes under apparently clear conditions. Physicist James E. McDonald, writing in the 1960s after interviewing Captain Smith, considered the report historically significant and accepted Smith as a reliable witness while carefully avoiding claims about the objects' origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. J. Smith...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 4, 1947</span></span></span>

Even McDonald's favourable assessment illustrates the limits of the evidence. He argued that the sighting resisted an obvious conventional explanation, but he also acknowledged that the objects' size, distance and exact nature could not be established. His conclusion was not that extraterrestrial craft had been demonstrated, but that the observation remained unidentified on the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/fig/ual105.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: UAL Flight 105, July 4, 1947 - Capt E. That distinction reflects the broader pattern seen throughout the 1947 Washington flap. Reliable witnesses can establish that an unusual observation occurred without establishing exactly what was observed.

## How strong was the Flight 105 sighting?

Flight 105 remains one of the most credible witness accounts connected with the Pacific Northwest's formative UFO wave because it combines multiple observers, experienced aviation personnel and relatively detailed descriptions. Those factors make it stronger than many brief newspaper anecdotes from the same period.

Its evidential ceiling, however, is equally clear. The report depends entirely on human observation during an era of intense publicity, and the key quantities needed to identify the objects—distance, size and speed—were never independently measured. As a result, the case continues to occupy a middle ground within Washington's UFO history: more persuasive than many contemporary reports, but far from proving the existence of extraordinary craft.

    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
