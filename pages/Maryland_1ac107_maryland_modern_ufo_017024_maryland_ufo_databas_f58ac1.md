## Introduction

[Maryland]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-marylands-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s UFO databases are useful, but they are often misunderstood. Public archives now contain close to 2,000 reports linked to the state, making Maryland one of the more heavily represented areas in national sighting collections. Yet these databases do not prove that 2,000 unexplained craft crossed Maryland skies. They prove something narrower: that thousands of people reported seeing something they could not immediately identify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/ndx/?id=loc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Reports by Location NUFORC Reports by Location; USA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC Reports by LocationNUFORC Reports by Location; USA - Louisiana, 1231; USA - Massachusetts, 2892; USA - Maryland, 1986; U...</span></span></span>

## Why witness estimates and map clusters mislead

The strongest visual impression many readers get from UFO databases comes from totals and maps. Maryland appears to have hundreds of reports concentrated around Baltimore, Washington's suburbs and major transport corridors. At first glance, such clusters can look like evidence of hotspots.

In practice, population density is a major confounding factor. More people looking at the sky generally produce more reports. Areas with large populations, busy airports, military traffic, extensive road networks and active internet users tend to generate more database entries regardless of whether anything unusual is present. A cluster on a map is therefore not automatically a cluster of unexplained phenomena. It may simply be a cluster of observers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10721628/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

Another problem involves witness estimates. Most reports depend on visual judgements made without reliable distance measurements. If the distance is unknown, estimates of size and speed become highly uncertain.

A Maryland report from North Bethesda in 2024 illustrates the issue. The witness described a metallic rectangular object apparently longer than two school buses, seen for only three or four seconds. Yet the report itself acknowledges uncertainty about distance. Without knowing how far away the object was, neither the witness nor later readers can accurately calculate its actual size. A distant conventional aircraft, drone or other object may appear dramatically different from its true dimensions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=181189" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nuforc.org.</span></span></span>

Maps can also be distorted by reporting behaviour. A highly publicised sighting, media coverage or a social-media discussion may encourage additional submissions from people who previously ignored similar observations. Rising report numbers therefore do not necessarily indicate rising numbers of unusual aerial events. They may reflect rising awareness and willingness to report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://marylandmatters.org/2019/06/26/20-ufo-sightings-reported-in-state-during-2019-so-far/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: marylandmatters.org">[marylandmatters.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">marylandmatters.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">20 ufo sightings reported in state during 2019 so far</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">20 ufo sightings reported in state during 2019 so far</span></span></span>

## What the databases can prove

Although the limitations are significant, Maryland’s UFO databases are not meaningless.

They can demonstrate several things with reasonable confidence.

### Reporting patterns are real

The databases clearly show that Maryland residents have continued reporting unusual aerial observations across many decades. Reports appear year after year rather than being confined to a single historical wave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/subndx/?id=lMD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC Reports for State MDNUFORC Reports for State MD.; Open, 08/31/2025 02:40, Ocean City; Open, 08/22/2025 04:30, Ocean City...</span></span></span>

### Certain descriptions recur

Witnesses repeatedly report similar categories of objects, including lights, triangular formations, spheres, discs and structured-looking craft. This does not prove those objects are physically identical, but it does reveal recurring patterns in perception and description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/subndx/?id=lMD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC Reports for State MDNUFORC Reports for State MD.; Open, 08/31/2025 02:40, Ocean City; Open, 08/22/2025 04:30, Ocean City...</span></span></span>

### Some events attracted multiple observers

A number of Maryland cases involve several witnesses rather than one observer. Multiple witnesses generally provide a stronger starting point for investigation than a solitary account because they reduce the chance that the entire event was a purely personal misperception. However, multiple witnesses still do not automatically establish what was seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=39907" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC UFO Sighting 39907NUFORC UFO Sighting 39907. ### Public concern and interest can be measured

The databases provide a useful record of how Maryland residents respond to unusual aerial events. In that sense, they are valuable historical and sociological sources even when the underlying object remains unidentified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10721628/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

## What the databases cannot prove

The most important limitation is that a database entry is not the same thing as a verified event.

A report usually cannot prove:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* The exact distance of an object.
* Its true size.
* Its speed.
* Its altitude.
* Its origin.
* Whether it was a drone, aircraft, satellite, balloon, astronomical object or something else.
* Whether it represented technology beyond known human capabilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

</div>

Most public reports contain only a witness description and perhaps a photograph or video taken under uncontrolled conditions. Critical information such as radar returns, calibrated sensors, multiple camera angles, atmospheric data and independent measurements is usually absent. The report noted persistent problems involving sensor calibration, missing metadata and the lack of multiple independent measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[NASA Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

## How to separate unresolved reports from strong evidence

Readers often assume that every unexplained report carries equal weight. In reality, some database entries are much more useful than others.

Stronger cases usually contain several of the following elements:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Multiple independent witnesses.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[science.nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>
* Immediate reporting rather than memories submitted years later.
* Corroborating photographs or video.
* Precise timing and location data.
* Aviation, radar or sensor information.
* Sufficient detail for investigators to test alternative explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: science.nasa.gov">[nasa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">science.nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NASA ScienceIndependent Study Team ReportAt present, analysis of UAP data is hampered by poor sensor calibration, the lack of multiple me...</span></span></span>

</div>

Weaker cases tend to involve:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Extremely brief observations.
* Single witnesses.
* Uncertain dates or locations.
* Large assumptions about size or speed.
* Missing supporting evidence.
* Reports submitted long after the event occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=181189" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nuforc.org.</span></span></span>

</div>

This distinction explains why impressive statewide totals do not automatically translate into impressive evidence. A database containing thousands of brief, poorly documented reports may offer less evidential value than a handful of cases supported by multiple independent data sources.

## The most useful way to read Maryland's UFO totals

Maryland's UFO databases are best viewed as archives of claims rather than archives of proven mysteries.

They show that unusual aerial observations are reported regularly across the state and that some locations generate repeated reports over time. They help identify patterns worthy of further investigation and preserve witness testimony that might otherwise be lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/subndx/?id=lMD" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[NUFORC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NUFORC Reports for State MDNUFORC Reports for State MD.; Open, 08/31/2025 02:40, Ocean City; Open, 08/22/2025 04:30, Ocean City...</span></span></span>
