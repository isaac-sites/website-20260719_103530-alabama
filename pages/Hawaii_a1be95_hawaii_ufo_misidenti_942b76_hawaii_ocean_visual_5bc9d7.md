## Introduction

One reason some Hawaiian UFO reports sound especially convincing is that the observer may be looking across a vast, dark ocean with almost no visual reference points. A light that is actually stationary, distant, or moving slowly can appear to drift, accelerate, descend, or even plunge into the sea. These effects are not unique to [Hawaii]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-hawaiis-ufo/' | relative_url }}), but the islands provide unusually favourable conditions for them: long views over open water, very dark skies away from cities, and horizons that can become nearly invisible at night. In UFO investigations, this matters because witnesses may accurately describe what they saw while unintentionally misjudging how far away it was or how it was moving.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/spatiald_visillus.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial Disorientation: Visual Illusions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationSpatial Disorientation: Visual IllusionsSeptember 15, 2016 — The Autokinetic Illusion gives you the impres...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2016</span></span></span>

## How Featureless Horizons Distort Distance

Human beings judge distance partly by comparing an object with nearby landmarks. Over Hawaii's ocean at night, many of those cues disappear. There may be no visible shoreline, buildings, mountains, roads or cloud layers to establish scale. A bright light could be a few kilometres away or hundreds of kilometres away, yet appear similarly isolated against the darkness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/spatiald_visillus.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[faa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial Disorientation: Visual Illusions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationSpatial Disorientation: Visual IllusionsSeptember 15, 2016 — The Autokinetic Illusion gives you the impres...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2016</span></span></span>

This creates a recurring problem in UFO reports. If an observer underestimates the distance to a light, its true motion can seem extraordinary. For example:

* A distant aircraft turning towards the observer may appear to stop suddenly and hover.
* A light moving directly towards or away from the observer may seem almost stationary.
* A distant object crossing a large stretch of sky can appear to move impossibly fast if its range is misjudged.
* A satellite or aircraft beyond the visible horizon can seem much lower than it really is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/spatiald_visillus.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[faa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial Disorientation: Visual Illusions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationSpatial Disorientation: Visual IllusionsSeptember 15, 2016 — The Autokinetic Illusion gives you the impres...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2016</span></span></span>

The agency treats such illusions as significant enough to affect trained pilots, not just casual observers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/spatiald_visillus.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial Disorientation: Visual Illusions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationSpatial Disorientation: Visual IllusionsSeptember 15, 2016 — The Autokinetic Illusion gives you the impres...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2016</span></span></span>

## Autokinesis and Apparent Motion at Night

One of the most important mechanisms in this category is autokinesis, also called the autokinetic illusion. It occurs when a person stares at a single light against a dark, featureless background. After several seconds, the light can appear to move even when it is stationary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/spatiald_visillus.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[faa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial Disorientation: Visual Illusions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationSpatial Disorientation: Visual IllusionsSeptember 15, 2016 — The Autokinetic Illusion gives you the impres...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2016</span></span></span>

The effect is well known in aviation. FAA safety material explains that a fixed light, such as a star, planet, aircraft light or ground light, can seem to drift across the observer's field of view when there are no surrounding reference points.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/spatiald_visillus.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial Disorientation: Visual Illusions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationSpatial Disorientation: Visual IllusionsSeptember 15, 2016 — The Autokinetic Illusion gives you the impres...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2016</span></span></span>

Over Hawaii's ocean, the conditions are often ideal for this illusion:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The background may be almost completely black.
* The horizon can disappear from view.
* The observer may focus intently on a single bright light.
* There may be no nearby objects against which motion can be judged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/spatiald_visillus.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[faa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial Disorientation: Visual Illusions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationSpatial Disorientation: Visual IllusionsSeptember 15, 2016 — The Autokinetic Illusion gives you the impres...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2016</span></span></span>

<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Wander left and right.
* Move in short zigzags.
* Rise and fall.
* Approach the observer.
* Pace a vehicle or vessel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/spatiald_visillus.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[faa.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial Disorientation: Visual Illusions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationSpatial Disorientation: Visual IllusionsSeptember 15, 2016 — The Autokinetic Illusion gives you the impres...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2016</span></span></span>

This mechanism has particular relevance to UFO reports involving bright planets or stars near the horizon. Venus, Sirius and other prominent celestial objects have repeatedly been mistaken for moving aircraft or unidentified objects because observers perceived motion that was not actually occurring.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensory_illusions_in_aviation" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sensory illusions in aviation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sensory illusions in aviation</span></span></span>

Importantly, autokinesis does not mean the witness imagined the light. The light is real. The error lies in the perceived motion.

## Why Descending Lights Seem to Enter the Sea

A recurring theme in coastal UFO reports is the claim that a mysterious light descended and disappeared into the ocean. In some cases, this description may be accurate. ## Atmospheric Effects Near the Ocean Horizon

Not every strange horizon sighting is purely a psychological illusion. The atmosphere above the ocean can bend light and alter the apparent position of distant objects. This process, known as refraction, can create mirages that raise, distort or stretch lights and vessels near the horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wikipedia.org.</span></span></span>

Under certain temperature conditions, especially when layers of warm and cool air form above the sea, distant objects may appear higher than their true position. Ships can seem suspended above the water, while lights beyond the geometric horizon can become visible and appear detached from the surface. More complex forms of mirage, often called Fata Morgana mirages, can produce rapidly changing shapes and unusual visual distortions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fata_Morgana_%28mirage%29" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fata Morgana (mirage</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fata Morgana (mirage</span></span></span>

These effects are genuine optical phenomena that can be photographed. However, to an observer who does not realise atmospheric refraction is involved, they may resemble hovering craft, elevated structures or lights behaving in unexpected ways.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.eoas.ubc.ca/courses/atsc113/sailing/met_concepts/10-met-local-conditions/10f-optical-phenomena/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: eoas.ubc.ca">[eoas.ubc.ca]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">eoas.ubc.ca</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ubc.ca.</span></span></span>

For Hawaii's UFO record, this provides another reason that unusual-looking lights over the Pacific are not automatically evidence of unusual objects.

## What This Means for Hawaiian UFO Reports

Dark ocean horizons do not explain every unidentified sighting in Hawaii. Some reports involve multiple witnesses, radar claims, video recordings or circumstances that require separate analysis. However, the combination of featureless night views, autokinesis, uncertain distance perception and atmospheric distortion offers a powerful explanation for many reports of lights apparently manoeuvring over the sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/spatiald_visillus.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial Disorientation: Visual Illusions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationSpatial Disorientation: Visual IllusionsSeptember 15, 2016 — The Autokinetic Illusion gives you the impres...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 15, 2016</span></span></span>

The key lesson is that visual certainty and physical certainty are not the same thing. A witness may be completely confident that a light moved, hovered or entered the ocean because that is exactly how it appeared.
