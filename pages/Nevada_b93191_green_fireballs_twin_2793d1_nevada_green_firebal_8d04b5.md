## Introduction

The short answer is that [Nevada]({{ 'why-nevada-became-americas-ufo-state/' | relative_url }}) became linked to the green-fireball mystery largely through later retellings, summaries and Pentagon histories rather than through the core operational work of Project Twinkle itself. The famous wave of bright green objects reported in the late 1940s was investigated because of concerns around sensitive military and atomic facilities in the American Southwest. Yet the surviving records show that Project Twinkle's dedicated observation programme was built around New Mexico, not Nevada. The confusion matters because Nevada's later association with nuclear testing and secret military activity has encouraged many readers to assume that the green-fireball investigation centred there. ## What later Pentagon summaries say about Nevada

One reason Nevada appears in modern discussions is that recent government histories describe the green-fireball reports as a phenomenon observed in both Nevada and New Mexico. The Pentagon's 2024 historical review by the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO) states that Project Twinkle was created to investigate reports witnessed by observers in Nevada and New Mexico.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AAROM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MAARO_Historical_Record_Repor...6 Mar 2024 — SECTION I: Introduction. That wording is historically significant because many earlier accounts focused almost entirely on New Mexico. Readers encountering the AARO summary can reasonably conclude that Nevada played a major operational role in the investigation. However, the surviving Project Twinkle documentation does not show Nevada functioning as the programme's principal observation area. Instead, the modern summary appears to describe the broader regional pattern of reports rather than the location where Twinkle's field effort was concentrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AAROM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MAARO_Historical_Record_Repor...6 Mar 2024 — SECTION I: Introduction. ## Where Project Twinkle actually placed its observers

The strongest evidence for Twinkle's geographical focus comes from the project's own reports and later studies of those records. Project Twinkle was established specifically to gather objective data on recurring green-fireball reports. To do that, investigators sought locations where sightings were considered frequent enough to justify continuous monitoring. Those locations were in New Mexico.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents2.theblackvault.com/documents/projectbluebook/ProjectTwinkle-November1951.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents2.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents2.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ProjectTwinkle November1951</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultProjectTwinkle-November1951.pdfNovember 1949, subject: &quot;Report of Conference on &#x27;&#x27;Green Fireballs&quot;. Contemporary descriptions of the programme repeatedly refer to New Mexico observation stations and New Mexico sighting concentrations. Reports from contractor personnel operating observation posts near Vaughn became some of the best-known Twinkle records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nbn.fm/ufo-files/episode/ep6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nbn.fm">[nbn.fm]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nbn.fm</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Neural Broadcast NetworkUFO Files: The New Mexico Green Fireball InvestigationOn May 24, 1950, personnel from the private contractor Land...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 24, 1950</span></span></span>

## Why the Nevada Test Site timeline is often misunderstood

A major source of confusion is the tendency to connect every unexplained aerial story in the Southwest with Nevada's later military history. Today, Nevada is strongly associated with secret aviation projects, [Area 51]({{ 'area-51/' | relative_url }}) and nuclear testing. Looking backwards, it can be tempting to place the green-fireball story into that same Nevada-centred narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AAROM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AARO Historical Record Report Vol</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MAARO_Historical_Record_Repor...6 Mar 2024 — SECTION I: Introduction. This report represents Volume I of the All-domain Anomaly Resol...</span></span></span>

The chronology is more complicated. The green-fireball wave that triggered official concern emerged in late 1948 and 1949, with particular attention focused on reports around Los Alamos, Sandia and other New Mexico installations connected to atomic research and defence work. Officials worried because the sightings appeared near sensitive facilities, not because they were already linked to Nevada's later test-site reputation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.history.com/articles/ufos-green-fireballs-nuclear-facilities-new-mexico" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.com">[History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos green fireballs nuclear facilities new mexico</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">When Mysterious Green Fireballs Worried the US...Aug 17, 2018 — The sightings were concentrated around the Los Alamos and Sandia...</span></span></span>

Nevada certainly became one of the most important locations in Cold War military history. Atmospheric nuclear testing and classified aerospace programmes later made the state a focal point for UFO speculation. However, that later reputation can obscure the fact that the original green-fireball investigation grew out of security concerns centred on New Mexico's atomic and military infrastructure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.11040" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fallout from U.S. atmospheric nuclear tests in New Mexico and Nevada (1945-1962)July 20, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 20, 2023</span></span></span>

Even though Twinkle's operational focus lay in New Mexico, Nevada remained attached to the green-fireball narrative for several reasons.
