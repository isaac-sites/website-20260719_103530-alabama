## Introduction

The most famous early UFO case from northern [Maine]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-maines-ufo-cases/' | relative_url }}) was not ultimately a mystery in the sky at all. On the night of 10 October 1952, military weather observers at [Presque Isle]({{ 'presque-isle/' | relative_url }}) Air Force Base and the nearby Limestone installation believed they were measuring a strange bright object. When their observations were combined, the calculations appeared to place an enormous object high above the Earth. For a time, the case looked important enough to attract the attention of Air Force investigators during one of the busiest years in American UFO history.

The twist was that the object was almost certainly Jupiter. A simple but crucial error in the way one observing station aligned its instrument with true north distorted the geometry of the sighting. Once investigators corrected the bearings, the apparent giant aerial object disappeared. The Presque Isle incident remains significant in Maine's UFO history because it shows how trained military personnel could produce a dramatic UFO report from a perfectly ordinary celestial object, and how a small technical mistake could transform an astronomical observation into an intelligence concern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66639/66639-h/66639-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project GutenbergThe World of Flying Saucers, by Donald H. ## What Observers Saw on 10 October 1952

The incident occurred at approximately 10 p.m. during a period when military installations across the United States were under pressure to report anything unusual in the skies. Weather personnel at Presque Isle observed a bright orange light low in the eastern sky and used a theodolite, a precision instrument designed to measure angles, to determine its position. According to later accounts, the object appeared as a bright circular light accompanied by several smaller greenish lights that seemed to flicker nearby.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66639/66639-h/66639-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project GutenbergThe World of Flying Saucers, by Donald H. ## How the Theodolite Alignment Error Distorted the Result

The key to the case lay not in the object itself but in the measurements.

A theodolite can accurately measure two different things: elevation, which is the angle above the horizon, and bearing, which is the horizontal direction of the target. Measuring elevation requires the instrument to be level. Measuring bearing requires something else: the instrument must first be aligned correctly with true north.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66639/66639-h/66639-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project GutenbergThe World of Flying Saucers, by Donald H. Menzel...Menzel AND Lyle G. Careless use of the theodolite had produced an er...</span></span></span>

Investigators later concluded that the Limestone observers had made a mistake when establishing that north reference. The error was not random. Instead, the entire set of bearings from that station was shifted by a consistent amount. Because triangulation depends on the intersection of directional lines from different locations, even a modest bearing error can produce a dramatically incorrect position for the supposed target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66639/66639-h/66639-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project GutenbergThe World of Flying Saucers, by Donald H. With the incorrect Limestone bearings included, the measurements appeared to intersect at a point in the atmosphere, creating the illusion of a huge airborne object. Once a constant correction was applied to account for the north-alignment mistake, the geometry changed completely. The lines of sight no longer converged on a nearby object. Instead, they pointed in the direction of Jupiter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66639/66639-h/66639-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project GutenbergThe World of Flying Saucers, by Donald H. Menzel...Menzel AND Lyle G. Careless use of the theodolite had produced an er...</span></span></span>

This transformed the case from an apparent aerial anomaly into a lesson in observational error. Nothing had changed in the sky; only the interpretation of the measurements had changed.

## Why Jupiter Was a Convincing Explanation

Jupiter was particularly well placed to generate confusion in October 1952. It was one of the brightest objects in the night sky and was visible in the eastern sky at the time of the observations. A bright planet near the horizon can appear unusually colourful because its light passes through more of Earth's atmosphere, producing orange, yellow or reddish hues and causing noticeable flickering.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66639/66639-h/66639-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project GutenbergThe World of Flying Saucers, by Donald H. The observers' report of additional lights near the main object also fits the astronomical explanation. Later analyses suggested that the smaller lights were likely Jupiter's bright Galilean moons, glimpsed through optical equipment and distorted by atmospheric turbulence. Through a theodolite or similar instrument, such nearby points of light can appear to shimmer, blink or shift position, especially when viewed close to the horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66639/66639-h/66639-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project GutenbergThe World of Flying Saucers, by Donald H. An especially revealing detail emerged after the initial report. According to the later reconstruction, some of the Presque Isle observers recognised the object as Jupiter when they saw it again the following night. However, that recognition apparently did not make its way back through the reporting chain in time to prevent the original UFO interpretation from circulating among investigators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66639/66639-h/66639-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project GutenbergThe World of Flying Saucers, by Donald H. The case therefore illustrates how an early conclusion can acquire a life of its own even after the witnesses themselves become less certain that anything unusual occurred.

## Why the Corrected Case Still Reached the Robertson Panel

The Presque Isle incident might seem too straightforward to deserve national attention, yet it became one of the cases reviewed by the CIA-sponsored Scientific Advisory Panel on Unidentified Flying Objects, better known as the Robertson Panel, in January 1953. The panel examined a selection of cases that Air Force personnel considered among the most important examples in the UFO files. ## What the Presque Isle Case Means in Maine UFO History

The Presque Isle incident remains one of Maine's most instructive UFO cases because it demonstrates a mechanism rather than a mystery.

Unlike many reports that depend entirely on witness recollections, this case involved recorded measurements and a reconstructable error. That makes it one of the clearer examples from the early Air Force files of a UFO report being generated by a combination of astronomical misidentification and flawed observational technique.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/files/66639/66639-h/66639-h.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project GutenbergThe World of Flying Saucers, by Donald H.         .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
