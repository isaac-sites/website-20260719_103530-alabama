## Introduction

The Lumberton water tower encounter is the single most discussed episode from the April 1975 UFO wave in south-eastern [North Carolina]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-north-carolinas/' | relative_url }}). Its importance rests on a combination of factors that are unusual in UFO history: a police officer reporting a close-range observation, a prominent landmark that appeared to provide a reference point for estimating distance and size, and a dramatic claim that a beam of light illuminated a water tower before the object departed silently. At the same time, the case illustrates a recurring problem in UFO investigations. The most striking details come primarily from witness testimony, while the kinds of evidence that might independently verify the event—photographs, radar tracks, instrument readings, or complete official records—are largely absent from the public record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/wp-content/uploads/1975-Lumberton-NC-Full-UFO-Report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE LUMBERTON REPORTPhil- Stanton, Luftberton patrol-man, was alone in his police car approximately 200 yards from the water tower. Within the wider [Lumberton UFO wave]({{ 'lumberton-wave/' | relative_url }}), the water tower sighting remains the closest thing to a flagship case. Yet its status depends less on physical evidence than on whether readers find the testimony of the witnesses, especially Officer Phil Stanton, persuasive. Understanding that tension is essential to understanding why the incident remains discussed decades later.

## What Really Happened Above Lumberton's Water Tower?

Shortly after 1:45 a.m. on 3 April 1975, [Lumberton police]({{ 'police-radio/' | relative_url }}) officers reported noticing an unusual light near the water tower behind Lumberton High School. Early impressions reportedly favoured a conventional explanation: some officers initially suspected they were looking at a helicopter. As they continued observing, however, they concluded that aspects of the object's behaviour did not fit that interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/the-1975-lumberton-ufo-wave-police-across-north-carolina-report-a-silent-v-shaped-craft/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The 1975 Lumberton UFO Wave: Police Across North...March 11, 2026 — 11 Mar 2026 — three Lumberton patrolmen—Phil Stanton, P.H...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 11, 2026</span></span></span>

The most detailed account came from Officer Phil Stanton. According to the later investigation published as *The Lumberton Report*, Stanton was positioned roughly 200 yards from the tower. He stated that he saw an object hovering directly above it and that it appeared silent. He described a triangular or wedge-like craft with lights along its edges and a bright forward-facing light or spotlight. The report further states that the beam illuminated lettering on the water tower before the object suddenly accelerated away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/wp-content/uploads/1975-Lumberton-NC-Full-UFO-Report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE LUMBERTON REPORTPhil- Stanton, Luftberton patrol-man, was alone in his police car approximately 200 yards from the water tower. The encounter became a focal point because it was not described as a distant light in the sky. Stanton believed he was observing something close to a known structure, which gave investigators a basis—at least in theory—for estimating altitude and apparent size. That perceived proximity is one reason the case has received more attention than many other reports from the same week.

## How the Water Tower Shaped Estimates of Size and Height

Most UFO reports suffer from a lack of scale. Witnesses often see lights against an empty sky, making it difficult to judge distance, speed, or dimensions. The Lumberton case is unusual because the object was reportedly observed in relation to a water tower that both witnesses and investigators could locate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/wp-content/uploads/1975-Lumberton-NC-Full-UFO-Report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE LUMBERTON REPORTPhil- Stanton, Luftberton patrol-man, was alone in his police car approximately 200 yards from the water tower. According to the investigative account, Stanton estimated the object to be around 200 feet above the ground. He also described it as roughly comparable in apparent size to his patrol car. Those estimates were not measurements but visual judgements based on the assumption that the object was genuinely near the tower rather than farther away in the background.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/wp-content/uploads/1975-Lumberton-NC-Full-UFO-Report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE LUMBERTON REPORTPhil- Stanton, Luftberton patrol-man, was alone in his police car approximately 200 yards from the water tower. This distinction is critical. If the object was positioned exactly where the witness believed it to be, the estimates become meaningful. If, however, the object was farther away than it appeared, then conclusions about its dimensions, altitude, and movement change dramatically. Night-time observations are especially vulnerable to such errors because darkness removes many of the visual cues people use to judge distance.

## The Spotlight Claim and Why It Matters

Among all the details associated with the encounter, the reported spotlight is arguably the most significant.

Witnesses described a bright beam directed downward toward the tower. According to Stanton's account, the light illuminated lettering on the structure. If accurate, this would imply more than a distant aerial light. It would suggest an object interacting with a specific target at close range.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/wp-content/uploads/1975-Lumberton-NC-Full-UFO-Report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE LUMBERTON REPORTPhil- Stanton, Luftberton patrol-man, was alone in his police car approximately 200 yards from the water tower. The spotlight claim also affects attempts to explain the sighting conventionally. A helicopter searchlight, for example, could produce a beam directed toward the ground. Yet witnesses emphasised the apparent silence of the object and its rapid departure, characteristics they believed did not match a helicopter. Conversely, sceptical observers note that perceptions of sound, distance and motion at night can be unreliable, particularly when an observer is focused on a bright light source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/wp-content/uploads/1975-Lumberton-NC-Full-UFO-Report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE LUMBERTON REPORTPhil- Stanton, Luftberton patrol-man, was alone in his police car approximately 200 yards from the water tower. He, t...</span></span></span>

The beam therefore sits at the centre of the debate. It is one of the most memorable elements of the story, but it is also supported almost entirely by eyewitness recollection rather than physical documentation.

## Why Missing Photographs, Radar and Records Limit the Claim

The strongest limitation on the Lumberton water tower encounter is not contradictory evidence. It is the lack of corroborating evidence.

No widely available photograph of the object is known from the incident. No film footage has emerged. No publicly accessible radar data has been presented showing an unidentified target over the tower. Nor has a complete package of original police records become available that would allow researchers to reconstruct the event minute by minute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openminds.tv/wp-content/uploads/1975-Lumberton-NC-Full-UFO-Report.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openminds.tv">[Openminds.tv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openminds.tv</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE LUMBERTON REPORTPhil- Stanton, Luftberton patrol-man, was alone in his police car approximately 200 yards from the water tower. As a result, several key questions remain difficult to answer:
