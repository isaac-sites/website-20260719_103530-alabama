## Introduction

The Anthony Hill incident is one of the most unusual reports from [Tennessee]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-tennessees-ufo/' | relative_url }})'s 1973 UFO wave because it combined three elements that rarely appear together: a claimed landed object, alleged physical traces on the ground and the reported sighting of a strange humanoid figure. According to contemporary accounts, three teenage boys near the Anthony Hill community in Giles County said they watched an egg-shaped craft descend on 1 October 1973 and later found marks where it had apparently landed. They also described seeing a large, hairy figure walking stiffly near the site. Despite these striking claims, the surviving evidence is sparse, largely limited to newspaper reports and later UFO catalogues, making the case difficult to investigate today. Rather than standing as proof of an extraordinary event, Anthony Hill illustrates both why the 1973 Tennessee wave attracted attention and why many of its most dramatic reports remain impossible to verify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nashvillescene.com/news/the-great-ufo-wave-of-1973-brought-an-autumn-of-aliens-to-middle-tennessee/article_1aedcb58-7b62-512d-9dc1-77bebdecb87a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nashvillescene.com">[nashvillescene.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nashvillescene.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nashville SceneThe Great UFO Wave of 1973 brought an &#x27;Autumn of Aliens&#x27; to Middle Tennessee &#124; News &#124; nashvillescene.comOctober 31, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2013</span></span></span>

## What the teenagers said they witnessed

On the evening of 1 October 1973, three teenage boys were reportedly in the Anthony Hill area of Giles County when they claimed to see an egg-shaped object descend behind nearby trees. According to reports published in *The Pulaski Citizen* and later reproduced by UFO researchers, the boys approached the area after the object disappeared from view.

Unlike many later UFO stories, the witness descriptions do not include extended interaction with the figure or detailed communication. ## What the ground marks actually prove

The reported landing traces are often cited as supporting evidence, but the surviving documentation does not show that they were examined using any formal investigative procedure.

Contemporary and later accounts refer to marks or impressions at the alleged landing location, yet there is no surviving record of:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* measured dimensions;
* professionally documented photographs;
* soil sampling;
* laboratory analysis; or
* independent forensic examination.

</div>

Without that information, it is impossible to determine whether the marks were unusual or even directly connected with the reported sighting. Ground impressions can result from many ordinary causes, including vehicle tyres, livestock, farm equipment or natural disturbances. Because the physical evidence was neither preserved nor systematically documented, it cannot now distinguish between those possibilities.

This places Anthony Hill in a category common to many 1973 reports: a claim of physical traces exists, but the traces themselves no longer provide independently testable evidence. Investigators today therefore rely almost entirely on witness testimony rather than surviving physical data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/chronos/1973fullrep.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[nicap.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Chronology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Chronology...</span></span></span>

## Why the occupant description remains uncertain

The reported "hairy figure" has become one of the most memorable aspects of the Anthony Hill case, but it is also one of its weakest pieces of evidence.

Several factors limit confidence in the description.

First, the observation appears to have been brief and made under evening conditions, reducing the opportunity to judge size, distance and detail accurately.

Second, the descriptions remained fairly general. Accounts consistently mention a large, hairy being with a stiff walk, but provide relatively little detail about facial features, clothing or behaviour. This makes comparison with known animals, people or later humanoid reports difficult.

Third, there are no corroborating photographs, audio recordings or independent witness statements from observers outside the original group. The entire occupant narrative depends upon the recollections of the three teenagers.

It is also worth noting that "hairy humanoid" reports were not unique to Tennessee during the early 1970s. Across the United States, some UFO reports blended with regional folklore about ape-like creatures or unidentified humanoids. That broader cultural backdrop does not disprove the Anthony Hill account, but it does mean investigators must consider whether witness expectations or contemporary media narratives influenced how an ambiguous sight was interpreted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/chronos/1973fullrep.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[nicap.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Chronology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Chronology...</span></span></span>

## How investigators have viewed the case

Anthony Hill has appeared in later UFO chronologies and catalogues, including compilations by the National Investigations Committee on Aerial Phenomena (NICAP) and later reference works on 1973 humanoid reports. These publications generally preserve the original outline of the event rather than presenting new investigative findings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/chronos/1973fullrep.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Chronology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Chronology...</span></span></span>

The absence of detailed investigative records has prevented the case from reaching the status of better-documented incidents. There is no publicly available evidence of radar data, police scene documentation, extensive witness interviews or scientific examination of the reported landing site.

As a result, later writers have tended to classify Anthony Hill as an intriguing but poorly documented close-encounter report rather than a well-established UFO case. ## Why Anthony Hill still matters in Tennessee's 1973 UFO wave

Although the evidence remains limited, the Anthony Hill report occupies an important place within Tennessee's UFO history because it demonstrates how quickly the state's 1973 sightings expanded beyond distant lights in the sky.

The incident introduced several features that captured public attention:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a reported landing rather than an aerial observation;
* alleged physical traces at the scene;
* a claimed humanoid occupant; and
* local newspaper coverage that helped spread awareness of the developing wave.

</div>

At the same time, the case illustrates a recurring problem in historical UFO research. Dramatic claims can become well known even when the supporting evidence is fragmentary. In Anthony Hill's case, the original witness accounts remain the primary source of information, while the reported ground marks and humanoid sighting cannot now be independently tested.

For that reason, the incident is best understood as an unresolved historical claim rather than confirmed evidence of an extraordinary event. Within the wider story of Tennessee's 1973 UFO wave, it remains noteworthy because it shows how witness testimony, physical-trace claims and media attention combined to produce one of the state's most enduring local UFO stories, even though the available evidence has not materially improved over time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nashvillescene.com/news/the-great-ufo-wave-of-1973-brought-an-autumn-of-aliens-to-middle-tennessee/article_1aedcb58-7b62-512d-9dc1-77bebdecb87a.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nashvillescene.com">[nashvillescene.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nashvillescene.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nashville SceneThe Great UFO Wave of 1973 brought an &#x27;Autumn of Aliens&#x27; to Middle Tennessee &#124; News &#124; nashvillescene.comOctober 31, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2013</span></span></span>
