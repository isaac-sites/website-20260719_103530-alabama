## Introduction

The May 1955 F-89 radar incident remains one of the strongest documented UFO-related cases in [Minnesota]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-minnesotas-ufo/' | relative_url }}) because it involved a military interceptor, trained aircrew and airborne radar rather than a brief civilian sighting. Yet the same records that make the case noteworthy also show why it is difficult to treat as evidence of an extraordinary craft. The Duluth-based crew genuinely tracked an unknown radar target for several minutes, and Air Force specialists could not confidently identify what produced the return. At the same time, uncertainties in radar geometry, target range, altitude estimates and relative motion make the more dramatic claims about impossible speeds far less secure than later retellings often suggest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/duluth-mn-may-1955" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">duluth mn may 1955</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Duluth, Minnesota, May 1955 · govweirdMay 1, 1955 — On the night of May 13, 1955, two Air Force pilots flying...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1955</span></span></span>

## What Did Duluth's F-89 Really Track?

In the early hours of 13 May 1955, an F-89D Scorpion interceptor operating from Duluth was flying a routine mission with pilot Lieutenant Charles M. Middleton and radar observer Lieutenant Donald G. Fisher. Around 2:30 a.m., the aircraft's radar acquired a target that neither crew member could identify. According to the Project [Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book-b61ee1/' | relative_url }}) file, the radar contact remained visible long enough for the crew to attempt an interception and track it for roughly four to five minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/duluth-mn-may-1955" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">duluth mn may 1955</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Duluth, Minnesota, May 1955 · govweirdMay 1, 1955 — On the night of May 13, 1955, two Air Force pilots flying...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1955</span></span></span>

The F-89 was not a simple fighter. It had been designed as an all-weather interceptor for Cold War air defence and carried radar specifically intended to locate and engage aircraft in darkness or poor weather. A radar return on such an aircraft therefore attracted serious attention, especially during a period when unidentified tracks could potentially represent hostile aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://wiki.warthunder.com/unit/f-89d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wiki.warthunder.com">[War Thunder Wiki]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wiki.warthunder.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">War Thunder WikiF-89DThe F-89D Scorpion was designed and built to be an all-weather interceptor intended to neutralize any potential inva...</span></span></span>

What made the incident unusual was that the target appeared to behave inconsistently. As the interceptor manoeuvred, the radar contact seemed at times to change altitude, direction and relative position. In some descriptions it appeared nearly stationary; in others it seemed to move away rapidly. Those apparent changes later became the basis for claims that the object displayed extraordinary performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/duluth-mn-may-1955" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">duluth mn may 1955</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Duluth, Minnesota, May 1955 · govweirdMay 1, 1955 — On the night of May 13, 1955, two Air Force pilots flying...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1955</span></span></span>

## The Crew's Four-Minute Radar Pursuit

The key fact is that the crew reported a radar target, not a prolonged visual observation. The strength of the case lies in the instrument detection; its weakness is that the available information comes primarily from interpreting radar behaviour rather than watching a clearly visible object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/duluth-mn-may-1955" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">duluth mn may 1955</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Duluth, Minnesota, May 1955 · govweirdMay 1, 1955 — On the night of May 13, 1955, two Air Force pilots flying...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1955</span></span></span>

During the pursuit, the interceptor attempted to close on the contact. Investigators later reconstructed the encounter from crew testimony and radar information, but they lacked the sort of detailed electronic recording that modern systems routinely generate. As a result, estimates of the target's movement depended heavily on assumptions about where the object actually was relative to the aircraft at different moments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/duluth-mn-may-1955" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">duluth mn may 1955</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Duluth, Minnesota, May 1955 · govweirdMay 1, 1955 — On the night of May 13, 1955, two Air Force pilots flying...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1955</span></span></span>

This distinction matters. If range, bearing or altitude are only approximately known, then small errors can produce very large differences in calculated speed. A target interpreted as moving hundreds of miles in a short period may instead reflect uncertainty in the geometry of the intercept. That possibility became central to later assessments of the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/duluth-mn-may-1955" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">duluth mn may 1955</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Duluth, Minnesota, May 1955 · govweirdMay 1, 1955 — On the night of May 13, 1955, two Air Force pilots flying...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1955</span></span></span>

## Why the Speed and Altitude Estimates Are Uncertain

Many summaries of the Duluth incident emphasise reports that the target accelerated to remarkable speeds. The problem is that those figures were not directly measured in the way modern radar systems measure velocity. They were inferred from estimated positions during a short and complex intercept.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/duluth-mn-may-1955" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">duluth mn may 1955</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Duluth, Minnesota, May 1955 · govweirdMay 1, 1955 — On the night of May 13, 1955, two Air Force pilots flying...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1955</span></span></span>

Several factors complicate the calculation:

* **Range uncertainty:** If investigators do not know the target's exact distance, they cannot determine its true speed with confidence.
* **Relative motion effects:** An interceptor turning, climbing or changing heading can create misleading impressions of a target's movement on radar.
* **Altitude ambiguity:** Errors in estimating height can alter reconstructed flight paths dramatically.
* **Radar behaviour:** Early airborne radar systems could produce returns that were difficult to interpret, particularly when tracking weak or unusual contacts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/duluth-mn-may-1955" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">duluth mn may 1955</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Duluth, Minnesota, May 1955 · govweirdMay 1, 1955 — On the night of May 13, 1955, two Air Force pilots flying...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1955</span></span></span>

Because of these uncertainties, the dramatic performance figures sometimes associated with the encounter should be treated cautiously. The records support the claim that an unidentified radar target was tracked. ## How Project Blue Book Judged the Evidence

Project Blue Book reviewed the incident and did not reach a firm identification. Rather than classifying the event as a solved case, investigators concluded that the available information was insufficient to determine exactly what had been tracked. The case therefore entered the category of unresolved reports rather than confirmed aircraft, weather phenomena or astronomical objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/duluth-mn-may-1955" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">duluth mn may 1955</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Duluth, Minnesota, May 1955 · govweirdMay 1, 1955 — On the night of May 13, 1955, two Air Force pilots flying...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1955</span></span></span>
