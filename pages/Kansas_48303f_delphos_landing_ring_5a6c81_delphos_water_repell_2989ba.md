## Introduction

The most unusual physical feature in the 1971 [Delphos]({{ 'delphos/' | relative_url }}), [Kansas]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-kansas-ufo/' | relative_url }}), landing-ring case was not the reported object but the soil itself. Investigators found that soil from the pale ring resisted wetting: water tended to bead on the surface, and some samples reportedly floated or remained difficult to soak even years later. Subsequent laboratory work did not identify an exotic substance, but it did find evidence of a coating on the soil particles that could explain the water-repellent behaviour. The key mystery is that researchers could describe the coating's properties without being able to determine with confidence how it got there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/analysis-soil-samples-related-delphos-kansas-november-2-1971/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultAnalysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas...October 13, 2016 — 13 Oct 2016 — This material coats the soil s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 13, 2016</span></span></span>

## How Hydrophobicity Was Observed

The Delphos ring became notable because its water resistance appeared to persist long after the reported encounter. Witnesses and investigators reported that water placed on the ring soil did not soak in normally. Some accounts also described the ground beneath the ring as unusually dry compared with surrounding soil. Later examinations of preserved samples found that the unusual behaviour could still be demonstrated years after collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/Budinger/UT001.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault DocumentsTSR UT0019 Aug 1999 — Analysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas. In soil science, this phenomenon is known as hydrophobicity or soil water repellency. It occurs when soil particles become coated with substances that repel water, causing droplets to bead rather than penetrate the surface. Such effects are real and well documented in natural environments, particularly where organic compounds create a thin water-repellent film around soil grains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/26997.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: research.fs.usda.gov">[US Forest Service R&amp;D]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">research.fs.usda.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">In chemistry, hydrophobic substances have no polarity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">US Forest Service R&amp;DWater Repellent Soils: a state-of-the-artApril 10, 2007 — by LF DeBano · 1981 · Cited by 963 — the mineral soil part...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 10, 2007</span></span></span>

## What the Chemical Coating Contained

The most important laboratory finding came from later analyses of preserved Delphos samples. Researchers concluded that the hydrophobic behaviour was caused by a water-soluble organic coating attached to the soil particles. When water was added and the sample was agitated, the coating dissolved and the soil largely lost its apparent water repellency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/Budinger/UT001.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault DocumentsTSR UT0019 Aug 1999 — Analysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas. The material was identified as a humic substance, most likely fulvic acid. Fulvic acid is a naturally occurring component of soil organic matter produced by biological activity and the decomposition of plant material. Investigators noted that the extracted organic material was present in significantly greater amounts in the ring samples than in nearby control samples.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/analysis-soil-samples-related-delphos-kansas-november-2-1971/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultAnalysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas...October 13, 2016 — 13 Oct 2016 — This material coats the soil s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 13, 2016</span></span></span>

This finding is important because it shifted the discussion away from ideas about metallic residues, radiation products or unknown industrial chemicals. The laboratory evidence pointed instead toward an organic coating associated with ordinary soil processes, even though the concentration and distribution within the ring remained unusual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/analysis-soil-samples-related-delphos-kansas-november-2-1971/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultAnalysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas...October 13, 2016 — 13 Oct 2016 — This material coats the soil s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 13, 2016</span></span></span>

## Why the Source Remains Unknown

The chemical analyses answered one question—how the soil repelled water—but not another: where the coating came from.

Fulvic acid and related humic compounds are common in soils. They can originate from decomposing vegetation, microbial activity and other natural biological processes. Soil scientists have long known that organic coatings can produce water-repellent conditions under certain circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://research.fs.usda.gov/download/treesearch/26997.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: research.fs.usda.gov">[usda.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">research.fs.usda.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">In chemistry, hydrophobic substances have no polarity</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">US Forest Service R&amp;DWater Repellent Soils: a state-of-the-artApril 10, 2007 — by LF DeBano · 1981 · Cited by 963 — the mineral soil part...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 10, 2007</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that no investigation established a direct mechanism linking the reported aerial object to the deposition of the coating. Laboratory tests identified the material responsible for the hydrophobicity, but they did not identify an external source or any process unique to a spacecraft landing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/analysis-soil-samples-related-delphos-kansas-november-2-1971/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultAnalysis of Soil Samples Related to the Delphos, Kansas...October 13, 2016 — 13 Oct 2016 — This material coats the soil s...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 13, 2016</span></span></span>

Several natural explanations have been proposed over the years. One suggestion is that biological activity in the soil created an unusually concentrated organic layer. Another is that a fungal or "fairy ring" process may have contributed to the circular pattern and water repellency. However, no explanation was conclusively demonstrated using samples collected from the site at the time, and no later study was able to reconstruct exactly how the ring formed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theconspiratory.com/theory/delphos-ufo-ring" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theconspiratory.com">[The Conspiratory]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theconspiratory.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The ConspiratoryThe 1971 Delphos, Kansas soil ring is physical proof that an...2 days ago — A fairy ring is a plausible source of hydrop...</span></span></span>

A further complication is that the site was not preserved under strict scientific controls immediately after the event.
