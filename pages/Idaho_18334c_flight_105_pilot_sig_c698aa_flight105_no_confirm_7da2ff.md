## Introduction

One of the most important limitations of the United Airlines Flight 105 sighting is that nobody outside the aircraft independently confirmed what the crew reported. On 4 July 1947, Captain Emil J. Smith, First Officer Ralph Stevens and flight attendant Marty Morrow all stated that they observed groups of unusual objects ahead of their DC-3 after departing Boise, [Idaho]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-idahos-ufo/' | relative_url }}). Yet despite efforts to obtain outside verification, no passengers, ground observers, air-traffic personnel or nearby pilots reported seeing the same objects at the same time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

This absence of corroboration does not prove that the crew imagined the sighting. It does, however, place important limits on what historians and investigators can conclude. Flight 105 remains notable because multiple trained aviation witnesses agreed that they saw something unusual. At the same time, the lack of independent confirmation means the case rests almost entirely on testimony from a single aircraft rather than on evidence from multiple locations or sensors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

## The Radio Calls and Attempted Checks

The crew did not simply keep the sighting to themselves. Contemporary accounts state that Smith and Stevens attempted to determine whether anyone else could see the objects. They radioed the Civil Aeronautics Administration facility at Ontario, Oregon, and also contacted another United Airlines aircraft operating in the region. Neither reported observing the objects described by Flight 105.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

This detail is often overlooked because later retellings focus on the pilots' observations rather than on the failed verification attempts. Yet from an evidential standpoint, the unsuccessful checks are significant. If the objects had been plainly visible over a wide area, investigators would normally expect at least some possibility of corroboration from another aircraft or a ground observer looking in the same direction. No such supporting report emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

The absence of confirmation also meant that investigators lacked a second viewing angle. Without observations from another location, there was no reliable way to determine the objects' distance, altitude or true speed. Those missing measurements became one of the central weaknesses of the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

## Why Passengers May Not Have Seen the Objects

The fact that the eight passengers aboard the DC-3 reportedly saw nothing unusual is sometimes presented as evidence against the sighting. The situation was more complicated than that. According to Captain Smith, the objects appeared mainly ahead of the aircraft rather than beside it. Cabin passengers seated behind the cockpit had a much poorer viewing angle toward that part of the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

Several practical factors may have reduced the chance of passenger observations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The sighting occurred during evening twilight, when contrast and visibility can change rapidly.
* Cockpit crews had a forward-facing view that passengers did not share.
* Passengers were not continuously scanning the sky for potential air traffic.
* Aircraft windows in 1947 offered more restricted viewing angles than the cockpit windscreen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

No passenger publicly reported seeing the objects, but neither did passengers publish detailed statements explaining that they had carefully searched the same area of sky and found nothing. ## Why Ground Witnesses Never Appeared

Another striking feature of the Flight 105 case is the absence of known ground witnesses in Idaho or eastern Oregon.

Several factors may explain this. The objects were reportedly observed from a moving aircraft at altitude and were visible for only about ten to fifteen minutes. The viewing geometry may have been highly specific to the crew's position. An object appearing prominent against the horizon from one location can be difficult or impossible to notice from another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

There is also the problem of timing. The sighting took place during the intense flying-disc wave of summer 1947, when hundreds of reports were appearing across the United States. Newspapers were collecting large numbers of claims, but not every unusual observation generated enough attention to produce matching reports from the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/1947-year-flying-saucer" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: airandspace.si.edu">[National Air and Space Museum]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">airandspace.si.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National Air and Space Museum1947: Year of the Flying Saucer24 Jun 2022 — Black and white comic strip depicting UFOs flying over the Whit...</span></span></span>

The result is a historical gap. Researchers can identify the crew's testimony, but they cannot point to a corresponding report from a police officer, farmer, airfield employee or civilian observer who independently recorded the same event at the same moment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

## What Single-Platform Testimony Can and Cannot Prove

Flight 105 is sometimes described as a “multiple-witness” case, and that description is technically correct. Three crew members reported seeing unusual objects. However, all three witnesses were on the same aircraft and therefore shared nearly identical viewing conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because independent confirmation normally comes from observers who are separated from one another. Multiple locations allow investigators to compare angles, estimate distances and check whether descriptions remain consistent. None of those opportunities existed in the Flight 105 incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kirkmcd.princeton.edu/JEMcDonald/mcdonald_hcsa_68.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kirkmcd.princeton.edu">[kirkmcd.princeton.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kirkmcd.princeton.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">STATEMENT ON UNIDENTIFIED FLYING OBJECTS...In interviewing UFO witnesses, it is important to try to ascertain whether the witness was, p...</span></span></span>

**What the testimony supports**
