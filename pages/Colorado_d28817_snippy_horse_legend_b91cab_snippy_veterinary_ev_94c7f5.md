## Introduction

The most important question in the [Snippy case]({{ 'snippy-case/' | relative_url }}) is not whether the carcass looked strange, but whether veterinary evidence supports the claim that the horse was deliberately and surgically mutilated. More than half a century later, the strongest evidence points in the opposite direction. Investigators who examined the remains found signs consistent with disease, possible gunshot injury, decomposition and scavenger activity rather than precision removal of tissue. The features that made the case famous—clean bone, little visible blood and apparently selective removal of flesh—are all phenomena that can occur naturally once a carcass is exposed on open range land. ## The Overlooked Bullet Wounds and Infection

One of the least-publicised aspects of the case emerged after the initial wave of sensational reporting. Local veterinarian Dr Wallace Leary obtained the carcass and, while preparing the skeleton, reportedly discovered two small-calibre bullet wounds in the mare's right flank. Investigators also concluded that the horse had suffered from a serious infection before death. These findings offered a conventional explanation for how the animal may have died, independent of any UFO claim.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[Denver Public Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos and horse called snippy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Denver Public LibraryUFOs and a Horse Called Snippy20 Oct 2020 — On September 7, 1967, Snippy was a mare. The significance of the bullet wounds is often misunderstood. They do not automatically prove that the horse was intentionally killed. A gunshot wound can lead to infection, tissue damage and eventual death if untreated. Veterinary literature documents severe infections and cellulitis developing from gunshot injuries in horses, demonstrating that seemingly small wounds can become life-threatening.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2216437/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">PMCManagement of a gunshot wound in a mare</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIHby MA Mellish · 2008 · Cited by 5 — A 5-year-old, Quarter horse mare was treated for severe cellulitis secondary to a gunshot wo...</span></span></span>

The University of [Colorado]({{ 'what-colorados-ufo-history-really/' | relative_url }})'s UFO-related investigation, conducted as part of the wider Condon Committee effort, ultimately found no evidence that the horse's death involved abnormal causes. Later summaries of the investigation noted evidence of infection and possible injury that could account for the animal's condition before decomposition began.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutilation_of_%22Snippy%22_the_horse" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[wikipedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mutilation of &quot;Snippy&quot; the horse</span></span></span>

This matters because many retellings start with the mutilated appearance of the carcass and work backwards. Veterinary investigators instead worked forwards from the animal's physical condition and found ordinary biological processes capable of explaining the evidence.

## How Scavengers and Drying Alter a Carcass

The feature that shocked witnesses was the horse's head and neck. Flesh appeared to be missing while the exposed bone looked unusually clean and pale. To observers unfamiliar with decomposition, this can resemble deliberate skinning or surgical removal.

In reality, the head is often among the first areas altered by scavengers. Birds and small predators naturally target soft tissues around the eyes, mouth, nose and throat because these regions are easier to access than heavily muscled parts of the body. Once skin is breached, additional tissue can be removed rapidly. Veterinary and forensic taphonomy—the study of what happens to remains after death—shows that scavenging can create wounds and tissue loss that are easily mistaken for cuts made before death. A key indicator is the absence of bleeding into surrounding tissue, because post-mortem scavenging occurs after circulation has stopped.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9027482/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Uncovering Forensic Taphonomic Agents: Animal Scavenging...by L Indra · 2022 · Cited by 48 — No haemorrhaging at a wound indicates a...</span></span></span>

The environment of Colorado's [San Luis Valley]({{ 'san-luis-valley/' | relative_url }}) likely amplified these effects. Dry air, sun exposure and wind can quickly desiccate exposed tissue. As moisture leaves the carcass, skin shrinks and tightens, edges become firm, and bones can appear unusually clean. What looks like a precise incision may actually be the combined result of drying, tearing and scavenger feeding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9027482/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Uncovering Forensic Taphonomic Agents: Animal Scavenging...by L Indra · 2022 · Cited by 48 — No haemorrhaging at a wound indicates a...</span></span></span>

Some investigators suggested that a wound to the neck may have provided an entry point for birds and other scavengers. Once access was available, natural feeding activity could account for much of the tissue loss that later appeared mysterious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://gazette.com/2022/05/22/ufo-legend-horse-found-dead-and-mutilated-55-years-ago-in-colorado-gets-new-life-at-roadside-attraction-130b0e6c-ca4a-11ec-af09-ffeb7d7ab0f4/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gazette.com">[Gazette]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gazette.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO legend: Horse found dead and mutilated 55 years ago...22 May 2022 — There was, according to the report, evidence of infection...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 2022</span></span></span>

## Why Bloodless and Surgical Descriptions Mislead

The two most repeated claims in the [Snippy]({{ 'snippy/' | relative_url }}) story are that the carcass was bloodless and that the missing tissue looked surgically removed. Both descriptions sound dramatic, but neither is strong forensic evidence on its own.

A carcass discovered days after death will not necessarily be surrounded by obvious pools of blood. Blood settles internally after circulation stops, is absorbed into soil, dries, decomposes or is consumed by insects and scavengers. By the time the horse was examined, decomposition was already underway. The absence of visible blood therefore does not demonstrate that blood was artificially removed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.denverlibrary.org/news/western-history/ufos-and-horse-called-snippy" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.denverlibrary.org">[Denver Public Library]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.denverlibrary.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos and horse called snippy</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Denver Public LibraryUFOs and a Horse Called Snippy20 Oct 2020 — On September 7, 1967, Snippy was a mare. The actual cause of death was a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 7, 1967</span></span></span>

Likewise, “surgical” appearance is often a subjective judgement. Natural tearing frequently follows anatomical boundaries, producing edges that appear surprisingly neat. Drying can further sharpen these boundaries. Investigators examining later livestock mutilation claims repeatedly noted that scavenger activity can create tissue loss that non-specialists interpret as knife work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9027482/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PMC]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Uncovering Forensic Taphonomic Agents: Animal Scavenging...by L Indra · 2022 · Cited by 48 — No haemorrhaging at a wound indicates a...</span></span></span>

Another frequently cited mystery involved the allegedly empty skull cavity. Yet decomposition provides a straightforward explanation. Soft brain tissue liquefies rapidly after death. If openings exist in the skull or surrounding tissue, fluids can drain, evaporate or be consumed by scavengers long before investigators arrive. The same principle applies to other supposedly missing organs. Once predators, insects and bacteria gain access to a carcass, internal tissues are among the first materials to be altered or removed. What appears selective can simply reflect which tissues are easiest to reach.
