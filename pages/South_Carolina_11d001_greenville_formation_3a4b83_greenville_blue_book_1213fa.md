## Introduction

Project Blue Book did not classify the 13 May 1952 [Greenville]({{ 'greenville/' | relative_url }}), [South Carolina]({{ 'south-carolina/' | relative_url }}), formation sighting as an unexplained mystery because it had exhausted every possible explanation. Instead, the Air Force reached a more cautious conclusion: the strongest conventional explanation—birds reflecting artificial light—could not be demonstrated with enough confidence to account for every reported detail. As a result, the case remained officially listed as **Unknown** rather than being forced into an identification that investigators considered incomplete. This distinction matters because Blue Book's "Unknown" category was intended for reports that resisted a satisfactory explanation after investigation, not as proof that something extraordinary had occurred. The Greenville case illustrates how the Air Force sometimes preferred uncertainty over an explanation it regarded as only partially convincing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AProject_Blue_Book%2C_BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf - Wikimedia CommonsDecember 31, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 1952</span></span></span>

## What the Air Force accepted and rejected

The Greenville report placed investigators in an unusual position. They found parts of the witnesses' account entirely consistent with an ordinary cause while finding other parts difficult to reconcile with that same explanation.

The report accepted several points:

* One of the four observers believed the objects could have been geese or other birds.
* A flock flying in a diamond-like formation could account for the reported group arrangement.
* The apparent wobbling motion was compatible with the changing wingbeats of birds.
* The very brief duration of the sighting—around three seconds—left little opportunity for careful observation or precise judgement of distance and speed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AProject_Blue_Book%2C_BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf - Wikimedia CommonsDecember 31, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 1952</span></span></span>

However, investigators were unwilling to accept the bird explanation without reservation because the reported illumination raised a significant problem. The witnesses described four distinctly luminous reddish objects visible against the night sky. If they were birds, the Air Force had to explain why they appeared so bright.

Rather than dismissing this difficulty, Blue Book explicitly addressed it. Investigators suggested that artificial lighting from Greenville could have reflected from the undersides of birds, making them appear self-luminous. Yet they stopped short of saying this had definitely happened. The report acknowledged that the available information did not allow that mechanism to be demonstrated convincingly enough to identify the sighting with confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AProject_Blue_Book%2C_BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf - Wikimedia CommonsDecember 31, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 1952</span></span></span>

## The dispute over reflected city light

The central disagreement in the investigation was not whether birds could fly in formation. It was whether birds could have appeared exactly as reported under the lighting conditions that night.

Blue Book noted that there was haze and urban illumination over Greenville, creating at least a theoretical opportunity for reflected light. At the same time, the observers had deliberately chosen a darker observing site away from local lighting because they were conducting astronomical observations. The report therefore recognised two competing facts:

* sufficient city illumination may have existed to light birds at altitude;
* the observers themselves were positioned in relatively dark conditions with exceptionally clear skies overhead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AProject_Blue_Book%2C_BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf - Wikimedia CommonsDecember 31, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 1952</span></span></span>

That combination made the reflection hypothesis plausible without making it certain.

The witnesses themselves also disagreed. According to the Air Force summary, only one observer favoured the bird explanation, while the remaining three did not accept it. Blue Book neither dismissed the minority opinion nor treated the majority view as decisive. Instead, it evaluated the physical plausibility of both positions and concluded that neither completely resolved the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AProject_Blue_Book%2C_BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf - Wikimedia CommonsDecember 31, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 1952</span></span></span>

This is an important feature of the Greenville investigation. Rather than choosing between witness confidence and a preferred conventional explanation, the report preserved both as part of the official record.

## What the "Unknown" label actually meant

Modern discussions sometimes interpret Blue Book's "Unknown" classification as implying that investigators believed an object displayed extraordinary or non-human characteristics. The Greenville file demonstrates that this is not how the category functioned.

Project Blue Book generally classified reports after attempting to match them with known causes such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--phrase-pills" markdown="1">

* aircraft;
* balloons;
* astronomical objects;
* weather phenomena;
* birds or other natural explanations.

</div>

Only when no explanation satisfactorily accounted for the available evidence did a report remain in the Unknown category. An "Unknown" therefore meant that investigators considered the evidence insufficient for a confident identification—not that they had ruled out ordinary explanations altogether.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on archives.gov.</span></span></span>

Greenville fits this definition closely. The Air Force believed birds deserved serious consideration, yet considered the explanation incomplete because it depended on an illumination mechanism that could not be verified from the available evidence. Rather than overstate its confidence, Blue Book retained the unresolved classification.

## Why the case remained balanced rather than sensational

The Greenville sighting is sometimes cited as evidence that Blue Book secretly recognised extraordinary aerial objects. The surviving documentation supports a more restrained interpretation.

Several aspects strengthened the report:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* four independent witnesses observed the same formation;
* the observers were experienced amateur astronomers already watching the sky;
* investigators considered and rejected a simple aircraft explanation because no sound accompanied the passage overhead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AProject_Blue_Book%2C_BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf - Wikimedia CommonsDecember 31, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 1952</span></span></span>

</div>

At the same time, several limitations prevented firmer conclusions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the observation lasted only a few seconds;
* there were no photographs or instrumental measurements;
* no radar confirmation was available;
* distance, altitude and speed could not be established reliably.

</div>

Those weaknesses prevented investigators from proving either an extraordinary explanation or the proposed bird explanation.

## Why Greenville remains significant in South Carolina's UFO history

Within South Carolina's UFO history, Greenville is noteworthy less because it was labelled "Unknown" than because the reasoning behind that label survives in unusual detail. The Air Force documented a conventional explanation that it considered credible, explained why that explanation fell short, and resisted the temptation to classify the case more confidently than the evidence allowed.

That makes the Greenville formation a useful example of how Project Blue Book sometimes operated. An official "Unknown" did not necessarily indicate an inexplicable event. In this case it reflected an unresolved balance between a plausible natural explanation and observational details that investigators believed they could not fully account for. The result was an official classification that acknowledged uncertainty rather than overstating certainty in either direction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AProject_Blue_Book%2C_BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[wikimedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Project Blue Book, BBA-PBSR8-300.pdf - Wikimedia CommonsDecember 31, 1952...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 31, 1952</span></span></span>

    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
