## Introduction

[Hayden Hewes]({{ 'hayden-hewes/' | relative_url }})’s *Interplanetary Intelligence Report* occupies an unusual place in [Oklahoma]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-oklahomas-ufo/' | relative_url }}) UFO history. It is both a valuable historical source and a publication that openly promoted a preferred explanation for the phenomenon it covered. For researchers trying to understand Oklahoma’s major sighting years, especially the intense wave of reports during the mid-1960s, the bulletin remains useful because it preserved witness accounts, local investigations, organisational correspondence and press coverage that might otherwise have disappeared. At the same time, readers must recognise that the publication was not a neutral archive. Its editors explicitly framed UFOs as potentially interplanetary craft and saw part of their mission as demonstrating that possibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmondlifeandleisure.com/edmond-underground-in-edmond-abuzz-with-uco-sightings-p10350-87.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmondlifeandleisure.com">[edmondlifeandleisure.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmondlifeandleisure.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">in &#x27;65 Edmond abuzz with UCO sightings11 Sept 2014 — Members of the IIOUFO included scientists, astronomers and others interested in the...</span></span></span>

The result is a source that deserves neither automatic dismissal nor automatic trust. Its greatest value lies in showing what people reported, how civilian investigators responded and how UFO claims circulated through Oklahoma and beyond. Its greatest weakness is that the editors often approached those reports with a clear belief about what UFOs might be.

## What the Bulletin Collected and Circulated

Published during the mid-1960s by Hewes’s organisation, Interplanetary Intelligence of Unidentified Flying Objects (IIOUFO), the *Interplanetary Intelligence Report* functioned as an informal information network. Surviving issues show a mixture of witness narratives, local sighting summaries, newspaper extracts, organisational updates and commentary on official investigations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/718106463/Interplanetary-Intelligence-Report-vol-1-no-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Interplanetary Intelligence Report vol 1 no 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USA UFO Files: July 1965 Edition &#124; PDF &#124; Ufology28 Mar 2024 — The Interplanetary Intelligence Report (IIR), is published bi-monthly...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1965</span></span></span>

For Oklahoma historians, this role is more important than it may first appear. During the 1960s there was no comprehensive state archive devoted to UFO reports. Newspaper articles were scattered across local publications, witnesses often had no permanent record of their experiences, and police observations could remain buried in departmental files. Hewes gathered these fragments into a single publication that circulated among investigators and enthusiasts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmondlifeandleisure.com/edmond-underground-in-edmond-abuzz-with-uco-sightings-p10350-87.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmondlifeandleisure.com">[edmondlifeandleisure.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmondlifeandleisure.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">in &#x27;65 Edmond abuzz with UCO sightings11 Sept 2014 — Members of the IIOUFO included scientists, astronomers and others interested in the...</span></span></span>

Several aspects of the bulletin make it historically useful:

* It preserved reports that may not survive elsewhere.
* It documented how witnesses described events before later retellings altered details.
* It recorded the reactions of local investigators during active sighting periods.

* It provides evidence of how civilian UFO networks operated before the internet era.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmondlifeandleisure.com/edmond-underground-in-edmond-abuzz-with-uco-sightings-p10350-87.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmondlifeandleisure.com">[edmondlifeandleisure.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmondlifeandleisure.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">in &#x27;65 Edmond abuzz with UCO sightings11 Sept 2014 — Members of the IIOUFO included scientists, astronomers and others interested in the...</span></span></span>

## How the Interplanetary Viewpoint Shaped Coverage

The main limitation of the bulletin is not hidden. It was built into the organisation’s identity.

The very name *Interplanetary Intelligence of Unidentified Flying Objects* reflected an assumption that UFOs might represent visitors from elsewhere in the solar system or beyond. Surviving descriptions of the bulletin state that one of its purposes was to explore and publicise the possibility that UFOs were interplanetary vehicles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/718106463/Interplanetary-Intelligence-Report-vol-1-no-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Interplanetary Intelligence Report vol 1 no 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USA UFO Files: July 1965 Edition &#124; PDF &#124; Ufology28 Mar 2024 — The Interplanetary Intelligence Report (IIR), is published bi-monthly...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1965</span></span></span>

This matters because editorial assumptions influence what gets published and how it is interpreted.

A publication operating from an interplanetary framework is more likely to:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Highlight reports that appear extraordinary.
* Give greater attention to witnesses who support unusual interpretations.
* Treat unresolved cases as suggestive evidence rather than unanswered questions.
* Emphasise perceived weaknesses in official explanations.
* Present clusters of sightings as part of a larger pattern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/718106463/Interplanetary-Intelligence-Report-vol-1-no-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Interplanetary Intelligence Report vol 1 no 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USA UFO Files: July 1965 Edition &#124; PDF &#124; Ufology28 Mar 2024 — The Interplanetary Intelligence Report (IIR), is published bi-monthly...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1965</span></span></span>

</div>

That does not mean reports were fabricated. Rather, it means the selection and framing process was not neutral. A witness statement reproduced in the bulletin may accurately reflect what someone claimed to see, while the surrounding commentary may encourage readers toward a particular conclusion.

The distinction is important. Historical researchers often separate the raw testimony from the interpretation attached to it. In Hewes’s publication, those two layers frequently appear together.

## Where Bias Becomes Most Visible

The clearest signs of bias appear when the bulletin discussed competing explanations.

During the 1960s, many UFO reports generated disagreements between civilian investigators and official programmes such as the US Air Force’s Project Blue Book. Civilian groups often believed government agencies were too quick to identify sightings as stars, planets, aircraft or atmospheric phenomena. Government investigators, meanwhile, argued that many dramatic reports had ordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Blue_Book" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book</span></span></span>

Hewes’s publication generally aligned with the civilian side of that debate. The bulletin's audience expected serious consideration of UFO reports and was often sceptical of official dismissals. As a result, readers can encounter a recurring pattern in which official explanations receive critical treatment while unexplained elements receive greater emphasis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/718106463/Interplanetary-Intelligence-Report-vol-1-no-2" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Interplanetary Intelligence Report vol 1 no 2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USA UFO Files: July 1965 Edition &#124; PDF &#124; Ufology28 Mar 2024 — The Interplanetary Intelligence Report (IIR), is published bi-monthly...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1965</span></span></span>

For historians, this does not invalidate the source. It simply means that the publication should be read alongside newspaper accounts, police records, Project Blue Book files and later analyses rather than in isolation.

## Using the Publication as History Without Treating It as Proof

The most reliable way to use the *Interplanetary Intelligence Report* is as a historical document rather than a final authority on what witnesses actually saw.

The bulletin can answer questions such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* What reports were circulating in Oklahoma at a particular time?
* Which cases attracted the attention of civilian investigators?

Despite its limitations, the *Interplanetary Intelligence Report* remains one of the most significant surviving civilian UFO records connected to Oklahoma.

Without it, many local reports would exist only as scattered memories, isolated newspaper clippings or lost correspondence. Hewes helped preserve a body of material that allows later researchers to reconstruct how Oklahoma’s UFO culture developed during one of the most active periods in American UFO history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://edmondlifeandleisure.com/edmond-underground-in-edmond-abuzz-with-uco-sightings-p10350-87.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: edmondlifeandleisure.com">[edmondlifeandleisure.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">edmondlifeandleisure.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">in &#x27;65 Edmond abuzz with UCO sightings11 Sept 2014 — Members of the IIOUFO included scientists, astronomers and others interested in the...</span></span></span>
