## Introduction

The Memphis police UFO case is one of [Tennessee]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-tennessees-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s more persuasive eyewitness incidents, but not one of its best-documented physical cases. At about 3.30 am on 17 May 1977, Memphis police officers Lamar Todd and Jerry Jeter reportedly watched a large, triangular object hovering near Pine Hill Golf Course in South Memphis. Todd later said that three other Memphis officers reported a similar object from elsewhere in the city, apparently giving the event several official witnesses rather than a single observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.memphisflyer.com/world-ufo-day/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memphisflyer.com">[Memphis Flyer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memphisflyer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">world ufo day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Memphis FlyerWorld UFO DayNovember 29, 1 — 2 Jul 2015 — It wasn&#x27;t your typical Tuesday morning for Memphis police officers Lamar Todd and...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Tennessee_1f4d4c_memphis_police_ufo_c_bba69d-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Memphis Case" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That corroboration is why the case still matters. Separate police observations reduce the likelihood that Todd and Jeter simply invented the episode or experienced an entirely private illusion. They do not, however, establish the object’s size, distance, speed or origin. No publicly available photograph, radar plot, recording, preserved police report or detailed set of independent witness statements has emerged to test the most extraordinary parts of the story. The fairest assessment is therefore that the officers probably saw something genuinely puzzling, but that the surviving evidence is too incomplete to determine what it was.

## What officers reported near Pine Hill Park

Todd and Jeter were working in an unmarked surveillance van in the early hours of 17 May. According to Todd’s later account, Jeter first noticed something above power lines as they travelled near the Norris Road junction. Todd did not immediately see it, so they continued north, turned around at South Parkway and approached again from the opposite direction. Both then reportedly saw the object above the power lines leading towards Pine Hill Golf Course and Pine Hill Park.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pbs.org/video/ufos-over-memphis-ibgvha/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pbs.org">[PBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pbs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Where to Watch UFOs Over MemphisLamar Todd is a retired Memphis police officer. The officers left the van near an exit ramp and watched for roughly four or five minutes. Todd described a triangular form with three lights along its sides. He said it appeared to be hovering close to the power lines and made no detectable engine, wind or propulsion noise. Published retellings have compared its apparent size with a football field, although this was an unaided estimate made at night without a confirmed distance or altitude.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.memphisflyer.com/world-ufo-day/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memphisflyer.com">[Memphis Flyer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memphisflyer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">world ufo day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Memphis FlyerWorld UFO DayNovember 29, 1 — 2 Jul 2015 — It wasn&#x27;t your typical Tuesday morning for Memphis police officers Lamar Todd and...</span></span></span>

Jeter, who was reportedly assigned as a police sniper, returned to the van for a rifle fitted with a telescopic sight. Todd said that as the rifle was brought out, the object began moving north and then disappeared across the horizon with startling speed. Later popular versions sometimes imply that it reacted directly to the weapon, but the observation does not demonstrate cause and effect. The movement may simply have coincided with Jeter retrieving the rifle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.memphisflyer.com/world-ufo-day/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memphisflyer.com">[Memphis Flyer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memphisflyer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">world ufo day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Memphis FlyerWorld UFO DayNovember 29, 1 — 2 Jul 2015 — It wasn&#x27;t your typical Tuesday morning for Memphis police officers Lamar Todd and...</span></span></span>

Todd then radioed the police dispatcher and asked for a check with airport ground control. In his later telling, the answer was that no unidentified traffic had been detected in the area. He also recalled being told that three officers associated with a north Memphis precinct had reported a similar object. Additional reports attributed to state troopers in Brownsville and Alamo have appeared in the later narrative, but the surviving public accounts provide little detail about those observations or whether they described the same object at compatible times and bearings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pbs.org/video/ufos-over-memphis-ibgvha/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pbs.org">[PBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pbs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Where to Watch UFOs Over MemphisLamar Todd is a retired Memphis police officer. This difference matters. Five Memphis police witnesses are repeatedly mentioned in reputable local coverage and in the 2015 WKNO documentary, whereas the more distant state-police reports are less clearly documented. It is therefore safest to treat the Memphis corroboration as the core of the case and the outlying reports as claims requiring further archival confirmation.

## Why multiple witnesses strengthen the account

Police officers are not automatically more accurate than other observers, but their testimony can be valuable. They are accustomed to reporting times, places, directions, vehicles and unusual activity. Todd and Jeter also had a prolonged observation rather than a fleeting glimpse, and they reportedly changed position before stopping to watch. Those features make the encounter more substantial than a report based on a lone point of light seen for a few seconds.

The strongest element is the claim that other officers reported a similarly shaped object from separate locations. Independent witnesses can help test whether an observation was caused by a problem specific to one person, such as fatigue, eyesight or momentary confusion. Reports from different parts of Memphis might also have allowed investigators to estimate a flight path or altitude through triangulation, had exact locations, times and viewing directions been preserved.

Several qualifications limit that apparent corroboration:

* **The degree of independence is uncertain.** It is unclear whether the other officers described the object before hearing Todd’s radio call or after the dispatcher had circulated information about its appearance.
* **Their full accounts are not readily available.** The public narrative is dominated by Todd’s later testimony. The names, sketches and verbatim statements of all five officers have not been presented together in an accessible primary file.
* **“The same description” is imprecise.** A comparison requires more than agreement that something was triangular or carried several lights. Investigators would need bearings, elevation angles, duration, direction of travel and the exact wording used by each witness.
* **Night-time scale estimates are fragile.** Without a known distance, a small nearby object and a much larger distant one can produce the same apparent size. Claims that the object was as large as a football field or crossed 14 to 16 miles in about a second depend on assumptions that were not instrumentally verified.

Todd’s credibility is helped by the consistency of his central narrative decades later: the location, the turn at South Parkway, the triangular appearance, the lights, the silence and Jeter’s attempt to use the scope recur in local accounts and his filmed interview.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.memphisflyer.com/world-ufo-day" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memphisflyer.com">[memphisflyer.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memphisflyer.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Memphis Flyer World UFO Day</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The National Enquirer listed the Memphis sighting among the best UFO stories of 1977, which, being the year of Star Wars and rampant 1950...</span></span></span> Yet consistency over time does not guarantee accuracy. Repeatedly recounting a vivid memory can preserve its main structure while estimates and interpretations become more definite.

## How the case was promoted and investigated

The incident attracted national attention through the *National Enquirer*, which operated an annual award scheme for UFO evidence. In June 1978, the paper reportedly awarded $7,500 to five Memphis police officers for what its panel considered the most scientifically valuable UFO evidence of 1977. Todd and Jeter received the largest share.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://isaackoi.com/ufog/best-ufo-cases/10-consensus-lists-national-enquirer-panel/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: isaackoi.com">[Isaac Koi Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">isaackoi.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Isaac Koi Archive10</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Consensus lists: National Enquirer Panel &#124; Isaac Koi ArchiveOn 13 June 1978, the National Enquirer reported (see Footnote 10.41) that a...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 1978</span></span></span>

The award shows that the account impressed a group assembled to judge reported UFO cases. It is not equivalent to a government finding or peer-reviewed scientific conclusion. The *National Enquirer* was a mass-market tabloid with a strong interest in dramatic UFO stories, and payment introduces a possible incentive even when witnesses are sincere. The award should therefore be treated as part of the case’s media history, not as independent proof of an extraordinary craft.

The timing also affected official scrutiny. The US Air Force had ended Project Blue Book, its formal UFO investigation programme, in 1969. By 1977 there was no routine federal system comparable to Blue Book that would automatically collect Memphis police statements, obtain radar tapes and publish an official conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">As a result of these investigations and studies and experience gained from investigating UFO reports since 1948, the conclusions of Proje...</span></span></span> The case instead passed largely through newspapers, private UFO investigators and later television reporting.

## What ordinary explanations can and cannot cover

No convincing, case-specific conventional explanation has been demonstrated in the accessible record. A bright planet would not readily account for an apparently structured triangular form that several officers reportedly saw from different positions. A meteor would be too brief to hover for several minutes. A normal aeroplane could show several lights, but Todd’s description of stationary hovering, silence and extreme departure speed does not resemble an ordinary aircraft as he perceived it.

That does not leave an exotic vehicle as the only alternative. Several ordinary mechanisms remain possible because the essential measurements are absent.

A distant aircraft seen nearly head-on can appear stationary until its direction changes. Several aircraft lights can create the impression of a single dark triangular body, especially against an unlit sky. Atmospheric conditions can alter the apparent brightness and position of lights. Helicopters, balloons, advertising devices or misjudged objects closer to the observers must also remain within the range of possibilities unless contemporary flight, weather and equipment records exclude them.

The reported acceleration is particularly difficult to assess. If witnesses misjudged the object’s distance, they would also misjudge its size and speed. A light that simply went out, turned away or became obscured could appear to have crossed the horizon almost instantaneously. Todd’s account clearly conveys what the departure looked like to him, but it does not provide the measured trajectory needed to demonstrate extraordinary performance.

The airport enquiry offers limited help. Todd recalled that ground control had no unidentified target, but this is not the same as confirming that radar covered the relevant altitude and direction or that no aircraft was present. No radar image, controller statement or communications recording has been published alongside the account. A negative verbal check therefore neither confirms a solid object nor rules out all conventional traffic.

## What evidence is still missing

The case would be much stronger if its underlying records could be assembled. The most important missing items are the original Memphis Police Department incident or communications report, the dispatcher’s radio log, statements from each of the five officers, airport radar or controller records, and contemporary weather and aviation data. A map showing each witness position and viewing direction could establish whether the reports genuinely converged on one location.

Modern UAP investigations emphasise precisely this type of supporting material. NASA has stated that observations are extremely difficult to verify without extensive data, while the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office asks for original imagery, sensor information and detailed metadata rather than relying only on narrative recollection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/general/nasa-announces-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-study-team-members/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Announces Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Study Team Members</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Announces Unidentified Aerial Phenomena Study Team Members</span></span></span> The Memphis case illustrates why: several credible people may honestly describe an impressive event, yet analysts still cannot calculate its altitude, dimensions or velocity.

There is also no known physical trace associated with the Pine Hill observation. Nothing was recovered, and no photograph, film, confirmed electromagnetic effect or environmental change has been tied to the object. ## How strong was the Memphis police UFO case?

As witness testimony, the Memphis case is stronger than most isolated UFO reports. Two officers apparently watched the object together at close apparent range, and three further officers reportedly described something similar from other parts of the city. Todd openly identified himself, gave a detailed account and continued to stand by it long after leaving the police. The encounter consequently deserves a place in Tennessee’s UFO history alongside the state’s better-known official and aviation-related reports.

As scientific evidence, however, it is much weaker. The story survives mainly through retrospective interviews, local journalism and UFO literature rather than a complete contemporary case file. The witnesses’ estimated size and speed cannot be checked, the independence of the additional reports is uncertain, and the claimed airport enquiry is unsupported by preserved radar data. Later reporting kept the account alive but did not materially strengthen its evidential foundation.

The most defensible conclusion is that Todd, Jeter and probably other officers witnessed an aerial display they could not identify. Their testimony makes casual dismissal inappropriate, but it does not establish a gigantic craft, impossible acceleration or a non-human origin. The Memphis police case remains unresolved because too little testable information survives, not because ordinary explanations have been conclusively eliminated.

    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
