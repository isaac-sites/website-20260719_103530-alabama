## Introduction

The link between Mothman and the Silver Bridge collapse is one of the best-known examples of how [folklore]({{ 'folklore/' | relative_url }}) can reshape real events. Reports of a large winged creature around Point Pleasant, [West Virginia]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-west-virginias/' | relative_url }}), began in November 1966 and continued intermittently for about a year. The Silver Bridge then collapsed on 15 December 1967, killing 46 people. Although the timing encouraged later speculation, there was no contemporary evidence that witnesses originally regarded Mothman as a supernatural warning about the disaster. That connection emerged mainly through later books, media retellings and popular culture, while official investigations found an entirely engineering-based explanation for the bridge's failure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/80267.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntsb.gov">[NTSB]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntsb.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">15, 1967...</span></span></span>

## What happened before the bridge collapse?

The main wave of [Mothman]({{ 'mothman/' | relative_url }}) reports began in the Point Pleasant area in November 1966. Witnesses described a large, grey or dark winged figure with glowing red eyes, often seen near the abandoned West Virginia Ordnance Works, commonly known as the TNT area. Local newspapers reported these claims cautiously, reflecting uncertainty over exactly what people had seen.

For the following thirteen months, scattered reports continued. They included sightings of unusual lights, strange animals and other unexplained experiences, but they did not form a single agreed narrative. Accounts varied considerably, and many incidents had no obvious connection with the Silver Bridge itself. Some sightings occurred miles away from the bridge, while others involved roads, farms or the former military site rather than the town centre.

When the Silver Bridge collapsed during the evening rush hour on 15 December 1967, the tragedy naturally overshadowed the earlier sightings. Forty-six people died when the bridge suddenly failed under heavy traffic, making it one of the deadliest bridge disasters in modern American history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/80267.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntsb.gov">[NTSB]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntsb.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">15, 1967...</span></span></span>

## How writers created the omen connection

The idea that Mothman had predicted the bridge disaster was developed largely after the collapse rather than before it.

The most influential figure was journalist and paranormal writer John Keel. His 1975 book *The Mothman Prophecies* brought together witness reports, UFO stories, mysterious phone calls, alleged psychic experiences and the bridge collapse into a single narrative suggesting that strange phenomena may precede major disasters. Rather than presenting isolated incidents, the book encouraged readers to interpret the year's events as parts of one unfolding mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.southernliving.com/culture/the-mothman-legend-west-virginia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southernliving.com">[Southern Living]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southernliving.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It was first sighted by gravediggers in Clendenin in November 1966, followed by reports from local couples who claimed the creature, with...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1966</span></span></span>

Several factors helped strengthen this interpretation:

* **The timing appeared meaningful.** The creature reports largely stopped after the bridge collapse, making it easy to view the sightings as leading towards a single dramatic event.
* **Human psychology favours patterns.** After a tragedy, people often look backwards for warnings or overlooked signs that might make a shocking event seem more understandable.
* **The story gained a clear ending.** Instead of a series of unrelated sightings with no resolution, the bridge collapse provided a powerful narrative climax.
* **Later adaptations amplified the connection.** The 2002 film *The Mothman Prophecies* introduced the omen interpretation to a much wider audience, reinforcing the association for people unfamiliar with the original newspaper reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.southernliving.com/culture/the-mothman-legend-west-virginia" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: southernliving.com">[Southern Living]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">southernliving.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">It was first sighted by gravediggers in Clendenin in November 1966, followed by reports from local couples who claimed the creature, with...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1966</span></span></span>

Importantly, Keel's interpretation was not presented as an engineering explanation for the collapse. Instead, it suggested that unexplained phenomena might accompany or foreshadow disasters. That distinction was often blurred in later retellings, where Mothman increasingly became known simply as a "harbinger of doom".

## Where folklore conflicts with engineering evidence

The strongest conflict between folklore and documented evidence concerns the cause of the bridge collapse.

The official investigation concluded that the disaster began with the brittle fracture of a single steel eyebar in the suspension chain. A tiny flaw grew over decades through stress corrosion and corrosion fatigue until the component failed. Because the bridge lacked structural redundancy, failure of that one member caused the suspension chain to separate, leading to the rapid collapse of the entire structure. Investigators found no evidence supporting paranormal causes or warnings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/80267.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntsb.gov">[ntsb.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntsb.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">15, 1967...</span></span></span>

The investigation also had lasting practical consequences. It prompted major changes in bridge inspection and maintenance across the United States, leading to the National Bridge Inspection Standards and a more systematic approach to identifying structural weaknesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ntsb.gov/Advocacy/SafetyIssues/Pages/Bridge-Collapse-and-Other-Infrastructure-Failures.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntsb.gov">[NTSB]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntsb.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bridge Collapses and Other Infrastructure Failures</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bridge Collapses and Other Infrastructure Failures...</span></span></span>

This creates two very different ways of understanding the same historical moment:

* **The engineering account** explains why the bridge failed through materials science, design limitations and structural mechanics.
* **The folklore account** explains why many people came to associate the disaster with earlier mysterious sightings through narrative, memory and cultural interpretation.

These explanations address different questions. One concerns physical causation; the other concerns how communities make sense of traumatic events.

## Why the omen story became more memorable than the original sightings

The original Mothman reports were inconsistent. Witnesses disagreed about the creature's size, shape, behaviour and even whether it flew. On their own, these accounts resemble many local monster reports that briefly attract attention before fading.

Linking them to a nationally reported disaster transformed their cultural significance. Instead of asking whether witnesses saw an unusual bird, an owl, a crane or something genuinely unexplained, later audiences increasingly focused on whether the creature had been a warning.

That shift changed Mothman from a local curiosity into a widely recognised symbol. The legend became associated not simply with Point Pleasant but with the broader idea that mysterious figures may appear before catastrophes. As the story spread through books, documentaries, films and tourism, the bridge collapse became inseparable from the public image of Mothman, even though the association rests primarily on retrospective interpretation rather than contemporary evidence or the findings of the official bridge investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/80267.aspx" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ntsb.gov">[NTSB]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ntsb.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">15, 1967...</span></span></span>

      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
