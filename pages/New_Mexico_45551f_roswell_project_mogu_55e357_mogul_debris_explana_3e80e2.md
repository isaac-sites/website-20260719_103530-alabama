## Introduction

The strongest evidence suggests that the debris recovered near [Roswell]({{ 'roswell/' | relative_url }}) in [New Mexico]({{ 'why-new-mexico-became-americas-ufo/' | relative_url }}) in 1947 came from Project Mogul, a highly secret American balloon programme designed to detect distant Soviet nuclear tests. The key question is not whether balloon material could resemble the reported wreckage—it clearly could—but whether the specific debris field found on the Foster ranch can be linked to a particular Mogul launch. Most official investigations concluded that it could, and that the materials described by witnesses closely match components used in Mogul balloon trains. ## What Project Mogul Balloon Trains Carried

Project Mogul was not a simple weather-balloon operation. Conducted from New Mexico in 1947, it used long trains of balloons carrying experimental equipment intended to detect low-frequency sound waves from Soviet nuclear explosions. ## How the Debris Descriptions Match Balloon Equipment

The most persuasive aspect of the Mogul explanation is the similarity between contemporary descriptions of the debris and known balloon-train materials.

When ranch foreman Mac Brazel described what he found, he spoke of lightweight fragments including rubber, foil-like material, paper and thin sticks. Early military and FBI communications likewise referred to balloon-related equipment and radar-reflector components. None of the surviving 1947 descriptions mention engines, heavy structural metal, wiring systems, propulsion equipment or other features one would expect from an advanced aircraft or spacecraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[fas.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on fas.org.</span></span></span>

## Why the Flight 4 Identification Remains Uncertain

The main weakness in the Mogul explanation is not the type of material involved but the attempt to connect the debris to a specific launch.

The Air Force investigation argued that the debris most likely came from Mogul Flight 4, launched from Alamogordo on 4 June 1947. According to that reconstruction, the flight travelled north-eastward and was lost, making it a plausible source for debris later discovered on the Foster ranch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[muller.lbl.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project MogulBased on the above, it appeared likely that the debris found by the rancher and was subsequently identified as a &quot;flying dis...</span></span></span>

Critics of the official explanation point to records suggesting that Flight 4 may not have been launched as originally planned because of weather or technical issues.
