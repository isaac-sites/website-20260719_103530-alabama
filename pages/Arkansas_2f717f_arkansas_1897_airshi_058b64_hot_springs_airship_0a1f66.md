## Introduction

The Hot Springs airship story is one of the strongest and weakest accounts from [Arkansas]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-arkansas-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s 1897 airship wave at the same time. It is strong because two identified law officers—Constable John J. Sumpter Jr. and Deputy Sheriff John McLemore—publicly claimed they approached a landed airship near Hot Springs and later swore affidavits describing what they had seen. It is weak because the case depends almost entirely on their narrative. No physical evidence was recovered, no independent witnesses confirmed the reported conversation with the crew, and the surviving versions of the story read more like a polished newspaper anecdote than a field report. As a result, the case remains an intriguing episode in Arkansas UFO history but not a verified encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/ufo-sightings-8576/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopediaofarkansas.net">[Encyclopedia of Arkansas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopediaofarkansas.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo sightings 8576</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Encyclopedia of ArkansasUFO Sightings22 Oct 2025 — Perhaps the most prominent of the 1897 sightings in Arkansas occurred in Hot Springs (...</span></span></span>

## The Officers' Account of the Landed Craft

According to contemporary accounts, Sumpter and McLemore were riding outside Hot Springs on the rainy evening of 7 May 1897 when they noticed an unusually bright light. Following it, they reportedly found a cigar-shaped craft on the ground in the Ouachita Mountains. The object was described as roughly sixty feet long and illuminated by powerful lights. Their horses allegedly became unwilling to approach further, forcing the officers to continue on foot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/ufo-sightings-8576/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopediaofarkansas.net">[encyclopediaofarkansas.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopediaofarkansas.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo sightings 8576</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Encyclopedia of ArkansasUFO Sightings22 Oct 2025 — Perhaps the most prominent of the 1897 sightings in Arkansas occurred in Hot Springs (...</span></span></span>

## What the Affidavits Do and Do Not Prove

Supporters of the Hot Springs story have long pointed to one feature that distinguishes it from many other airship reports: the officers reportedly swore affidavits affirming their account. Because Sumpter and McLemore held law-enforcement positions, later writers frequently treated this as evidence that the story deserved serious consideration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arkansasstatearchives.blogspot.com/2015/08/ufos-and-arkansas.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arkansasstatearchives.blogspot.com">[arkansasstatearchives.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arkansasstatearchives.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arkansas History&#x27;s Mysteries - UFOs and Arkansas25 Aug 2015 — Perhaps the most prominent of the sightings in Arkansas came on May 7, 1897...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 7, 1897</span></span></span>

However, an affidavit proves only that a person formally stated something under oath. It does not independently verify that the event happened as described. Courts and historians routinely distinguish between the existence of a sworn statement and proof of the statement's contents.

Several limitations remain:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The affidavits do not provide physical evidence of an airship.
* No photographs, sketches made at the scene, or recovered materials are known.
* No additional witnesses are known to have confirmed the reported conversation.
* The original event cannot be reconstructed independently from surviving evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/ufo-sightings-8576/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopediaofarkansas.net">[Encyclopedia of Arkansas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopediaofarkansas.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo sightings 8576</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Encyclopedia of ArkansasUFO Sightings22 Oct 2025 — Perhaps the most prominent of the 1897 sightings in Arkansas occurred in Hot Springs (...</span></span></span>

</div>

This distinction is important when assessing credibility. The affidavits strengthen the historical reality of the claim—the officers genuinely said they experienced something—but they do not establish that an advanced aircraft actually landed near Hot Springs in May 1897.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arkansasstatearchives.blogspot.com/2015/08/ufos-and-arkansas.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arkansasstatearchives.blogspot.com">[arkansasstatearchives.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arkansasstatearchives.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Arkansas History&#x27;s Mysteries - UFOs and Arkansas25 Aug 2015 — Perhaps the most prominent of the sightings in Arkansas came on May 7, 1897...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 7, 1897</span></span></span>

## Why the Story Reads Like a Polished Anecdote

One reason historians and sceptical researchers treat the case cautiously is the style of the narrative itself. The encounter contains many features common in late nineteenth-century airship stories.

The crewman is unusually cooperative. Rather than fleeing or hiding, he chats with the witnesses and offers them a tour. The conversation is neatly structured, memorable and easily retold. Such details make the story entertaining, but they also resemble newspaper storytelling conventions of the era.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readex.com/blog/ufo-fever-americas-historical-newspapers-mysterious-airships" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readex.com">[readex.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readex.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo fever americas historical newspapers mysterious airships</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Mysterious Airships of 1896-9712 Sept 2014 — For five months beginning in November 1896, newspapers across the country described stra...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1896</span></span></span>

The Hot Springs account also appeared during a period when newspapers were competing intensely for readers. Airship reports frequently blended eyewitness testimony, speculation, humour and outright invention. Across the United States, newspapers published stories of mysterious inventors, secret flying machines and chance meetings with airship crews. Some reports were later exposed as jokes or fabrications, while others remain impossible to verify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.readex.com/blog/ufo-fever-americas-historical-newspapers-mysterious-airships" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: readex.com">[readex.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">readex.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo fever americas historical newspapers mysterious airships</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Mysterious Airships of 1896-9712 Sept 2014 — For five months beginning in November 1896, newspapers across the country described stra...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1896</span></span></span>

Several aspects of the Hot Springs narrative therefore raise caution flags:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The dialogue is unusually polished for a supposedly spontaneous encounter.
