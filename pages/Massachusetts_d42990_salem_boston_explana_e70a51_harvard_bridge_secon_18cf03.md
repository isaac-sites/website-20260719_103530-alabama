## Introduction

The strongest challenge to the conventional explanation for Boston’s September 1964 UFO reports came from just two witnesses. Most of the evening’s sightings were eventually linked to a small aircraft towing an illuminated political advertising banner along the Charles River. Yet two observers insisted that they saw both the advertising aircraft and a separate unidentified light at the same time. For readers examining the broader Boston sightings discussed elsewhere in this [Massachusetts]({{ 'which-massachusetts-ufo-stories-still/' | relative_url }}) UFO history project, the importance of the “second light” claim is straightforward. If the witnesses were correct, the aircraft explanation accounted for only part of what people saw that night. If they were mistaken, then the case becomes a useful example of how multiple lights, changing perspectives and imperfect observation can create a more complex mystery than actually existed.

## Did Witnesses See a Second Object Over Boston?

Air Force investigators examining the Boston reports concluded that many sightings matched the movements of a plane towing a brightly illuminated political advertisement. The explanation fit the timing, location and appearance reported by numerous witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/news/nation/2015/02/06/air-force-voluminous-ufo-files-illuminate-bygone-era-new-england/5ZDe8dsNhQunIt13jLwedI/story.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bostonglobe.com">[BostonGlobe.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bostonglobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Boston Globe.com UF O ﬁles shed light on a bygone era in New England</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">on Sept. 9, 1964, and the two were among 11 Bostonians who reported UFO sightings that night. Air Force investigators...</span></span></span>

The difficulty arose because a small number of observers described something more than the advertising aircraft. According to later summaries of the case and surviving investigative records, two witnesses reported seeing the known aircraft while simultaneously observing another object that appeared distinct from it. Those accounts prevented investigators from treating every report as a simple misidentification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universalhub.com/2024/year-martians-took-interest-charles-river" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universalhub.com">[Universal Hub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universalhub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universal HubThe year the Martians took a couple of spins around the Hancock...But then there were two reports from people who had spott...</span></span></span>

Unlike many UFO disputes, the issue was not whether an aircraft was present. Investigators already knew that it was. The question was whether witnesses had accurately perceived a second object moving independently in the same part of the sky.

## Warsher's Account from the Harvard Bridge

The most frequently discussed witness was James Louis Warsher, described in contemporary reporting as an MIT engineering student. He and a companion reported observing an unusual object while travelling across the Harvard Bridge over the Charles River. Their account became central because it was detailed and because they maintained that what they saw could not be reduced to the advertising aircraft alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universalhub.com/2024/year-martians-took-interest-charles-river" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universalhub.com">[Universal Hub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universalhub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universal HubThe year the Martians took a couple of spins around the Hancock...But then there were two reports from people who had spott...</span></span></span>

According to the Air Force file, Warsher described an object approaching over the river toward the bridge. He reported that traffic slowed or stopped while people watched. The object was said to pass over the bridge, manoeuvre sharply, move toward the illuminated beacon atop the John Hancock Building and execute abrupt turns before changing direction again. The report emphasised movements that the witness believed were inconsistent with a conventional aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/75/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1964-09-9421388-Boston-Massachusetts.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span></span></span>

What made the testimony especially important was Warsher’s insistence that he also saw the banner-towing aircraft. If the aircraft and the unidentified light were visible together, then one could not simply be a mistaken identification of the other. That claim became the key point of contention in later discussions of the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universalhub.com/2024/year-martians-took-interest-charles-river" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universalhub.com">[Universal Hub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universalhub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universal HubThe year the Martians took a couple of spins around the Hancock...But then there were two reports from people who had spott...</span></span></span>

## Why Investigators Considered the Witness Credible

The significance of the Harvard Bridge account does not rest on extraordinary evidence but on the witness's apparent sincerity and observational confidence.

Several factors encouraged investigators and later researchers to take the report seriously:

* Warsher was not describing a distant point of light glimpsed for a moment; he claimed a prolonged observation with identifiable landmarks nearby.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/75/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1964-09-9421388-Boston-Massachusetts.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span></span></span>
* His background as an engineering student gave the account an appearance of technical competence, even though expertise in engineering does not guarantee accurate aerial observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universalhub.com/2024/year-martians-took-interest-charles-river" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universalhub.com">[Universal Hub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universalhub.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universal HubThe year the Martians took a couple of spins around the Hancock...But then there were two reports from people who had spott...</span></span></span>
* Investigators directly questioned him about possible conventional explanations, including searchlight effects, indicating that they viewed the report as worth examining rather than dismissing immediately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/75/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1964-09-9421388-Boston-Massachusetts.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span></span></span>

At the same time, credibility should not be confused with correctness. Many well-documented UFO cases involve honest witnesses who accurately report what they believed they saw while still being mistaken about the object's nature, distance or motion. The Air Force investigation itself treated Warsher as a genuine witness while continuing to explore ordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/75/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1964-09-9421388-Boston-Massachusetts.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span></span></span>

## Why the Second-Light Claim Remained Unresolved

The surviving records show that investigators specifically considered whether the reported object might have been a searchlight or spotlight projected onto low clouds. Boston had scattered cloud cover that evening, and moving light projections can sometimes create the illusion of abrupt turns or unusual speed. Investigators raised this possibility directly with Warsher.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/75/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1964-09-9421388-Boston-Massachusetts.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span></span></span>

The problem is that neither side could conclusively prove its case.

## What Evidence Is Still Missing?

The Harvard Bridge testimony survives largely through witness statements and investigative summaries. The crucial evidence that might have settled the matter never emerged.

Missing pieces include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* Independent photographs showing both the aircraft and the alleged second object.
* Radar data confirming a separate target.
* Multiple detailed witness statements from different locations describing the same independent object.
* Precise reconstruction of all aircraft, searchlight and advertising operations in the area at the exact time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/7/75/Project_Blue_Book_report_-_1964-09-9421388-Boston-Massachusetts.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: upload.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">upload.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book report 1964 09 9421388 Boston Massachusetts</span></span></span>
