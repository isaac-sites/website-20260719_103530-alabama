## Introduction

[Vermont]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-vermonts-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s Project Blue Book files prove that residents, aircrew and military personnel reported unusual objects, and that the US Air Force sometimes investigated those reports through aircraft checks, weather enquiries, radar logs and witness forms. They do **not** prove that extraterrestrial craft visited the state. The surviving records range from likely aircraft reflections and meteors to balloon sightings, incomplete reports and one notably stronger aircrew case officially left unidentified.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Vermont_835aef_vermont_blue_book_re_b1ccfa-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Blue Book" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

A current state index links eight principal Vermont case files dating from 1950 to 1962. Other Vermont-tagged entries appear through the archive’s cross-references, partly because locations, nearby installations and reporting offices were not indexed consistently. The most useful conclusion is therefore not a dramatic case count, but a pattern: official recording was broad, investigation quality varied, and the label attached to a case depended heavily on how much information reached Blue Book.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/state/vermont" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book UFO Sightings in Vermont (1947 to 1969) · govweird · govweird...</span></span></span>

## Where Vermont reports were recorded

The Vermont material forms part of a much larger federal archive. The National Archives holds Project Blue Book’s chronological case files, administrative papers and Office of Special Investigations records. Researchers normally encounter the textual collection through 94 rolls of microfilm, with individual sightings indexed chiefly by date and location. The Air Force recorded 12,618 reports nationally between 1947 and 1969, of which 701 were ultimately categorised as unidentified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

## How the cases were classified and explained

Blue Book broadly sorted reports into **identified**, **insufficient data** and **unidentified**. Identified cases could be attributed to aircraft, balloons, astronomical objects, meteors, satellites, reflections, searchlights, false radar returns and other conventional causes. “Insufficient data” meant that investigators lacked enough reliable information to reach a sound conclusion. “Unidentified” meant that the available case could not be satisfactorily matched to a conventional explanation; it did not mean that the Air Force had established an alien origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-PREX28-PURL-gpo223327/pdf/GOVPUB-PREX28-PURL-gpo223327.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB PREX28 PURL gpo223327</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1...</span></span></span>

Vermont’s files illustrate all three outcomes.

### Ordinary objects could look extraordinary

The Montpelier report of 22 March 1950 began with a high-altitude aircraft producing several vapour trails. A civilian witness then noticed two small white or silver objects apparently travelling with it. Investigators established that a modified B-29 was conducting a test flight in the area and classified the smaller objects as probable reflections associated with the aircraft. No photographs, measured speed or physical evidence supported a more unusual interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/montpelier-vt-march-1950" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">montpelier vt march 1950</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Montpelier, Vermont, March 1950 · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: march 1950</span></span></span>

Four days later, a witness at Adamant reported a crescent-shaped object resembling a DC-3, travelling at an estimated 300 miles per hour and 10,000 feet. The record judged it a possible aircraft because its described behaviour was consistent with one and the witness supplied no evidence that ruled aircraft out. The file did not record the observation’s duration and contained no photographs or physical traces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/adamant-vt-march-1950" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">adamant vt march 1950</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Adamant, Vermont, March 1950 · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: march 1950</span></span></span>

The Concord report of 29 November 1962 was more straightforward. A bright yellow-blue object with a long trail crossed the sky for about ten seconds and appeared to burn out before reaching the ground. Blue Book identified it as a meteor. Its inclusion in the UFO archive therefore records the initial uncertainty of the witness, not a lasting mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/concord-vt-november-1962" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">concord vt november 1962</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">concord vt november 1962</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: november 1962</span></span></span>

The August 1960 [North Concord]({{ 'north-concord/' | relative_url }}) Air Force Station sighting was also classified astronomically. Military witnesses described a circular object with a blue tip and white trail at dawn. Although one officer wondered whether it might be a manufactured object with a contrail, Blue Book linked it with a contemporaneous sighting from Maine and concluded that it was probably a meteor whose apparent size and behaviour had been overestimated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/north-concord-afs-vt-august-1960" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">north concord afs vt august 1960</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: North Concord AFS, Vermont, August 1960 · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: august 1960</span></span></span>

### Balloon checks could involve real investigation

The May 1956 Waterville and Morrisville reports provide the clearest Vermont example of Blue Book gathering information from several official sources. Members of the Ground Observer Corps—a civilian aircraft-warning network—reported groups of illuminated objects, some visible through binoculars for extended periods. Descriptions included triangular forms, lights resembling car headlamps and what appeared to be small structures hanging below the objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/waterville-morrisville-vt-may-1956" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">waterville morrisville vt may 1956</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Waterville &amp; Morrisville, Vermont, May 1956 · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1956</span></span></span>

Investigators checked Burlington Airport, the local weather station and the 764th Aircraft Control and Warning Squadron. They found no corresponding aircraft or radar track, but learned that high-altitude balloon work was taking place in the region. Blue Book concluded that clusters of research balloons associated with Operation Skyhook, including instrument packages suspended beneath them, could explain both the lights and the reported “gondolas”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/waterville-morrisville-vt-may-1956" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">waterville morrisville vt may 1956</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Waterville &amp; Morrisville, Vermont, May 1956 · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1956</span></span></span>

The case is useful because the explanation was not merely “probably a balloon” written on a card without supporting work. It emerged from enquiries into winds, balloon activities, aircraft movements and radar logs. The absence of a radar return did not automatically make the objects extraordinary: balloons could be difficult to detect, and the visual descriptions were broadly compatible with sunlit or illuminated balloon equipment.

### Missing information often decided the result

The Rutland case of 20 September 1959 shows what “insufficient data” meant in practice. A woman and her husband saw a large, round, silver object cross the sky from east to west over roughly two minutes. The witness thought it turned or rotated in small arcs, and investigators found no known aircraft traffic matching the observation. Yet the report lacked useful estimates of speed, altitude and distance. Blue Book requested more information but recorded that no answer was received, leaving the case without enough evidence for either a firm identification or a meaningful unknown classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/rutland-vt-september-1959" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rutland vt september 1959</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">rutland vt september 1959</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: september 1959</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to reading Vermont’s files. A report may remain unexplained because the observations resist ordinary explanations, but it may also remain unresolved because investigators never obtained the measurements needed to test those explanations. Rutland belongs primarily to the second category.

The September 1961 North Concord file is similarly complicated. Surviving material describes an unusual [radar target]({{ 'radar-target/' | relative_url }}) with relatively low apparent speed and an erratic or hovering course, while associated visual information refers to a round or spinning object changing between red, white, blue and green near the North Star. The record card considered a high-altitude weather balloon probable, yet the archive’s evaluation is presented as insufficient data or unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/n-concord-afs-vt-september-1961" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">n concord afs vt september 1961</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: N Concord, AFS, Vermont, September 1961 · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: september 1961</span></span></span>

The apparent contradiction is not proof that officials concealed a definite conclusion. It more likely reflects the way Blue Book files combined preliminary check-box assessments, brief summaries, incoming communications and later evaluations. A proposed explanation could appear in the paperwork without being established strongly enough to close the case cleanly.

## The strongest unresolved Vermont file

The Bellevue Hill sighting of 24 April 1952 stands apart because it was officially marked **unidentified** and involved three military aircrew members observing from a C-124 transport aircraft. At about 5 a.m., while flying at 11,000 feet, the pilot, navigator and another crew member saw three round bluish objects near the horizon. The objects appeared to keep pace with the aircraft for two or three minutes, disappeared, then reappeared on the opposite side of the aircraft’s course before fading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/bellevue-hill-vt-april-1952" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bellevue hill vt april 1952</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Bellevue Hill, Vermont, April 1952 · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: april 1952</span></span></span>

The witnesses reported clear weather, no trails and apparently rapid movement. They estimated that the objects were roughly 50 miles away and comparable in size to their own aircraft. Those distance and size estimates were necessarily uncertain because the crew had no known reference object beside the lights. Even experienced aircrew cannot reliably calculate the range or dimensions of an isolated light without radar, triangulation or another scale reference.

The investigation checked whether radar stations had detected unusual traffic. The 764th Aircraft Control and Warning Squadron at St Albans reported a period of radar jamming later that morning but no corresponding unidentified returns. The timing does not establish a connection between the jamming and the sighting, and the file provides no radar confirmation of the three objects. The aircrew also waited about 43 hours before making the report, which reduced the opportunity for immediate weather, traffic and radar reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/bellevue-hill-vt-april-1952" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bellevue hill vt april 1952</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Bellevue Hill, Vermont, April 1952 · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: april 1952</span></span></span>

Blue Book’s own record briefly considered whether conditions around sunrise might have produced the appearance but did not settle on that explanation. Bellevue Hill therefore remains the strongest Vermont Blue Book case in the narrow official sense: multiple trained witnesses, several minutes of observation and no accepted identification. Yet it still lacks photographs, instrumental tracking, physical evidence or independently measured motion. “Unidentified” is justified; “extraordinary craft” is not demonstrated.

## Why an official record is not proof of the unexplained

Project Blue Book’s authority can easily be overstated. The programme was an official Air Force investigation, but an official file is first of all evidence that a report entered a government system. It does not guarantee that the event was independently confirmed, exhaustively investigated or accurately reconstructed.

Three limitations recur across the Vermont files.

**Witness estimates were often untestable.** Apparent size was commonly expressed through comparisons such as a bowling ball, basketball, dime or grapefruit held at an unspecified distance. Such descriptions convey what an object looked like but do not establish its true dimensions. Estimates of height, distance and speed were usually not supported by triangulation.

**Follow-up varied sharply.** Waterville and Morrisville generated checks with an airport, weather station, radar unit and balloon programme. Rutland ended after a request for additional information received no answer. Montpelier and Adamant were settled largely from brief descriptions and known aircraft activity. The resulting classifications reflect the quality of the available investigation as much as the original sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/rutland-vt-september-1959" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">rutland vt september 1959</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">rutland vt september 1959</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: september 1959</span></span></span>

**The categories answered different questions.** “Identified” indicated that investigators considered a conventional cause adequate. “Insufficient data” indicated that the record could not support a dependable judgement. “Unidentified” indicated a residual case after evaluation, but did not establish what the object was. The Air Force’s final national position was that none of its investigated cases demonstrated a threat, technology beyond contemporary scientific knowledge or extraterrestrial vehicles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesProject BLUE BOOK - Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National Archives...</span></span></span>

Criticism of Blue Book remains relevant when weighing these conclusions. Later reviews have acknowledged testimony from former participants that the programme sometimes faced weak information, restricted resources and pressure to dispose of routine cases efficiently. Such criticism means that a Blue Book explanation should not be accepted merely because it was official. It does not follow, however, that rejected or poorly investigated cases become evidence of alien technology. The correct response is to examine whether the proposed explanation fits the actual observations and whether enough information survives to test it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-PREX28-PURL-gpo223327/pdf/GOVPUB-PREX28-PURL-gpo223327.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB PREX28 PURL gpo223327</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AARO Historical Record Report Volume 1...</span></span></span>

## What the Vermont files prove

Taken together, the Vermont records establish a modest but informative official history. People across the state reported objects they could not immediately identify. Some witnesses were civilians; others belonged to military aircrew, radar stations or the Ground Observer Corps. Air Force investigators sometimes compared reports with aircraft operations, meteor activity, balloon programmes, weather conditions and radar records.

Most of the surviving principal cases do not remain powerful mysteries. Montpelier was linked to aircraft reflections, Adamant to a possible aircraft, Waterville and Morrisville to research balloons, and the 1960 North Concord and 1962 Concord reports to meteors. Rutland and the 1961 North Concord case were weakened mainly by incomplete or internally ambiguous data. Bellevue Hill remains the clearest officially unidentified Vermont case, but its evidence is observational rather than physical or instrumental.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/bellevue-hill-vt-april-1952" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">bellevue hill vt april 1952</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book: Bellevue Hill, Vermont, April 1952 · govweird...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: april 1952</span></span></span>

The files therefore prove something narrower—and historically more useful—than extraterrestrial visitation. They show how Cold War authorities converted strange experiences into administrative records, how ordinary aerial and astronomical phenomena generated convincing reports, and how missing measurements could leave a case unresolved for decades. In Vermont’s UFO history, “officially recorded” is meaningful evidence that a claim was made and processed. It is not, by itself, evidence that the claim’s most extraordinary interpretation was true.
