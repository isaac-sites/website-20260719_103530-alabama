## Introduction

The [Piedmont UFO wave]({{ 'piedmont-wave/' | relative_url }}) of 1973 became [Missouri]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-missouris-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s most famous concentration of UFO reports not simply because unusual lights were reported, but because publicity dramatically expanded the number of people looking for them. Between February and April 1973, hundreds of reports reached police, newspapers and local officials in and around Piedmont. What began as a local story quickly attracted investigators, journalists and curious visitors, turning a rural Missouri community into a nationally known UFO hotspot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cityofpiedmont.com/parks-resorts-loding/page/ufo-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cityofpiedmont.com">[Piedmont, MO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cityofpiedmont.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Piedmont, MOUFO Park &#124; Piedmont, MOUFO Park... Between February and April of 1973, hundreds of reports were made to local law enforcemen...</span></span></span>

For historians of Missouri UFO reports, the key question is not only what people saw, but how media attention changed what people reported. The Piedmont episode offers a useful case study in social amplification: publicity encouraged more observers to watch the sky, generated new reports, attracted organised observation efforts and produced valuable testimony. At the same time, it increased the likelihood that ordinary aircraft, planets and distant lights would be interpreted through the lens of an ongoing UFO story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cityofpiedmont.com/parks-resorts-loding/page/ufo-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cityofpiedmont.com">[Piedmont, MO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cityofpiedmont.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Piedmont, MOUFO Park &#124; Piedmont, MOUFO Park... ## How Local Reports Became a National Story

The wave began with reports from residents of Wayne County and neighbouring areas who described unusual lights and objects in the night sky. As sightings accumulated, local newspapers and law-enforcement agencies received growing numbers of calls. The concentration of reports was large enough that the events became widely discussed throughout south-eastern Missouri.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cityofpiedmont.com/parks-resorts-loding/page/ufo-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cityofpiedmont.com">[Piedmont, MO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cityofpiedmont.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Piedmont, MOUFO Park &#124; Piedmont, MOUFO Park... Coverage soon spread beyond local media. Later accounts consistently describe the Piedmont reports as attracting national attention, bringing investigators and UFO researchers into the area. The publicity transformed a regional mystery into one of the better-known UFO episodes of the 1970s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.missourinet.com/2024/06/14/greetings-earthlings-piedmont-opens-ufo-capital-of-missouri-park/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: missourinet.com">[Missourinet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">missourinet.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">greetings earthlings piedmont opens ufo capital of missouri park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Greetings, Earthlings: Piedmont opens UFO Capital of...14 Jun 2024 — It includes a 16 foot UFO, a six foot tall alien, and pl...</span></span></span>

## Why More Skywatching Produced More Sightings

Publicity changes behaviour. Once a community becomes convinced that something unusual may be appearing in the sky, more people begin watching. In Piedmont, media coverage and word-of-mouth reports encouraged residents to spend evenings actively scanning the horizon for lights and movements they might otherwise have ignored.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cityofpiedmont.com/parks-resorts-loding/page/ufo-park" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cityofpiedmont.com">[Piedmont, MO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cityofpiedmont.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo park</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Piedmont, MOUFO Park &#124; Piedmont, MOUFO Park... Between February and April of 1973, hundreds of reports were made to local law enforcemen...</span></span></span>

This process creates a feedback loop:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. Initial sightings receive publicity.
2. More people start observing the sky.
3. More unusual lights are noticed.
4. Additional reports generate further publicity.
5. The cycle repeats.

</div>

Importantly, this does not mean witnesses were dishonest. Most reports were probably sincere observations. The issue is that increased attention greatly increases the number of ambiguous events that become reportable incidents. A bright star near the horizon, a distant aircraft approaching head-on, atmospheric distortion or an unfamiliar light can suddenly acquire significance when observers are expecting to see something extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_flap" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO flap</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO flap</span></span></span>

The Piedmont wave coincided with a broader period of elevated UFO reporting in the United States during 1973. In such environments, news stories from one location can encourage people elsewhere to reinterpret ordinary observations as part of a larger pattern. Historians of UFO “flaps” have long noted that reporting surges and media attention often rise together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_flap" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO flap</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO flap</span></span></span>

## How Publicity Improved the Evidence

Publicity did not only distort the evidence. It also created opportunities that would not otherwise have existed.

The growing attention persuaded Southeast Missouri State University physics professor Harley Rutledge to organise a sustained investigation. Rather than relying solely on second-hand stories, Rutledge assembled observers and attempted direct field observation during the period when reports were still occurring. His work later became the basis of *[Project Identification]({{ 'project-identification/' | relative_url }})*, one of the most frequently cited studies associated with the Piedmont events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sos.mo.gov/symbol/ufo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sos.mo.gov">[mo.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sos.mo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Missouri Secretary of StateState UFO Capitals of MissouriHe issued a public paper in 1973, which he later turned into a book called Proje...</span></span></span>

The publicity also attracted outside investigators. Even critics and sceptics who remained unconvinced benefited from having a larger body of witness testimony available for comparison. More observers meant more opportunities to compare descriptions, locations and times. In principle, this could help distinguish genuinely unusual reports from routine misidentifications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.professorwham.com/post/project-identification-and-harley-d-rutledge-part-1-challenges-opportunities-synchronicities" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: professorwham.com">[Professor Wham]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">professorwham.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on professorwham.com.</span></span></span>

Another advantage was preservation. Because the wave became newsworthy, reports were documented in newspapers, interviews and later publications. ## How Publicity Distorted the Evidence

The same publicity that generated useful records also introduced significant problems.

Once a sighting becomes widely known, witnesses may unconsciously influence one another. People discuss what they saw, compare descriptions and read newspaper accounts before giving their own statements. Over time, separate observations can begin to resemble one another because individuals absorb details circulating in the community. This makes it harder for investigators to determine which features originated from direct observation and which emerged through social transmission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_flap" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO flap</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO flap</span></span></span>

Publicity can also create selection effects. During a UFO wave, unusual observations are much more likely to be reported than ordinary nights with nothing noteworthy. The resulting record may exaggerate the apparent frequency of unexplained events because only the most interesting observations enter the historical archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFO_flap" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO flap</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO flap</span></span></span>

When a location becomes known as a UFO hotspot, observers are primed to interpret ambiguous stimuli through that framework. A light that might have been dismissed elsewhere becomes part of the local UFO narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://spectrumlocalnews.com/mo/st-louis/news/2023/04/07/should-missouri-designate-a-state-capital-for-ufos-" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: spectrumlocalnews.com">[Spectrum Local News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">spectrumlocalnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">should missouri designate a state capital for ufos</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">as the state&#x27;s UFO capital 50 years after hundreds of reported sightings brought attention to...</span></span></span>

This is one reason why unresolved cases from the Piedmont period should be treated carefully. A large number of reports demonstrates that many people believed they saw something unusual.
