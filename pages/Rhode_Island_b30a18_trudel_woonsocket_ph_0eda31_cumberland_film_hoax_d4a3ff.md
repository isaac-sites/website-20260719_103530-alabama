## Introduction

The 1968 Cumberland film is one of the most important reasons Harold Trudel’s earlier [Rhode Island]({{ 'what-really-defines-rhode-islands-ufo/' | relative_url }}) UFO photographs came under sustained sceptical scrutiny. Unlike the famous 1967 still photographs taken near Woonsocket, the film provided moving imagery and a fixed reference point in the landscape. When investigators examined it frame by frame, they found behaviour that appeared far more consistent with a small suspended object than with the large craft Trudel described. Rather than proving that every Trudel photograph was fabricated, the film changed the balance of probability. For many investigators, it transformed the debate from “Could the photographs show an unknown object?” to “Do Trudel’s claims fit a model-and-wire hoax?”<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

## Why the Cumberland Film Changed the Trudel Debate

By the late 1960s, Trudel had already become known for a series of daylight UFO photographs taken in northern Rhode Island. Supporters regarded those images as unusually clear. Critics countered that photographs alone could not establish an object's distance, size or reality. A nearby model can often resemble a distant craft when viewed in a still image.

The Cumberland film altered that situation. According to Trudel, he was alone in woodland near Cumberland in September 1968 when he filmed a cylindrical object using a borrowed Super 8 camera. He later estimated the object to be about 90 feet long and moving at roughly 40 to 50 miles per hour. The resulting black-and-white film lasted about 105 seconds and showed the object moving through a scene that included a tree, creating a fixed visual reference unavailable in most of his earlier photographs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

That tree became crucial. Once investigators could compare the object's movement against a stationary landmark, they could evaluate whether the motion matched Trudel’s description of a large airborne object or something much smaller and closer to the camera.

## Trudel's Claims About the 1968 Filmed Object

Trudel maintained that the film showed a substantial airborne object crossing the sky. The claim depended heavily on his estimate of the object's size and speed. If correct, the film would have represented one of the rare motion-picture records of a large unidentified craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

However, critics noted a fundamental problem that would recur throughout analysis of the footage: the size estimate was not independently verified. The film itself did not establish that the object was 90 feet long. That figure came from Trudel’s interpretation of what he believed he had seen. If the object were actually much smaller and much nearer to the camera, its apparent movement could be explained very differently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

Investigators also questioned why the cameraman never appeared to make a determined effort to move closer or obtain a clearer view. While that observation does not prove deception, it became part of the broader argument that the footage did not behave like documentation of a remarkable and nearby aerial object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

## Frame-by-Frame Signs of a Suspended Lightweight Prop

The most damaging criticism emerged from detailed frame-by-frame examination.

Observers noticed that the object appeared to bob rhythmically. Analysis of individual frames reportedly showed a regular up-and-down motion peaking about once per second. Such movement is consistent with a lightweight object being affected by wind while suspended, but much harder to reconcile with a large, fast-moving craft under controlled flight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

Investigators also highlighted the object's apparent lack of momentum. In the analysed frames, it seemed capable of stopping abruptly, shifting position rapidly and then stopping again. One analysis noted that if Trudel’s size estimates were accepted, the object would have had to make extraordinarily sudden movements inconsistent with the expected behaviour of a large airborne vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

The tree reference was equally important. The footage shows the object moving in relation to the tree, and several reviewers concluded that the relative motion suggested the object was physically close to the tree rather than far beyond it. That observation weakened the interpretation of a distant craft and strengthened the possibility of a small object positioned near the foreground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

These features led multiple investigators to converge on a similar explanation. William H. Spaulding of Ground Saucer Watch concluded that computer analysis indicated the object was very small and very close. Veteran UFO investigator Walter N. Webb stated that the movements were consistent with a small tube suspended on a thin horizontal wire and pulled by a thread. Even Donald Todd, described as a UFO enthusiast rather than a debunker, said the footage appeared hoaxed and argued that the object's movement relative to the tree showed it was nearby.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

The significance of these assessments lies not merely in their scepticism but in their source. The criticism did not come exclusively from professional debunkers. It included investigators who were generally open to the possibility of genuine UFO cases but found the film unconvincing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

## The Wire-and-Model Explanation Became More Plausible

Before the Cumberland film surfaced, critics of Trudel’s photographs largely relied on photographic principles and probability arguments. They could point out that a model suspended near the camera might imitate a distant craft, but they could not directly observe the object in motion.

The film changed that. The rhythmic bobbing, abrupt starts and stops, and apparent proximity to a tree all matched what investigators might expect from a lightweight prop suspended on a wire. Critics no longer had to rely solely on theoretical possibilities; they could point to specific behaviour visible within the footage itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

This did not constitute mathematical proof that the 1967 photographs were staged. The film and the photographs are separate pieces of evidence. Nevertheless, many researchers regarded the film as a test case for Trudel’s reliability. If one of his most important moving-image claims appeared consistent with a hoax mechanism, confidence in his earlier still photographs naturally declined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

## Missing Footage, Lost Originals and the Failed Site Reconstruction

Questions surrounding the film's provenance added further doubts.

When the footage was examined publicly in the early 1980s, the original film was reportedly unavailable. Investigators were working from a copy because Trudel said the original had gone missing. Analysts also noted that a standard Super 8 cartridge contained more film than appeared on the surviving reel. Roughly half of the expected footage was absent, and neither Trudel nor associates reportedly provided an explanation for the missing section.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

Another missed opportunity involved the filming location. When a sceptical investigator sought to revisit the site and take measurements that could help determine distances and geometry within the scene, Trudel reportedly declined to participate. Without a reconstruction of camera position, tree location and sight lines, it became far harder to test competing interpretations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

## What the Film Meant for Rhode Island's Best-Known UFO Photographs

Within Rhode Island UFO history, the Cumberland film is important not because it solved the mystery of the Trudel photographs but because it shifted the evidential burden.

The 1967 images remain visually striking, and no single analysis has demonstrated exactly how each photograph was produced. Yet the 1968 film supplied a concrete example in which motion, background references and independent review all pointed towards a mundane explanation. Investigators who might have remained undecided about the photographs found the film harder to defend.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>

As a result, the Cumberland footage became a turning point in the broader Trudel debate. For believers, it was an unfortunate and disputed piece of evidence. For sceptics, it provided the strongest indication that at least some of Trudel’s celebrated UFO imagery could be explained by a small suspended object rather than a large unknown craft. That interpretation has remained one of the central reasons why the Trudel photographs are generally treated today as a weak and heavily contested part of Rhode Island's UFO record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/1983/07/22165401/p56.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com">[centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centerforinquiry.s3.amazonaws.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on amazonaws.com.</span></span></span>
