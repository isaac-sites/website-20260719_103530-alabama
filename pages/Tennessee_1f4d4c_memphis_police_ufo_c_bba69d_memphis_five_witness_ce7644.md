## Introduction

The claim that **five Memphis police officers independently reported the same unusual object** is one of the strongest features of the 17 May 1977 [Memphis case]({{ 'memphis-case/' | relative_url }})—if the reports were genuinely independent. Multiple witnesses can reduce the chance that an event resulted from a single person's mistake or imagination. However, that strength depends on one crucial question: **did the officers describe the object before they could influence one another through police radio traffic or later discussion?**



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Tennessee_1f4d4c_memphis_police_ufo_c_bba69d_memphis_five_witness_ce7644-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Five Witnesses illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Tennessee_1f4d4c_memphis_police_ufo_c_bba69d_memphis_five_witness_ce7644-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Tennessee_1f4d4c_memphis_police_ufo_c_bba69d_memphis_five_witness_ce7644-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The surviving public evidence suggests that there is at least some basis for treating the reports as partly independent. At the same time, the documentary record is incomplete. No publicly available set of original police reports has been released, and most modern accounts rely on later interviews with Officer Lamar Todd and documentary retellings rather than contemporaneous paperwork. As a result, the case is stronger than a single-witness sighting, but weaker than a fully documented multi-observer investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pbs.org/video/ufos-over-memphis-ibgvha/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pbs.org">[PBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pbs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos over memphis ibgvha</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Over Memphis &#124; PBSMay 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 14, 2024</span></span></span>

## Did the officers describe the same object independently?

The central claim is that two groups of Memphis police officers observed a similar triangular object from different parts of the city during the same night.

According to later interviews, Lamar Todd and Jerry Jeter encountered the object near Pine Hill Golf Course around 3.30 am. After the object departed, they contacted police dispatch to ask whether airport radar had detected any unidentified aircraft. Todd later said the dispatcher informed them that officers from the North Precinct had also reported a similar object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pbs.org/video/ufos-over-memphis-ibgvha/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pbs.org">[PBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pbs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos over memphis ibgvha</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Over Memphis &#124; PBSMay 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 14, 2024</span></span></span>

The most important detail concerns **what was said over the radio before the other officers responded**.

Several published accounts state that Todd and Jeter initially reported only that they had seen an unidentified object. They allegedly **did not broadcast its triangular shape or distinctive lighting** before officers at the North Precinct came onto another radio channel and asked whether the object was triangular and carried multiple coloured lights. Todd and Jeter reportedly confirmed that description only after hearing the question. If accurate, this sequence means the second group supplied key descriptive details without first hearing them from the original witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/1977-police-encounter-triangle-ufo-memphis-tennessee/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1977 police encounter triangle ufo memphis tennessee</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1977: Police Encounter Triangle UFO, Memphis, Tennessee - ArticleDecember 26, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 26, 2012</span></span></span>

That chronology is the strongest argument that the reports were not simply copies of one another.

## What Todd, Jeter and the other officers reportedly saw

The publicly available accounts do not preserve equally detailed testimony from all five officers.

Todd and Jeter gave the most extensive descriptions. They reported a large triangular object hovering silently above power lines for several minutes before accelerating rapidly away. Their account has been repeated in interviews, local journalism and the later PBS documentary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wknofm.org/news-and-features/2015-07-01/close-encounters-of-the-memphis-kind" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wknofm.org">[wknofm.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wknofm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">WKNO-FMClose Encounters of the Memphis Kind &#124; WKNO FM</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">WKNO-FMClose Encounters of the Memphis Kind &#124; WKNO FM</span></span></span>

The three North Precinct officers are generally identified in later sources as John Birdsong, Michael Davidson and Forrest Bartlett. Their reported observation was briefer. They allegedly saw a triangular object with bright or multicoloured lights passing over or near their substation earlier in the night. Unlike Todd and Jeter, they did not become the focus of later interviews, and relatively little first-person testimony from them survives in the public record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/1977-police-encounter-triangle-ufo-memphis-tennessee/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1977 police encounter triangle ufo memphis tennessee</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1977: Police Encounter Triangle UFO, Memphis, Tennessee - ArticleDecember 26, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 26, 2012</span></span></span>

## Why timing and radio contact matter

Witness independence is not simply a matter of having several people present. Investigators instead ask whether witnesses could have influenced one another before recording their observations.

Several factors would support greater independence:

* The officers were reportedly working in different parts of Memphis rather than standing together.
* The observations appear to have occurred before direct conversation between the two groups.
* According to later accounts, the North Precinct officers volunteered the object's triangular appearance before Todd and Jeter had broadcast those details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/1977-police-encounter-triangle-ufo-memphis-tennessee/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1977 police encounter triangle ufo memphis tennessee</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1977: Police Encounter Triangle UFO, Memphis, Tennessee - ArticleDecember 26, 2012...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 26, 2012</span></span></span>

However, there are also reasons for caution.

Police radio systems allow information to spread quickly. Even if the initial description was limited, officers could have been aware that colleagues were discussing something unusual. Once an event becomes the subject of radio traffic, later recollections may also become less independent as memories are reinforced through conversation.

This does not mean contamination definitely occurred. Rather, it means investigators need precise records of exactly what was transmitted, when it was transmitted, and which officers heard each message before giving their own descriptions.

## What weakens the corroboration

The main limitation is not evidence that the officers influenced each other. Instead, it is the absence of records needed to demonstrate that they did not.

Publicly available sources do not include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* original incident reports from each officer;
* dispatcher logs showing the sequence of radio transmissions;
* recordings or transcripts of police communications;
* signed witness statements written before the officers compared accounts;
* radar or aviation records directly tied to the reported object.

</div>

Instead, much of the surviving narrative comes from retrospective interviews conducted decades after the event. While Todd's recollections have remained broadly consistent, memory naturally becomes less reliable over long periods, particularly regarding precise timing and wording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pbs.org/video/ufos-over-memphis-ibgvha/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pbs.org">[PBS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pbs.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufos over memphis ibgvha</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Over Memphis &#124; PBSMay 14, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 14, 2024</span></span></span>

Because of these gaps, historians cannot confidently reconstruct exactly how independent each report was.

## What records would be needed to test the corroboration

The Memphis case could be evaluated much more rigorously if additional archival material were located.

The most valuable evidence would include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Individual police reports** written separately by each officer before discussion with colleagues.
* **Dispatch logs** showing the exact times of incoming calls and radio exchanges.
* **Radio recordings** confirming what information was broadcast before the North Precinct officers responded.
* **Duty rosters and patrol assignments** confirming the officers' locations.
* **Airport and FAA records** documenting whether any conventional aircraft could match the reported observations.

</div>

Such material would not necessarily prove the object was extraordinary. It would, however, allow investigators to measure how much of the agreement between witnesses resulted from genuinely separate observation rather than later information sharing.
