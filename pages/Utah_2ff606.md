## Introduction

The balanced conclusion is that Utah has produced a few genuinely difficult or historically important cases, many ordinary misidentifications and a large body of testimony that cannot now be tested properly. No publicly available Utah case establishes extraterrestrial visitation. “Unresolved” usually means that the surviving information is insufficient to identify an object, not that investigators proved it was extraordinary. That distinction is essential when judging both early government files and recent reports.

## Why Utah produces so many reports

Utah offers many of the conditions in which unusual lights and distant objects are likely to be noticed. Large areas have dark skies, low population density, open horizons and limited tree cover. At the same time, observers may see military aircraft, civilian traffic, balloons, [satellites]({{ 'satellites/' | relative_url }}), meteors and experimental activity whose distance or purpose is difficult to judge.

A University of Utah-led study examined 98,724 public UFO or unidentified anomalous phenomenon reports from the contiguous United States between 2001 and 2020. It found that reports were more common where people had a clearer view of the sky and where there were more opportunities to see objects associated with airports or military installations. The western United States stood out, but the research did not claim that unusual craft were concentrated there. It showed that visibility, human activity and reporting opportunity help shape the map of sightings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-49527-x" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The dependent variable includes over 98,000 publicly reported UAP sightings in the conterminous United States during the 20-year period f...</span></span></span>

This finding is especially relevant to Utah. The state contains major aviation and defence facilities, including Hill Air Force Base and Dugway Proving Ground, as well as broad stretches of desert in which an unfamiliar aircraft can appear isolated and dramatic. Dugway was established in 1942 for military testing, initially involving chemical weapons, incendiaries and aircraft-based dissemination systems. Utah’s geography therefore creates two effects at once. People can see more of the sky, but they may have fewer reference points for judging the size, speed and range of what they see. A light that seems to accelerate may instead be changing direction, fading behind thin cloud or moving towards the observer. A silent object may be much farther away than assumed. These are not explanations for every report, but they are recurring weaknesses in unaided visual testimony.

## The Tremonton film: Utah’s landmark case

The most important incident in Utah UFO history occurred near Tremonton on 2 July 1952. Delbert C. Newhouse, a US Navy photographer travelling with his family, noticed a group of bright objects moving in the daytime sky. He retrieved a film camera and recorded part of the event on colour motion-picture film.

The footage mattered because most early UFO cases rested only on memories, sketches or written statements. Here, investigators had an original film made by an experienced military photographer. The case entered the records of Project Blue Book, the US Air Force programme that investigated UFO reports, and was later reviewed during the CIA-sponsored Robertson Panel in January 1953. The film remains preserved within the broader federal UFO record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://unwritten-record.blogs.archives.gov/2013/09/30/project-blue-book-looking-to-the-film-record/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: unwritten-record.blogs.archives.gov">[The Unwritten Record]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">unwritten-record.blogs.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Unwritten Record Project Blue Book: Spotting UFOs in the Film Record</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Unwritten RecordProject Blue Book: Spotting UFOs in the Film RecordOctober 1, 2013 — 30 Sept 2013 — Project Blue Book was actually th...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 1, 2013</span></span></span>

Military photographic analysts devoted considerable effort to studying the images. The bright points did not reveal clear wings, fuselages or other identifying features, and early technical work reportedly treated the film as evidence worthy of close attention. Newhouse said the objects looked more distinct to the eye than they did on film, but that creates an unavoidable evidential problem: the camera recorded lights or reflective forms without enough detail to establish their physical nature.

The Robertson Panel reached a sceptical assessment. It considered the Tremonton images likely to be birds reflecting sunlight and recommended comparison work involving bird flight and reflection under similar local conditions. The panel did not demonstrate the bird explanation through a conclusive reconstruction; rather, it judged it the most credible ordinary interpretation and believed further testing would probably produce a satisfactory account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/DOC_0000015458.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[cia.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">comments and suggestions of ufo panelTremonton, Utah, Sighting. That difference explains why the film is still debated. Supporters emphasise Newhouse’s professional background, the duration of the sighting and the attention given to the footage by Navy analysts. Sceptics point to the lack of visible structure, uncertain range and the familiar ability of distant birds to produce bright, apparently featureless reflections. Without reliable distance information, calculations of speed or manoeuvrability are highly speculative.

The fairest classification is **historically significant but not compelling evidence of an exotic craft**. The film strengthened the case compared with an unsupported eyewitness account, yet it did not preserve the information needed to eliminate birds or other ordinary airborne objects. Its lasting importance lies partly in showing how even apparently strong imagery can remain ambiguous when scale, distance and geometry are unknown.

## Project Blue Book and Utah’s official record

Utah sightings formed part of the national system of Air Force investigations conducted under Project Sign, Project Grudge and, most famously, Project Blue Book. Blue Book operated until 1969 with two stated purposes: assessing whether reported UFOs threatened national security and analysing the reports scientifically. Across the United States, it collected 12,618 reports, of which 701 were officially left unidentified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/104590/unidentified-flying-objects-and-air-force-project-blue-book/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[U.S. Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on af.mil.</span></span></span>

Those numbers are often misunderstood. An unidentified classification did not mean that Air Force investigators believed a craft came from another world. It generally meant that the evidence available in a particular file was insufficient for a confident conventional identification. Some cases lacked exact times, weather information, radar records or detailed interviews; others involved observations too brief or distant to resolve.

Utah reports in the files included lights, formations and objects seen near communities or military facilities. Local reporting on the release of the Blue Book archive highlighted that the state was represented by cases ranging from plausible aircraft or astronomical misidentifications to reports that remained formally unresolved. The records are valuable because they show what witnesses said and how officials responded, but the age and uneven quality of the files limit retrospective conclusions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.standard.net/news/military/2015/jan/26/files-declassified-ufo-investigations-in-utah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: standard.net">[heraldextra.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">standard.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">heraldextra.com Files declassified: UFO investigations in Utah</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">heraldextra.com Files declassified: UFO investigations in Utah</span></span></span>

The official history also reveals a tension that shaped public distrust. Intelligence officials were concerned not only with the objects but with the possibility that a surge of reports could overload defence communications or be exploited by an adversary. The Robertson Panel therefore recommended public education aimed at reducing sensational reporting and suggested monitoring civilian UFO organisations. Its members concluded that the available evidence did not show an extraterrestrial origin or a direct national-security threat.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on aaro.mil.</span></span></span>

For believers, this approach looked like institutional debunking decided in advance. For officials, it was partly an air-defence problem: large numbers of false alarms could obscure a genuine hostile aircraft or missile. Both points can be true. The government had legitimate security reasons to classify aviation activity and discourage alarm, but secrecy and dismissive public statements also helped create suspicion around places such as Dugway.

## Dugway: military secrecy without alien proof

Dugway Proving Ground is frequently described in UFO literature as Utah’s “Area 52”. Its scale, isolation and classified work make it a natural focus for speculation. Observers outside the installation have reported unusual lights, aircraft and activity over the western desert, while enthusiasts have claimed that secret programmes moved there from better-known test locations.

The verified history is substantial enough without embellishment. Dugway occupies a large restricted testing region and has conducted work involving chemical and biological defence, weapons and aerial delivery systems. Civilians cannot readily determine what every distant light or flight represents, and some military projects may remain undisclosed for legitimate security reasons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://home.army.mil/dugway/my-dugway-proving-ground" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: home.army.mil">[Army IMCOM]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">home.army.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">IMCOMMy Dugway Proving Ground</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Army IMCOMMy Dugway Proving Ground - Army GarrisonsOn February 6, 1942, Dugway Proving Ground was established, with official activation o...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 6, 1942</span></span></span>

What is missing is equally important. Public claims about recovered saucers, alien bodies, reverse-engineered propulsion or enormous underground extraterrestrial facilities have not been supported by verifiable documents, independently examined materials or testable sensor records. The fact that an installation is secret does not establish the content of every rumour attached to it.

Dugway nevertheless matters to Utah’s UFO history because classified aerospace activity can generate sincere sightings. Witnesses may accurately observe something unusual while being wrong about its origin. Secret aircraft, drones, flares, balloons and weapons trials can all appear extraordinary when seen at night or from a great distance. Secrecy also prevents immediate explanation, allowing an unidentified report to evolve into a stronger claim through repetition.

A useful distinction is therefore:

* **Credible observation:** a witness may genuinely have seen an unfamiliar object or military activity.
* **Unresolved identification:** available information may be insufficient to name it.
* **Alien interpretation:** this requires additional evidence and does not follow automatically from the first two points.

## The Uinta Basin and Skinwalker Ranch

The Uinta Basin has become Utah’s most famous modern UFO location, largely because of Skinwalker Ranch, a private property associated with reports of luminous objects, strange animals, equipment failures, injuries and other paranormal events. The ranch was investigated during the 1990s and early 2000s by the National Institute for Discovery Science, an organisation funded by businessman Robert Bigelow. It later became connected to a federally funded programme studying advanced aerospace threats, while television productions brought its claims to a much larger audience.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/story/inside-robert-bigelows-decades-long-obsession-with-ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside Robert Bigelow&#x27;s Decades-Long Obsession With UFOs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside Robert Bigelow&#x27;s Decades-Long Obsession With UFOs</span></span></span>

Skinwalker Ranch occupies an awkward place in a state UFO history. It cannot be ignored because it influenced private research, media interest and the development of later government-associated investigations. Yet much of its reputation depends on testimony, books, television episodes and accounts of events for which complete raw data are not publicly available.

There have been reports of glowing orbs and aerial objects from the ranch and surrounding basin, but no publicly released image, sample or instrument record has established a vehicle displaying demonstrably non-human technology. Years of investigation have produced stories and anomalies rather than a reproducible finding accepted through independent scientific review. Even accessible accounts sympathetic to the mystery acknowledge the absence of hard evidence establishing extraterrestrial activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.utah.com/articles/post/what-is-skinwalker-ranch-and-whats-really-going-on-there/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: utah.com">[Utah.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">utah.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on utah.com.</span></span></span>

The television era adds another difficulty. Experiments may use real instruments, but entertainment programmes select footage, compress timelines and encourage dramatic interpretations. An unexplained signal, failed device or odd light can be interesting without demonstrating a common cause. Claims involving radiation, magnetic effects or GPS anomalies require calibrated instruments, control locations, repeatable procedures and open data before they can support conclusions about the ranch itself.

Skinwalker Ranch is consequently best described as **an influential but weakly demonstrated cluster of claims**. It has strengthened Utah’s cultural association with UFOs and helped attract money and official attention to the wider subject. It has not yet strengthened the physical case for alien visitation.

## Civil aviation, city sightings and cases that collapse

Not every Utah report remains mysterious for long. In June 2007, residents and news organisations received reports of a silver, cigar-shaped object over the Salt Lake City area. The object was subsequently identified as a roughly 30-foot remote-controlled blimp that had drifted away from its operator. The episode is a useful reminder that multiple sincere witnesses and considerable media attention can gather around an object whose explanation is entirely ordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/2007/06/ufo-sightings-i" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Sightings In Utah On The Rise</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Sightings In Utah On The Rise</span></span></span>

Other reports have involved pilots or aviation radio traffic. A 2016 case near Nephi attracted attention after an amateur radio operator heard communications concerning an object and researchers obtained related material through a Freedom of Information Act request. Such cases deserve attention because pilots are trained observers and air-traffic records can preserve times and locations. However, professional experience does not automatically supply distance, size or identity, particularly during a brief night-time encounter. The surviving Nephi material did not establish an extraordinary craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/ham-radio-operator-hears-american-airlines-pilot-report-ufo-near-nephi-utah/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault Ham Radio Operator Hears American Airlines Pilot Report UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black Vault Ham Radio Operator Hears American Airlines Pilot Report UFO</span></span></span>

## What usually explains Utah sightings

Utah’s reported objects range from isolated lights to formations, cigar shapes, spheres and apparent fast-moving craft. No single explanation covers them all, but several ordinary sources repeatedly account for cases:

**Aircraft and military exercises.** Navigation lights, formation flying, refuelling operations, drones, flares and test activity can be difficult to interpret from outside restricted airspace.

**Balloons and small airships.** Their apparent stillness, slow movement or sudden change of direction with the wind can look unlike normal powered flight. The 2007 Salt Lake City blimp is a documented local example.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wired.com/2007/06/ufo-sightings-i" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wired.com">[WIRED]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wired.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFO Sightings In Utah On The Rise</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO Sightings In Utah On The Rise</span></span></span>

**Satellites and satellite formations.** Bright passes and strings of recently deployed satellites can appear as silent, coordinated lights, especially to observers who do not follow launch schedules.

**Astronomical objects.** Venus, bright stars, meteors and re-entering debris may seem to move because of cloud motion, observer movement or the lack of a stable horizon.

**Birds, insects and optical effects.** Sunlit birds remain the leading historical explanation proposed for the [Tremonton film]({{ 'tremonton-film/' | relative_url }}). Objects close to a camera can also look large and fast when their true distance is unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cia.gov/readingroom/docs/DOC_0000015458.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cia.gov">[CIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cia.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">comments and suggestions of ufo panelTremonton, Utah, Sighting. These explanations should not be applied mechanically. Calling every light a balloon is no more rigorous than calling every unexplained light an alien craft. A sound investigation asks which explanation fits the time, direction, weather, appearance and movement, and whether independent records support it.

## How strong is Utah’s UFO evidence?

Utah has an unusually rich UFO history, but richness of narrative is not the same as strength of evidence. The cases fall into three broad groups.

**Resolved or probably explained cases** include known balloons, aircraft, astronomical objects and reports for which a convincing ordinary source was later found. These are important because they show how persuasive a mistaken sighting can appear before identification.

**Unresolved but limited cases** include brief pilot reports, distant lights and old [Blue Book files]({{ 'blue-book-files-5133c7/' | relative_url }}) without enough information for a firm conclusion. They may describe real objects, but the surviving evidence does not show extraordinary performance or origin.

**Historically important disputed cases** are led by the Tremonton film. It provided better documentation than most sightings of its period and was examined by serious analysts, yet its lack of scale and detail prevented a decisive identification. The bird explanation remains plausible, while claims of advanced craft exceed what the images can prove.

Recent federal reviews have not changed that overall assessment. The Pentagon’s All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office states that it has found no verified evidence of extraterrestrial technology, while acknowledging that some reports remain unresolved and that analysis continues. Its historical review concluded that past US investigations did not establish an extraterrestrial origin for UFO reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.aaro.mil/Portals/136/PDFs/AARO_Historical_Record_Report_Vol_1_2024.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aaro.mil">[AARO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aaro.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on aaro.mil.</span></span></span>

Utah therefore matters less as proof of alien visitation than as a concentrated lesson in why UFO cases persist. Dark skies expose more objects; military secrecy obscures some explanations; striking testimony can outlive the records needed to test it; and ambiguous imagery invites competing interpretations. The state’s strongest cases justify careful study, but they also demonstrate why “unidentified” should remain a description of incomplete knowledge rather than a conclusion about what is flying.

    return encodeURIComponent(String(value || '').replace(/\s+/g, ' ').trim()).replace(/%20/g, '+');
  }
  function buildUrl(query, reference, marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket];
    if (!cfg) return '#';
    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
