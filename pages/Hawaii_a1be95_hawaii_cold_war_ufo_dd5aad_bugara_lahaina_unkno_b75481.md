## Introduction

The USS *Bugara* sighting remains one of the most frequently cited entries in [Hawaii]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-hawaiis-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s early UFO records because it was not formally explained by the US Air Force. Yet the reason it stayed unexplained is less dramatic than it is often portrayed. The surviving record confirms that submarine personnel reported an unusual object off Lahaina, Maui, in July 1952 and that investigators could not confidently identify it. What the record does not provide is enough detail to demonstrate extraordinary performance, prove a non-conventional craft, or eliminate all ordinary possibilities. The case is important within Hawaii’s UFO history precisely because it illustrates the difference between an officially “unknown” report and a confirmed mystery. The object remained unidentified largely because the evidence was limited, not because investigators established that it behaved beyond known technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Public Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesPublic Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue...December 4, 2019 — 5 Dec 2019 — Project Blue Book, from...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2019</span></span></span>

## What submarine personnel reported off Lahaina

On 16 July 1952, personnel aboard the submarine USS *Bugara* reported seeing an oval-shaped object off Lahaina on the west coast of Maui. According to later summaries of the case, the object was viewed through binoculars and was described as changing colour while moving across the sky. The sighting occurred during a period when *Bugara* was conducting operations in Hawaiian waters following its return to Pearl Harbor in June 1952.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Bugara" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">USS Bugara</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">USS Bugara</span></span></span>

What makes the report notable is not the description itself but the observers. Military personnel, especially those accustomed to maritime observation, were often regarded by Air Force investigators as more reliable witnesses than casual civilian observers. That did not guarantee accuracy, but it meant the report was treated seriously enough to enter official UFO files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Public Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesPublic Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue...December 4, 2019 — 5 Dec 2019 — Project Blue Book, from...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2019</span></span></span>

The surviving public summaries, however, are remarkably brief. No widely available case file contains detailed range estimates, radar data, photographs, precise bearings, or a lengthy witness reconstruction. The event survives mainly as a short official entry rather than as a richly documented investigation. That limitation becomes crucial when assessing why the case remained unresolved.

## What the Air Force record confirms

The strongest evidence in the Bugara case is not the object itself but the existence of an official record.

A 1952 Air Force summary of Hawaiian sightings reported that, among the state's recorded UFO reports from 1947 to 1952, the Lahaina submarine sighting was the only case left in the “unknown” category. Most other Hawaiian reports were attributed to balloons, astronomical objects, or aircraft. The Air Force therefore acknowledged that it could not confidently identify the object reported by *Bugara* personnel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fold3.com/publication/461/us-project-blue-book-ufo-investigations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fold3.com">[Fold3]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fold3.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">US, Project Blue Book - UFO Investigations, 1947-196926 Feb 2007 — This series consists of sanitized case files on sightings of unid...</span></span></span>

That designation carried a specific meaning within early Air Force UFO investigations. Projects such as Blue Book, and the programmes that preceded it, routinely classified some reports as unidentified when available information was insufficient for a reliable conclusion. An “unknown” classification did not automatically imply advanced technology, extraterrestrial origin, or even that investigators had ruled out conventional explanations. It simply meant that no explanation could be assigned with confidence from the evidence available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Public Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesPublic Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue...December 4, 2019 — 5 Dec 2019 — Project Blue Book, from...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2019</span></span></span>

The distinction is important because later retellings sometimes treat “unidentified” as equivalent to “unexplainable”. The Air Force's own procedures did not use the term that way.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Blue_Book" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book</span></span></span>

## Why the case stayed unresolved

The Bugara sighting remained unexplained for several practical reasons.

First, the reported object appears to have been observed visually rather than through multiple independent forms of evidence. There is no widely available indication that radar tracked the object, that photographs were obtained, or that physical traces existed. Without corroborating data, investigators were forced to rely almost entirely on witness descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fold3.com/publication/461/us-project-blue-book-ufo-investigations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fold3.com">[Fold3]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fold3.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">US, Project Blue Book - UFO Investigations, 1947-196926 Feb 2007 — This series consists of sanitized case files on sightings of unid...</span></span></span>

Second, the surviving information is too sparse to reconstruct the object's true distance, altitude, or speed. This is a common problem in UFO investigations. A distant light, atmospheric phenomenon, aircraft, balloon, or astronomical object can appear to move in unusual ways when viewed against a dark sky or horizon. Without accurate range information, apparent motion cannot be converted into actual motion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://docsteach.org/document/ufo-questionnaire/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: docsteach.org">[DocsTeach]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">docsteach.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">civilians made tens of thousands of reports to Project Blue Book personnel claiming to have seen a UFO. Third, colour-changing objects are not automatically evidence of unusual technology. Atmospheric effects, haze, changing viewing angles, and the scintillation that affects bright celestial objects can alter perceived colour. Even if such explanations do not perfectly fit the Bugara description, they illustrate why investigators would hesitate to draw strong conclusions from a brief visual report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Blue_Book" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book</span></span></span>

Finally, many early 1950s UFO reports suffered from incomplete documentation. Air Force investigators often received reports after the event, with limited opportunity for follow-up interviews or technical analysis. The Bugara case appears to belong to this category of sightings where too little information survived to support either a firm identification or a firm rejection of ordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/news/articles/project-blue-book-50th-anniversary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National Archives Public Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesPublic Interest in UFOs Persists 50 Years After Project Blue...December 4, 2019 — 5 Dec 2019 — Project Blue Book, from...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2019</span></span></span>

## Why “unknown” did not mean extraordinary

The lasting significance of the Bugara case lies in what it reveals about official UFO records rather than in what it proves about the object itself.

An unresolved classification can result from several different situations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The object may have had an ordinary explanation that could not be demonstrated from the available evidence.
* Witnesses may have accurately described something unusual but not provided enough information for identification.
* Key records may have been lost, abbreviated, or never collected in the first place.
* Multiple possible explanations may fit the observation equally well.

</div>

The Bugara sighting falls into this broader category of evidential uncertainty. The official record confirms a report and confirms that investigators could not identify it. It does not confirm extraordinary manoeuvres, exotic technology, or a non-human origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Blue_Book" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Blue Book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Blue Book</span></span></span>

This is why the case continues to appear in discussions of Hawaii's Cold War UFO history. It was the lone Hawaiian report in a 1952 Air Force summary that remained officially unknown, yet the surviving evidence is too limited to transform that administrative label into proof of something extraordinary.
