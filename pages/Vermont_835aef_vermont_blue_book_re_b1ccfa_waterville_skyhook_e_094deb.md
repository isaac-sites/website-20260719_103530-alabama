## Introduction

The short answer is that **Project [Blue Book]({{ 'blue-book-614925/' | relative_url }})'s Skyhook balloon explanation for the May 1956 Waterville and Morrisville sightings is plausible and remains the strongest conventional interpretation**, although it cannot be proved beyond doubt. What makes this [Vermont]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-vermonts-ufo/' | relative_url }}) case notable is not simply that witnesses reported unusual illuminated objects, but that investigators compared those reports with radar information, aircraft activity, weather conditions and records of high-altitude balloon operations before reaching a conclusion. Rather than dismissing the reports outright, the Air Force attempted to test competing explanations and ultimately judged that research balloons provided the best fit for the available evidence. The case therefore illustrates how one of Vermont's better-documented Blue Book investigations moved from an apparently mysterious sighting towards a conventional explanation while still leaving some room for uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/state/vermont" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Project Blue Book Archive, and other declassified curiosities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">govweird &#124; The Project Blue Book Archive, and other declassified curiosities...</span></span></span>

## What witnesses saw over Waterville and Morrisville

In May 1956, observers in the Waterville and Morrisville area reported bright objects that appeared unusual enough to trigger a formal Project Blue Book investigation. Unlike many one-page Blue Book records, this case generated an 11-page file, indicating that investigators gathered supporting material rather than relying solely on an initial witness statement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/state/vermont" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Project Blue Book Archive, and other declassified curiosities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">govweird &#124; The Project Blue Book Archive, and other declassified curiosities...</span></span></span>

Accounts generally described illuminated objects seen against the sky over northern Vermont. As with many Blue Book cases from the mid-1950s, the central question was not whether the witnesses genuinely saw something—they almost certainly did—but what that object actually was. The reported appearance could potentially match several possibilities, including aircraft, astronomical objects, weather balloons or specialised research balloons operating at much greater altitude.

Because the observations occurred during a period when the United States was conducting numerous scientific balloon flights, investigators treated balloons as a serious working hypothesis rather than an afterthought. High-altitude balloons could remain visible for long periods, reflect sunlight after sunset and appear stationary or slow-moving because of their extreme altitude and the observer's perspective.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sacred-texts.com/ufo/rufo/rufo13.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sacred-texts.com">[Internet Sacred Text Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sacred-texts.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sacred-texts.com.</span></span></span>

## How aircraft, radar and weather records were checked

One reason the Waterville case continues to attract interest is that Blue Book did more than record witness testimony. Investigators checked several independent sources before assigning an explanation.

These checks included:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* aircraft activity in the area to determine whether conventional flights matched the reported object;
* radar information where available;
* local and upper-atmosphere weather conditions;
* information on balloon launches and known research balloon operations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sacred-texts.com/ufo/rufo/rufo13.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sacred-texts.com">[Internet Sacred Text Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sacred-texts.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sacred-texts.com.</span></span></span>

</div>

This approach reflected Blue Book's normal investigative procedure during the 1950s. Former project personnel later explained that the programme routinely obtained information on Skyhook flights, standard weather balloons, civil and military aircraft, astronomical conditions and meteorological data before classifying reports. High-altitude balloon positions were considered especially important because classified research balloons were frequently mistaken for unidentified flying objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sacred-texts.com/ufo/rufo/rufo13.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sacred-texts.com">[Internet Sacred Text Archive]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sacred-texts.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sacred-texts.com.</span></span></span>

The emphasis on cross-checking is significant. Had radar, weather records and known balloon activity all contradicted one another, the Vermont case would have been more difficult to explain. Instead, investigators concluded that the available evidence pointed in the same general direction.

## Why the Skyhook explanation fits

The Skyhook explanation rests on several characteristics of these research balloons.

Skyhook balloons were enormous polyethylene balloons designed to carry scientific instruments into the stratosphere. Operating at very high altitudes, they could appear surprisingly bright when illuminated by the Sun long after ground observers had entered dusk or darkness. They often seemed to drift slowly or even hover because of their distance, making speed and altitude difficult to judge accurately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/mss85590/004/004.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tile.loc.gov">[Library of Congress Tile]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tile.loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">M, 11/28/94]...</span></span></span>

For the Waterville and Morrisville reports, investigators found that balloon activity, together with the supporting weather analysis, offered a reasonable explanation for the reported appearance and behaviour. Blue Book therefore linked the Vermont observations to high-altitude research balloons rather than leaving the case unidentified. Contemporary summaries of the case likewise describe the official solution as involving Skyhook-type research balloons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/bluebookdesk/pbbcases-1947-1958.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[documents.theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Rob Mercer</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Rob Mercer</span></span></span>

The explanation also fits a broader pattern within Blue Book. During the 1950s, investigators repeatedly identified high-altitude balloons as the cause of sightings that initially appeared extraordinary because of unusual lighting, altitude or apparent lack of motion. This was particularly true during years when large numbers of scientific and military balloon launches were taking place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/mss/mss85590/004/004.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tile.loc.gov">[Library of Congress Tile]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tile.loc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">M, 11/28/94]...</span></span></span>

## Where doubts remain

Although the Skyhook explanation is persuasive, it is not perfect.

The surviving records do not provide the kind of definitive evidence that would eliminate every alternative interpretation. Modern researchers cannot always reconstruct the precise position of every balloon at every moment, and witness estimates of distance, size and speed are inherently uncertain. As a result, it is impossible to demonstrate beyond all doubt that a particular balloon was the exact object seen from every observation point.

There is also a broader limitation common to Blue Book investigations. Witnesses often perceived objects differently depending on viewing angle, lighting and local atmospheric conditions. Such variations can make even an accurate conventional explanation appear incomplete when compared with the original reports.

However, these limitations do not necessarily weaken the balloon hypothesis. Instead, they reflect the practical difficulty of reconstructing fleeting aerial observations decades later. In this case, the combination of weather analysis, radar enquiries and known balloon activity provides considerably stronger support than speculation alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sacred-texts.com/ufo/rufo/rufo13.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sacred-texts.com">[sacred-texts.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sacred-texts.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on sacred-texts.com.</span></span></span>

## Why this case matters within Vermont's Blue Book history

The Waterville and Morrisville investigation illustrates an important feature of Vermont's Project Blue Book record. Rather than treating every unusual report as either unexplained or easily dismissed, investigators assembled multiple strands of evidence before reaching a conclusion.

That makes the case useful as a comparison with other Vermont Blue Book files. Some reports remained unidentified because evidence was insufficient, while others—such as the 1956 Waterville and Morrisville sighting—were eventually judged to have a credible conventional explanation after technical checks were completed. The case therefore demonstrates both the strengths and the limits of Blue Book's investigative process: witness testimony was taken seriously, but it was weighed against independent records whenever those records could be obtained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govweird.com/topics/ufo/project-blue-book/state/vermont" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govweird.com">[govweird.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govweird.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Project Blue Book Archive, and other declassified curiosities</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">govweird &#124; The Project Blue Book Archive, and other declassified curiosities...</span></span></span>

    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
