## Introduction

The blue object filmed over leeward Oahu on 29 December 2020 became [Hawaii]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-hawaiis-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s best-known modern UFO case because several people saw it, multiple phone videos circulated, witnesses called 911 and the Federal Aviation Administration was asked whether an aircraft had gone down. The strongest conclusion, however, is narrower than the headlines suggested: the recordings show an unusual blue illuminated object, but they do not establish its size, distance, speed or entry into the ocean.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Hawaii_a1be95_blue_oahu_object_202_4d4cfd-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Blue Oahu Object" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

A lightweight object carrying blue LEDs, most plausibly a kite or illuminated kite tail, fits important features of the footage without requiring an exotic aircraft. That explanation has not been confirmed by identifying its owner or recovering it, so the case cannot be called conclusively solved. Even so, later analysis weakened the original “large craft crashing into the sea” interpretation. The incident remains unidentified in the everyday sense, but the available evidence does not make it a strong case for extraordinary technology.

## What witnesses reported and recorded

The sighting began at about 8.30 pm on Tuesday, 29 December. Hawaii News Now reported that Misitina Sape recorded the blue object at 8.26 pm near Haleakala Avenue in Nanakuli. A second witness, identified in the original report as Moriah, then saw what appeared to be the same object passing Princess Kahanu Estates. She and relatives entered a car and followed it for less than three miles before stopping on Farrington Highway near a Board of Water Supply property. From there, they believed it descended into the sea.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2020/12/31/faa-notified-after-mysterious-ufo-seen-above-oahu-appeared-drop-into-ocean/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hawaiinewsnow.com">[https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hawaiinewsnow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on hawaiinewsnow.com.</span></span></span>

The videos showed a luminous blue shape that appeared elongated, curved or irregular rather than a sharply defined solid vehicle. Witnesses described it as moving quickly and making no audible noise. One estimated that it was larger than a telephone pole, although there was no reliable way to measure that size from the observation. The witnesses called 911, and police passed on a report of a possible aircraft down.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2020/12/31/faa-notified-after-mysterious-ufo-seen-above-oahu-appeared-drop-into-ocean/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hawaiinewsnow.com">[https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hawaiinewsnow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on hawaiinewsnow.com.</span></span></span>

The Federal Aviation Administration found no corresponding aviation emergency. Its spokespeople said there had been no aircraft accident in the area, no overdue or missing aircraft and no aircraft disappearing from radar. This was useful negative evidence against the fear that the light was a crashing aeroplane. It was not, however, proof that the object was extraordinary: many small, low-flying or non-aviation objects would not appear as tracked aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2020/12/31/faa-notified-after-mysterious-ufo-seen-above-oahu-appeared-drop-into-ocean/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hawaiinewsnow.com">[https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hawaiinewsnow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on hawaiinewsnow.com.</span></span></span>

Witnesses also reported seeing a second, smaller white light while police were present. According to the local account, it approached from the same general direction before passing over higher ground and disappearing from view. The white light was not documented as clearly as the blue one, and no evidence established that the two were physically connected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2020/12/31/faa-notified-after-mysterious-ufo-seen-above-oahu-appeared-drop-into-ocean/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hawaiinewsnow.com">[https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hawaiinewsnow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on hawaiinewsnow.com.</span></span></span>

The case spread rapidly because the essential ingredients were unusually compelling for a local UFO story: more than one observer, dramatic colour footage, a pursuit by car, police involvement and an apparent descent over the Pacific. National reports often hardened the witnesses’ impression into a statement that the object had “crashed” into the ocean, although the original local headline was more careful, saying that it “appeared to drop” into the water.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2020/12/31/faa-notified-after-mysterious-ufo-seen-above-oahu-appeared-drop-into-ocean/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hawaiinewsnow.com">[https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hawaiinewsnow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on hawaiinewsnow.com.</span></span></span>

## Did it really enter the ocean?

The videos do not prove that the object entered the sea. They record a bright light against a dark background, without a visible horizon, measured camera position, known object size or independent range information. Those missing reference points matter because a camera records direction, not distance. A small object a few hundred metres away and a much larger object several kilometres away can occupy the same apparent area of the frame.

The reported telephone-pole comparison is therefore an impression rather than a measurement. To calculate physical size, investigators would need at least a dependable distance or a second synchronised viewpoint with accurately known locations. Although several people reportedly saw the object, the publicly circulated material was not developed into a documented triangulation study. No published reconstruction fixed a flight path, altitude or speed.

Following the light by car did not solve the distance problem. A nearby object drifting along the coast could seem to be moving rapidly as the observers and camera moved. Conversely, a distant light near the horizon could remain visible during a drive while appearing to descend. Camera shake, digital zoom, loss of focus and motion blur further change the apparent length and movement of a bright object at night.

Sceptical analyst Mick West stabilised part of the footage and argued that much of its apparent jitter resulted from camera movement, blur and fluctuating focus. In the stabilised view, the source looked more like a flexible or curved line of lights changing shape than a rigid craft performing rapid manoeuvres. That analysis was informal rather than an official forensic examination, but it directly addressed the behaviour visible in the recording instead of relying on the most dramatic witness description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/blue-ufo-over-hawaii.11526/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blue UFO Over Hawaii &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue UFO Over Hawaii &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

The water sequence is especially ambiguous. Hawaii News Now described videos showing the glowing mass “both in the sky and in the water”, but there was no recorded impact flash, explosion, debris field or confirmed splash. One sceptical reconstruction suggested that the changing lower portion of the light could include reflection from the water as the object dipped close to the surface. Whether it actually touched the sea cannot be determined from the available footage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2020/12/31/faa-notified-after-mysterious-ufo-seen-above-oahu-appeared-drop-into-ocean/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hawaiinewsnow.com">[https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hawaiinewsnow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on hawaiinewsnow.com.</span></span></span>

That distinction is central to the case. “Witnesses believed it entered the ocean” is supported by their testimony. “The video proves that a large object entered the ocean” is not.

## Why an illuminated kite fits the footage

Within days, online investigators compared the blue object with kites carrying LED strips or illuminated tails. Such systems can produce long, flexible lines of light whose visible shape changes as fabric, a tail or a suspended lighting cable twists in the wind. At night, the dark kite body and line may disappear, leaving only the LEDs visible. The result can look like a self-luminous object with no obvious means of support.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/blue-ufo-over-hawaii.11526/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blue UFO Over Hawaii &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue UFO Over Hawaii &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

The comparison explains several otherwise puzzling characteristics:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **The changing outline:** the blue form appears curved and variable rather than consistently rigid.
* **The lack of sound:** a kite at moderate distance would not be expected to produce audible engine noise.
* **The bright, uniform colour:** blue LEDs can remain conspicuous even when the supporting material is invisible.
* **The apparent low altitude:** the object seems to move within the local coastal scene rather than behaving like a meteor, satellite or high-altitude re-entry.
* **The gentle “descent”:** a kite or illuminated tail losing lift could settle towards the shoreline or water without an explosive impact.
* **The poor radar relevance:** an ordinary lightweight kite would not normally be tracked as a missing aircraft.

</div>

West compared stabilised frames from Oahu with footage of known remote-controlled and LED-equipped kites, noting similarities in curvature and shape changes. A separate Hawaii-based paranormal research group also judged an LED kite to be the most likely explanation, while acknowledging that the specific object had not been identified and that its review remained open to further information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/blue-ufo-over-hawaii.11526/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blue UFO Over Hawaii &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue UFO Over Hawaii &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

The kite proposal is not a complete identification. No publicly documented investigation found the flyer, recovered the kite or established where its line had been anchored. Suggestions that it might have been towed behind a boat were speculative. The reported second white light also remains unexplained, although it could have been unrelated or another illuminated object.

Nor does the coincidence that Hawaii News Now broadcast a feature about a local kite maker earlier that day establish a connection. The feature merely demonstrates that sophisticated recreational kite flying existed on Oahu; it does not identify the blue object or its operator. Treating the coincidence as proof would repeat the same mistake made by more sensational interpretations: filling evidential gaps with an appealing story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/blue-ufo-over-hawaii.11526/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blue UFO Over Hawaii &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue UFO Over Hawaii &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

The value of the kite hypothesis lies instead in its visual and physical fit. It reproduces the broad appearance of the object using known equipment and does not depend on estimates of enormous size or extreme speed that the videos cannot support.

## What the official response did—and did not—establish

The official involvement was limited. Honolulu police responded because callers feared that something had fallen into the water. When Hawaii News Now later asked whether investigators had determined what it was, a police spokesperson said the department had no information to provide. The FAA checked the aviation-safety question and found no missing or crashed aircraft associated with the report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2020/12/31/faa-notified-after-mysterious-ufo-seen-above-oahu-appeared-drop-into-ocean/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hawaiinewsnow.com">[https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hawaiinewsnow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on hawaiinewsnow.com.</span></span></span>

There is no public indication that the incident prompted a recovery operation, military investigation, radar-data release or formal unidentified anomalous phenomena case assessment. The FAA’s role was not to classify every light in the sky; it was principally to determine whether the report corresponded to an aviation accident. Once no aircraft was found missing, the most urgent official concern had effectively been answered.

This means claims that “radar showed nothing” should be interpreted carefully. The FAA statement weakens the aircraft-crash theory, but it does not establish that an object with extraordinary radar-evading properties was present. The same result would be expected for a kite, small balloon, lightweight drone accessory or other object outside normal aircraft tracking.

No physical evidence was reported from the alleged point of entry. There was no publicly verified wreckage, unusual residue, sonar contact or independent marine observation. The absence of such evidence is not proof that nothing touched the water, but it prevents the strongest witness claim from being tested.

## How later reporting changed the case

The earliest coverage gave the incident its lasting public image: a large, silent blue UFO travelling over Oahu and plunging into the Pacific. Repetition by national outlets often removed the cautious wording and presented the ocean entry almost as a recorded fact. Some reports also blurred the identities and statements of the witnesses, illustrating how quickly details can shift when a local account is condensed for wider circulation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2020/12/31/faa-notified-after-mysterious-ufo-seen-above-oahu-appeared-drop-into-ocean/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: hawaiinewsnow.com">[https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">hawaiinewsnow.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on hawaiinewsnow.com.</span></span></span>

Later analysis did not uncover stronger evidence for a structured craft. Instead, it drew attention to the footage’s lack of scale, the effects of camera motion and the resemblance to illuminated kites. The case therefore became less anomalous as it received closer scrutiny, even though no person came forward with a definitive conventional identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/blue-ufo-over-hawaii.11526/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Blue UFO Over Hawaii &#124; Metabunk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Blue UFO Over Hawaii &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

It is best classified as **unresolved but plausibly explained**, rather than either fully debunked or genuinely inexplicable. “Unresolved” acknowledges that the exact object and operator remain unknown. “Plausibly explained” reflects the fact that a familiar class of object can account for the main visual features without conflicting with the FAA’s findings.

## Why the blue object matters in Hawaii’s UFO history

The Oahu sighting matters less for what it proves than for what it demonstrates about modern UFO evidence. Unlike many older Hawaii reports, it was not preserved only as a recollection or short written account. It produced several witnesses, immediate phone footage, calls to emergency services and near-contemporary local reporting. Yet even with those advantages, the basic physical facts remained uncertain.

The case shows why multiple videos are not automatically multiple measurements. Recordings from phones can confirm that people observed a real light, but unless their times, locations, directions and original files are preserved, they may still be unable to determine distance or size. Testimony that an object was “huge”, “fast” or “in the water” can be sincere while depending on assumptions that the images do not verify.

It also illustrates the effect of Hawaii’s coastal geography. From leeward Oahu, a descending light can be seen against open ocean with few fixed visual references. Once the object approaches the dark horizon, its apparent movement can easily be interpreted as an entry into the sea. The ocean then removes the expectation of recoverable evidence: if nothing is found, the absence itself may be folded into the mystery.

For Hawaii’s wider UFO record, the Blue Oahu Object is therefore a useful cautionary case. It began with credible witnesses and genuinely puzzling footage, but the central claims of great size, exceptional speed and ocean entry were never measured. The illuminated-kite interpretation did not prove exactly what flew over Nanakuli that evening, but it substantially reduced the need for a more extraordinary explanation.
