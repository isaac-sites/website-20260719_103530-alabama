## Introduction

Many people assume that because NORAD is headquartered in [Colorado]({{ 'what-colorados-ufo-history-really/' | relative_url }}) and operates some of North America's most sophisticated defence networks, it must know about every unusual object seen in the state's skies. The reality is more complicated. NORAD was created to provide aerospace warning and aerospace control for North America, focusing on potential threats such as hostile aircraft, missiles and other objects relevant to continental defence. It was not designed as a scientific observatory that records every unidentified light, drone, balloon or ambiguous radar return over Colorado.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.norad.mil/About-NORAD/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: norad.mil">[NORAD]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">norad.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">About NORADAerospace warning includes the detection, validation, and warning of attack against North America whether by aircraft, mi...</span></span></span>

The short answer is no.

NORAD's mission is centred on defending North America. Its systems are optimised to identify potential threats, assess them quickly and support military decision-making. Aerospace warning focuses on detecting possible attacks by aircraft, missiles or space vehicles, while aerospace control is concerned with identifying and responding to airborne threats.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.norad.mil/About-NORAD/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: norad.mil">[norad.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">norad.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">About NORADAerospace warning includes the detection, validation, and warning of attack against North America whether by aircraft, mi...</span></span></span>

That mission shapes what information is collected, how sensors are configured and which radar returns receive attention. A system designed to detect a cruise missile, strategic bomber or suspicious aircraft does not necessarily operate in the same way as a system intended to catalogue every unexplained object seen by the public.

This is one reason Colorado's reputation as the home of NORAD can create unrealistic expectations. Witnesses sometimes assume that any unusual aerial event must have generated a detailed military track. In practice, whether an object is detected depends on its size, altitude, speed, location, radar signature and perceived relevance to defence operations.

## Why Small or Low-Altitude Objects May Be Missed

The most important limitation is not a lack of technology but the basic physics and operational realities of radar surveillance.

### Terrain and radar geometry

Colorado is not flat. Mountain ranges, valleys and uneven terrain can block or degrade radar coverage. Aviation guidance used by the Federal Aviation Administration notes that low-altitude aircraft may be hidden from radar by terrain or may fly below the radar beam because of Earth curvature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/atpubs/aim_html/chap4_section_5.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationChapter 4. For UFO reports, this means that an object observed from the ground near mountain terrain may not necessarily appear on every surveillance system. A witness can have a clear visual view while a distant radar installation has a limited one.

### Slow and small targets

Radar networks are often designed to distinguish meaningful targets from enormous amounts of background clutter. Birds, weather effects, ground reflections and other non-threatening returns can overwhelm operators if every weak signal is treated as equally important.

Military and technical sources have long noted the difficulty of tracking low, slow and small targets. Objects with minimal radar signatures can blend into background clutter, particularly at low altitude.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/315868273_Study_of_Low-altitude_Slow_and_Small_Target_Detection_on_Radar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) Study of Low-altitude Slow and Small Target</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) Study of Low-altitude Slow and Small Target...January 1, 2017 — 4 Jun 2026 — Radar has always been a key equipment for...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 2017</span></span></span>

### Not every object looks like a threat

A further complication is that some aerial objects simply do not resemble the kinds of targets NORAD was originally built to prioritise.

Defence systems were historically optimised around aircraft, missiles and other recognised threats. That does not mean Colorado UFO reports involve extraordinary craft. It does mean that "NORAD would have seen it" is not always a reliable argument when the reported object was allegedly small, slow, distant or operating in an unusual manner.

## Detection Is Not the Same as a Public Record

Even when a sensor detects something, that does not guarantee a publicly accessible record.

Military systems collect large amounts of information, much of which is classified, temporary, filtered or retained only for operational purposes. Defence organisations are generally concerned with assessing threats, not preserving every ambiguous track for future public analysis.

As a result, many UFO discussions assume a binary choice—either NORAD tracked the object or it did not. Reality includes a third possibility: an object may have generated data that was never considered significant enough to become part of a publicly released record.

This distinction is especially relevant in Colorado, where military facilities, restricted operations, training flights and national-security activities can complicate what information is available to civilian researchers.

## What Records Would Actually Strengthen a UFO Claim?

For serious investigators, the most persuasive evidence is not a witness statement alone and not merely a claim that "radar saw something."

A stronger Colorado UFO case would ideally include multiple independent forms of evidence recorded at the same time:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* Radar data showing an object's position and movement.
* Visual observations from more than one location.
* Pilot, air-traffic-control or military reports that can be compared against one another.
* Time-stamped photographs or video with known camera information.
* Flight records that rule out conventional aircraft.
* Weather data and astronomical checks that eliminate common explanations.

</div>

Researchers studying unidentified aerial phenomena increasingly emphasise the value of multi-sensor observations because a single source of information can be misleading. Combining radar, optical imagery and other measurements provides a much firmer basis for analysis than eyewitness testimony alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18566" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

This is why many Colorado sightings remain unresolved rather than confirmed. The key missing ingredient is often not belief or scepticism but measurement.

## Why This Matters for Colorado UFO History

Colorado's military presence has encouraged a persistent belief that extraordinary sightings should be easy to verify. Yet the state's UFO history repeatedly demonstrates the opposite lesson.

NORAD's existence does not mean every unusual object is tracked. Defence sensors have specific missions, coverage limits and filtering priorities.     } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
