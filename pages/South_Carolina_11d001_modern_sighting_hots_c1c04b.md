## Introduction

Modern UFO reports in [South Carolina]({{ 'south-carolina/' | relative_url }}) cluster most visibly around [Myrtle Beach]({{ 'myrtle-beach/' | relative_url }}) and North Myrtle Beach, followed by Charleston, Columbia and Greenville. That pattern is real in civilian reporting databases, but it is not evidence that unusual craft favour those places. The leading “hotspots” are also where large numbers of residents, tourists and air passengers spend evenings outdoors beneath busy coastal or urban skies.

The Grand Strand is the clearest example. A recent ranking based on National UFO Reporting Center data from 1995 onwards counted 322 reports assigned to Myrtle Beach and 141 to North Myrtle Beach, compared with 85 for Charleston, 72 for Columbia and 59 for [Greenville]({{ 'greenville/' | relative_url }}). These figures identify concentrations of *reports*, not concentrations of confirmed anomalies. They are shaped by who was present, what was visible, how locations were labelled and whether witnesses knew where to submit an account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://stacker.com/stories/south-carolina/cities-most-ufo-sightings-south-carolina" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: stacker.com">[Stacker]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">stacker.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">cities most ufo sightings south carolina</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Cities With the Most UFO Sightings in South Carolina6 Jul 2026 — Stacker compiled a ranking of cities with the most UFO sightings...</span></span></span>

## Where modern reports concentrate

### The Grand Strand stands apart

Myrtle Beach dominates South Carolina’s modern reporting map. NUFORC’s records contain repeated accounts of orange, red or white lights seen from beaches, hotels and balconies, often looking east over the Atlantic. One North Myrtle Beach submission from April 2017 described orange “orbs” seen over the ocean by three observers and claimed similar lights had appeared repeatedly at roughly the same evening hour. The entry records the testimony but does not independently establish the objects’ identity, distance or altitude.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/sighting/?id=133716" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[National UFO Reporting Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National UFO Reporting CenterNUFORC UFO Sighting 133716Location: North Myrtle Beach, SC, USA. The setting helps explain why this area generates so many reports. The Myrtle Beach region promotes 60 miles of coastline and receives more than 17 million visitors in a typical year; its tourism authority reported 18.2 million visitors in 2024. Many of those people are outdoors after dark, facing an unobstructed horizon and carrying phones capable of recording or immediately reporting unfamiliar lights. A city with a modest permanent population can therefore produce a surprisingly high total when reports are divided only by resident population.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.visitmyrtlebeach.com/media/media-resources/fact-sheet" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: visitmyrtlebeach.com">[Visit Myrtle Beach]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">visitmyrtlebeach.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Visit Myrtle BeachMyrtle Beach Area Fact Sheet60 miles of wide, sandy beaches, exciting entertainment, family attractions … welcoming ove...</span></span></span>

The coastal view also creates difficult observing conditions. A light over the ocean may appear to hover because there are few landmarks against which to judge movement. Aircraft approaching at a shallow angle can seem stationary before turning; distant navigation lights may disappear behind haze or cloud; flares can descend slowly; and satellites may brighten or fade as their angle to the Sun changes. Without a reliable compass bearing, elevation, duration and comparison with flight or satellite records, “over the ocean” is a broad description rather than a precise location.

### Charleston, Columbia and the Upstate

Charleston’s position near the top of the list is less surprising when population and aviation are considered. Charleston International shares facilities with Charleston Air Force Base, while the wider Lowcountry includes a major port, military activity and heavily travelled civil air routes. Federal Aviation Administration figures show more than 3.1 million passenger boardings at Charleston in 2024, the highest total for a South Carolina airport. Myrtle Beach recorded about 1.89 million and Greenville–Spartanburg about 1.41 million. Busy airports do not explain every sighting, but they greatly increase the number and variety of visible aircraft lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://aeronautics.sc.gov/airports/charleston-afbintl-airport" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: aeronautics.sc.gov">[aeronautics.sc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">aeronautics.sc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CHARLESTO N AFB/INTL &#124; South Carolina Aeronautics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AC Individual Airport Report_Charleston.pdf (3.94 MB)... Columbia occupies another observation-rich zone. It is the state capital and a large urban centre, with Fort Jackson nearby and McEntire Joint National Guard Base to the south-east. Greenville and the wider Upstate combine a large metropolitan population with Greenville–Spartanburg International Airport and extensive cross-state air traffic. South Carolina also contains Shaw Air Force Base, Marine Corps Air Station Beaufort and other military installations, meaning that unfamiliar military aircraft or training activity are plausible background factors in several regions. Their presence should not be treated as an automatic explanation, but neither should it be ignored.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://installations.militaryonesource.mil/state/SC/state-installations" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: installations.militaryonesource.mil">[MilitaryINSTALLATIONS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">installations.militaryonesource.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on militaryonesource.mil.</span></span></span>

These inland clusters differ from Myrtle Beach in one important respect. Coastal reports often describe lights low over a dark horizon; urban and Upstate accounts more often take place amid conventional flight paths, road travel and suburban illumination. Different observing environments can produce similar witness language—“silent”, “hovering”, “suddenly vanished”—even when the underlying causes are unrelated.

## What civilian databases can and cannot show

NUFORC is useful because it preserves large numbers of first-hand accounts in a searchable public form. Entries commonly include the date, time, location, duration, number of observers, reported shape and a narrative. That makes the database valuable for identifying repeated descriptions, seasonal surges and places where reporting is unusually common. It also allows old memories to be compared with contemporary submissions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/report-a-ufo/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[National UFO Reporting Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">report a ufo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">report a ufo</span></span></span>

It is not, however, a controlled scientific survey. People choose whether to report, sometimes years after the event. NUFORC has acknowledged receiving batches containing retrospective cases, correcting information placed in the wrong fields, deleting duplicates and processing backlogs before publication. A report’s appearance in the database therefore means that an account was submitted and posted; it does not mean that investigators verified an extraordinary object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/722-new-ufo-reports/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[National UFO Reporting Center]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">722 new ufo reports</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">722 new ufo reports</span></span></span>

Several limitations matter when comparing South Carolina locations:<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.dew.sc.gov/node/506" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dew.sc.gov">[dew.sc.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dew.sc.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

* **Unequal awareness:** Residents or visitors who know about NUFORC are more likely to submit than equally puzzled witnesses who do not.
* **Delayed reporting:** An event remembered from the 1970s may enter the database alongside one reported the following morning.
* **Inconsistent place names:** A sighting over the Grand Strand might be filed as Myrtle Beach, North Myrtle Beach, Surfside Beach, a nearby community or simply Horry County.
* **Multiple witnesses:** One event can generate separate submissions, while a group may instead file a single report.
* **Changing technology:** Smartphones, social media and reporting apps make submission easier than it was in earlier decades.
* **Uneven evidence:** Some entries contain precise direction and duration; others offer only a short recollection with no image, bearing or corroborating record.

NASA’s independent UAP study reached a closely related conclusion about eyewitness material generally: such reports can be compelling, but they are usually not reproducible and often lack the information needed to determine what produced the observation. The central weakness is therefore not necessarily witness honesty. It is the absence of calibrated measurements and independent data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report-0.pdf?emrc=6890827de51b3" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uap independent study team final report 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">uap independent study team final report 0</span></span></span>

A national RAND Corporation analysis of more than 101,000 NUFORC reports similarly treated the material as public-reporting data rather than proof of unusual vehicles. RAND found that the likelihood of reports varies with local conditions, including population, access to dark skies, proximity to military operating areas and other factors affecting what people can see. Its conclusions reinforce the need to interpret South Carolina’s clusters as a mixture of observation opportunity, local activity and reporting behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.rand.org/content/dam/rand/pubs/research_reports/RRA2400/RRA2475-1/RAND_RRA2475-1.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rand.org">[RAND Corporation]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rand.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on rand.org.</span></span></span>

## Why beaches and busy skies produce “hotspots”

The most useful way to understand South Carolina’s map is to think in terms of exposure. A location produces more reports when more people are looking upwards, for longer periods, under conditions that make ordinary objects difficult to identify.

Myrtle Beach combines nearly every factor that raises exposure: enormous visitor turnover, high-rise balconies, outdoor nightlife, broad horizons, airport traffic and repeated viewing from the same east-facing coastline. Summer also brings more people outdoors during clear evening weather. Research using NUFORC data at national level has found a strong seasonal component, with reports generally rising in warmer months and favourable observing conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-024-04182-z" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nature.com">[Nature]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nature.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nature.com.</span></span></span>

The coastline also concentrates attention in one direction. Hundreds of observers may see the same distant aircraft, launch-related phenomenon, meteor or military illumination from different points along the beach. Their accounts may use different town names and estimates of distance, making one event look like a series of separate local mysteries. Conversely, a single witness who repeatedly sees similar lights may submit several reports over time. Both effects can inflate the apparent persistence of a hotspot without deliberate duplication.

Modern satellite constellations have added another recurring source of confusion. NUFORC has specifically asked witnesses seeing a string or bar of lights to compare it with images of Starlink before reporting. Technical studies have shown that newly launched satellite trains and brief reflected-light flares can appear unfamiliar even to commercial pilots, especially when the geometry makes individual satellites brighten and fade.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nuforc.org/post20220908/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nuforc.org">[nuforc.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nuforc.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nuforc.org.</span></span></span>

Other common candidates include aircraft viewed head-on, drones, balloons, bright planets, meteors and military training. The 2023 Chinese surveillance balloon offered an unusually clear demonstration of how conspicuous known objects can become over the Grand Strand: residents and visitors watched it near Myrtle Beach before an F-22 shot it down offshore. That event was identified, tracked and widely documented, but many observers initially encountered it simply as an unexpected object in the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://apnews.com/article/cc07bd066880c847e8acc0d86cb396af" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: apnews.com">[AP News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">apnews.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AP News Chinese balloon&#x27;s downing creates spectacle over tourism hub</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AP News Chinese balloon&#x27;s downing creates spectacle over tourism hub</span></span></span>

## Population, coastlines and misleading comparisons

Raw city totals are easy to understand but difficult to interpret. A ranking that places Myrtle Beach above Columbia does not adjust adequately for tourism, time spent outdoors, municipal boundaries or airport traffic. A per-capita calculation based only on permanent residents can be even more misleading in a resort city receiving many times its resident population in annual visitors.

Geography also affects the count. “Myrtle Beach” may function as a familiar regional label for sightings made well outside the city limits. Greenville, by contrast, is part of a broad Upstate urban area whose reports may be divided among Greenville, Greer, Spartanburg, Simpsonville, Easley and other municipalities. Charleston-area observations can similarly be distributed among Charleston, North Charleston, Mount Pleasant, Summerville and the barrier islands. City-level rankings therefore partly measure naming habits and boundary choices.

A stronger comparison would normalise reports by several measures at once:

1. resident and visitor population;
2. hours of darkness and seasonal weather;
3. airport movements and known military activity;
4. the number of separate witnesses to the same event;
5. reporting delay and possible duplicate submissions;
6. whether photographs, radar, flight tracking or satellite predictions are available.

## What the modern pattern really tells us

South Carolina’s modern reporting map has a stable and understandable shape: the Grand Strand leads, while Charleston, Columbia and the Greenville–Spartanburg area form secondary concentrations. This pattern matters because it directs attention towards places where witnesses repeatedly encounter unfamiliar lights. It can help investigators check recurring flight routes, satellite passes, tourism seasons and military activity more efficiently.

What it does not provide is a reliable count of anomalous craft. The strongest interpretation is that South Carolina’s hotspots are largely observation hotspots—places with many viewers, open horizons, busy airspace and an established culture of reporting. Some individual cases may remain unresolved because the necessary records were never collected. That unresolved status should be preserved where appropriate, but it should not be converted into evidence that the state’s beaches or cities attract extraordinary vehicles.

Modern reports would become much more useful if they routinely included exact time to the second, GPS position, direction, angular height, duration, weather, unedited original imagery and immediate checks against aircraft and satellite data. Until then, the apparent clusters reveal far more about where South Carolinians and their visitors watch the sky than about what, if anything, unusual is moving through it.
