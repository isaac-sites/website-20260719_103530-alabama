## Introduction

Among [Idaho]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-idahos-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s many reports during the great flying-saucer wave of 1947, the sighting by the crew of United Airlines Flight 105 stands out because it involved trained aviation personnel rather than casual ground observers. On the evening of 4 July 1947, a United Air Lines DC-3 departed Boise for Pendleton, Oregon. Within minutes, the captain, first officer and a flight attendant reported seeing several unusual objects ahead of the aircraft. Their account quickly became one of the most widely discussed sightings of the early saucer era because the witnesses were professionals accustomed to observing the sky and identifying aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

Yet Flight 105 is also a useful example of the limits of eyewitness evidence. ## What Did the Flight 105 Crew Really See?

### The crew's account from Boise to Pendleton

Flight 105 left Boise shortly after 9 p.m. on 4 July 1947. Contemporary accounts noted that the crew departed during the height of the flying-saucer craze, and Boise tower personnel reportedly joked that they should keep an eye out for the mysterious objects dominating newspaper headlines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_flying_disc_craze" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1947 flying disc craze</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1947 flying disc craze</span></span></span>

About eight minutes after take-off, First Officer Ralph Stevens noticed several unusual objects ahead of the aircraft, roughly in the direction of the setting sun. Captain E. J. Smith joined the observation, and both men watched what they later described as four or five thin objects flying in a loose formation. One appeared larger than the others. According to their statements, the objects seemed smooth underneath and rougher on top, but the crew could not determine their exact shape. They specifically avoided claiming certainty that the objects were classic “saucers”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

The crew followed the objects for roughly ten to fifteen minutes while travelling northwest from Boise. Reports indicate that the formation disappeared and was then replaced by another group of similar objects. Flight attendant Marty Morrow was called forward and reportedly confirmed seeing the same phenomenon. The crew estimated that they tracked the objects over a distance of around 45 miles before they vanished.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/470704emmett_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportThis is case #34 of the Project Blue Book &quot;Unknowns&quot;. Notably, the passengers did not provide comparable observations. Captain Smith later explained that the objects were mostly visible through the forward cockpit view, limiting what people seated in the cabin could see. The crew also contacted other aviation personnel by radio, but no corroborating sighting was obtained from nearby aircraft or ground stations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

## Why Professional Witnesses Gave the Case Weight

The Flight 105 report gained attention because it arrived during a period when many saucer stories were being dismissed as mistakes, rumours or publicity. Unlike many witnesses of the era, Smith and Stevens were experienced airline pilots whose jobs required them to distinguish aircraft, weather phenomena and navigation lights. The addition of a third witness from the cabin strengthened the story in the eyes of many newspaper readers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_105_UFO_sighting" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Flight 105 UFO sighting</span></span></span>

Contemporary journalists repeatedly emphasised the crew’s professional status. Later writers looking back at the [1947 wave]({{ '1947-wave/' | relative_url }}) often cited Flight 105 as one of the first major pilot sightings of the modern UFO era. The report was widely circulated nationally and was frequently presented as more credible than the numerous anonymous or poorly documented saucer claims appearing in newspapers at the same time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.americanheritage.com/was-first-reported-ufo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: americanheritage.com">[americanheritage.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">americanheritage.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">American Heritage Was This the First Reported UFO?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AMERICAN HERITAGEOn Independence Day, United Airlines Flight 105 sat on the tarmac before a scheduled flight from Boise to Pendleton. ## Why the Evidence Remains Limited

The credibility of the witnesses is not the same thing as proof of the objects’ nature. The strongest argument for the case is that three aviation professionals reported an unidentified aerial sight. The strongest argument against any extraordinary conclusion is that almost every critical measurement is missing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/470704emmett_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportThis is case #34 of the Project Blue Book &quot;Unknowns&quot;. Several limitations stand out:

* No radar data were recorded.
* No precise range estimates were available.
* No independent ground observation confirmed the objects.
* The crew could not determine size, altitude or speed with confidence.
* The objects were observed largely against the bright sunset sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nicap.org/470704emmett_dir.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nicap.org">[NICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO ReportThis is case #34 of the Project Blue Book &quot;Unknowns&quot;. These missing details matter because apparent motion in the sky can be highly misleading when distance is unknown. An object close to an aircraft may seem to pace it. A distant object can appear stationary. Without reliable range information, even experienced observers can struggle to estimate speed or manoeuvrability. Modern studies of aerial observations repeatedly emphasise that accurate identification depends heavily on independent measurements rather than visual impressions alone.

## Sunset Conditions, Missing Measurements and Possible Explanations

The sighting occurred shortly after departure, with the aircraft flying generally toward the setting sun. That detail became central to later sceptical assessments. Air Materiel Command, which reviewed early saucer reports, concluded that sunset conditions are especially prone to visual misinterpretation.
